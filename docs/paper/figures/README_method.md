## Fig. 1. Method schematic

`fig1_method_schematic.png` / `.pdf`

*Figure 1. Full-mixing flow tracing. (a) Cell i receives intercell flows q_ji and source terms (here recharge) and passes water to neighbours (q_ik) and to sink terms (here a well, d_i). I_i and O_i are its total inflow and outflow, and r_i = I_i − O_i its cell budget error. (b) Backward fractions F (numbers in cells) to a 6 m³/d well on an eight-cell example under gross accounting, with boundary inflow on the west, recharge of 1 on every cell and a river on the east. Arrow widths show flows; the flows of the well cell are numbered (m³/d). The well takes 6 of the 11 units passing through its cell, so F = 6/11 = 0.55 there and in the cells upstream of it that drain only through it. (c) A weak-sink well cell (flows in m³/d) and the water credited to the well: gross accounting credits its pumping, 5.0 m³/d; net accounting, as in the reference method [FB13], credits the whole inflow to the cell, 33.7 m³/d. (d) Left: a snapshot traces one time step on its own flows, with storage release as a source term of unknown origin. Right: transient tracking follows the same cell through time; the water stored at the end of step n (volume V_i^n, tagged fraction c_i^n) mixes with the inflows of step n+1, and the outflows and the water stored at the end of step n+1 carry the mixture. Shading shows the tagged fraction. The sketch leaves out the cell budget error.*

## Table S1 and Fig. 2. Verification

`verification_table.csv` (long format: model, check, error, tolerance, measure, number of compared values, status, seconds, note), `verification_table.md` (models × checks in two parts, steady and snapshot checks and transient tracking checks, plus the largest error per check), `fig2_verification.png` / `.pdf`.

*Figure 2. Errors reached by the verification checks on the synthetic test models (markers) against machine precision (dashed) and the tolerance each check is tested to (grey bars). Upper block, steady and snapshot checks: the closure checks (the traced volumes add up to the destination or source inflow), the forward–backward check (backward and forward tracing give the same volume between a source and a destination) and the boundary-control check (in every cell the fractions to all sinks add up to 1) test exact identities of the method; their errors are round-off (≤ 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁵) on every model, including the transient model as snapshots (13 steps, storage as source and sink terms) and the stream, multi-aquifer-well, lake and unsaturated-zone networks. The steady forward fraction G equals the MF6 GWT unit concentration run to equilibrium (10⁷ days, upstream advection, no dispersion) to ≤ 2.8 × 10⁻¹¹ on models 1 and 2, including partly capturing river cells and a weak well; on the stream network (model 7) the difference, 1.0 × 10⁻⁸, is limited by the flow solver tolerance and falls to 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ with the flow model solved about 100 times tighter. DISU builds of model 1 give the DIS results exactly, and simulations split into 2 to 4 models with GWF-GWF exchanges agree with the single model to ≤ 7.4 × 10⁻¹⁰, the MF6 solver tolerance. The flow that flow-weighted, weight-losing PRT particles deliver to the well matches the well inflow to within 0.6 %, a release-point error (finite number of release points). Net accounting reproduces the worked example of [FB13] exactly. Lower block, transient tracking checks: transient tracking matches MF6 GWT unit concentrations to ≤ 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴. Forward tracking and backward tracking in time give the same tagged volume between a source and a destination to ≤ 7.7 × 10⁻¹⁶ on model 6 (also with five transport sub-steps per flow step), model 6 split into four models, and the stream and lake networks (models 7 and 9), with both volume options. With the default "flow" volumes, which carry each cell's water volume on from its storage flows, the water balance of every cell holds by construction; its error, ≤ 9.5 × 10⁻¹⁷ of the cell's water volume, tests the volume carry, the clipping of volumes at zero and round-off, not the flow solution. For each tagged group the change in tagged storage over the model equals tagged inflow minus outflow in every step to ≤ 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ of the largest balance term. With model 1's flows held for 4 × 10⁵ days in 120 equal steps (model 10), the tagged fraction at the end equals the steady forward fraction G to 9.7 × 10⁻¹⁴, and the backward fraction in time at the start equals the steady backward fraction F to 6.8 × 10⁻¹⁰. Both errors are limited by the flow solver tolerance, not by round-off: with the flow model solved 100 times tighter they fall to 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁴ and 1.4 × 10⁻¹¹. At the other end of the run, where the transient result has not yet approached the steady one, the two errors are at least 0.40 and 0.52. Three transport sub-steps per flow step give the same fractions and volumes as a flow model with three times as many steps of the same flows, to 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ (hand-built 15-cell network with flow loops and no storage, not an MF6 model). "Exact" marks errors of zero. Error measures are given in Table S1.*

Every check ran. Error measures:
- closure and forward–backward: |lhs − cell budget error term − rhs| / max(|rhs|, 1 m³/d), one group per package, the largest over groups, all destination/source pairs and all steps;
- boundary control: max |Σ_g F_g − 1| over cells with outflow, one group per sink term;
- DISU vs DIS and split vs single: max |a − b| / max |a| per output, the largest over all backward, forward and (split) travel-time outputs;
- steady G vs GWT at equilibrium: max |C − G| over active cells (and reaches on model 7) at 10⁷ days, the largest over the tagging cases of `tests/test_gwt_equilibrium.py` (models 1 and 2: recharge, river leakage, CHD inflow; model 7: recharge and stream inflow, with SFT and MVT); computed with the test's `run_to_equilibrium`, `steady_fraction` and `network_errors`, which also check that GWT has reached equilibrium (last change < 10⁻¹²);
- tracking vs GWT: max |c − C_GWT| over all steps and active cells (and reaches), the largest over the tagging cases of `tests/test_tracking_mf6.py`;
- PRT flow to the well: |particle flow reaching the well / destination inflow − 1|;
- [FB13] example: max |value − published| over fraction, volume through and volume from;
- transient forward–backward: |tagged outflow from forward tracking − the same volume from backward tracking in time| / forward value (absolute when zero), the largest over both volume options, all source (and initial-water) and destination pairs; model 6 also with five transport sub-steps (`tests/test_tracking_mf6.py::check_duality`);
- water balance ("flow" volumes, models 6 and 9): max over cells and steps of |V^new − V^old − Δt (I − O − r)| / max(V^old, V^new), each cell's error relative to its own water volume (`tests/test_tracking_mf6.py::cell_water_balance_errors`). The "flow" volumes are V^new = V^old − Δt S, with S the cell's storage flows, so this balance holds by construction: it tests the volume carry, the clipping of volumes at zero and round-off, not the flow solution;
- tagged-water balance ("flow" volumes, models 6 and 9): for each tagged group, including initial water, the model-wide balance in every step (change in tagged storage against tagged inflow minus outflow); its largest error relative to the largest term of that balance (`check_flow_mode`). Unlike the water balance, it tests the tracking solve;
- steady limit (model 10, 120 equal steps over 4 × 10⁵ days): max |c − G| over active cells at the end and max |w − F| at the start, the largest over groups (`steady_limit_errors`); the smallest over groups at the other end of the run is in the CSV note. With 30 steps growing by 1.2 over the same time (the previous model 10) the errors were 1.4 × 10⁻⁸ and 5.9 × 10⁻⁷: initial water not yet flushed out by the last, long steps (implicit time stepping). With the flow model solved 100 times tighter (IMS closure criteria of `TIGHT_FLOW_IMS` in `tests/test_gwt_equilibrium.py`) the errors are 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁴ and 1.4 × 10⁻¹¹ (CSV note), so with 120 equal steps they are limited by the flow solver tolerance, not by round-off;
- sub-steps vs refined steps: every fraction, outflow and volume of 3 sub-steps against a flow model with 3 times more steps of the same flows, max |a − b| / max |b| per output (`tests/test_tracking.py::substep_refinement_pairs`). The test itself asserts elementwise (rtol 10⁻¹², atol 10⁻¹⁴ on fractions), which is stricter for small entries than this measure.

Each value is computed by the tests' own helpers, which assert the test tolerances, and the collector refuses to record an "ok" row whose error is not finite or exceeds its tolerance (`verification.record`), so the table cannot record a value that fails its check.

## Fig. 3. Numerical dispersion

`fig3_numerical_dispersion.png` / `.pdf`

Decision: the two convergence figures of `docs/dispersion_study.md` (Figs 2 and 4, `docs/figures/dispersion_analytic_convergence.png` and `dispersion_model1_convergence.png`) are merged into one paper figure, one row per problem, with the same three columns. Both problems carry the same three messages (band width ~h^0.5, F > 0.1 too large but containing the zone, F > 0.5 close in area), and one figure saves a slot. It is redrawn from the study's tables (`studies/dispersion/output/a_metrics.csv`, `b_metrics.csv`) with the study's axis helper (`studies/dispersion/figures.py::_size_axis`). The Jaccard panel of the old problem-B figure is replaced by the F > 0.5 area error, so both rows have the same columns. The panel titles use strict thresholds (F > 0.1, F > 0.5), as the paper text does; the study tables (`area_err_0.1`, `area_err_0.5`) select F ≥ 0.1 and F ≥ 0.5, but no full-mixing value in these maps equals 0.1 or 0.5 exactly (noted in the SI), so the selected cells, and the plotted full-mixing and face-routing errors, are the same. The two comparison series are not: the cell-averaged exact zone (A) and PRT on the same grid (B) hold values of exactly 0.5 (and the exact zone also 0.1) in the stored maps (`a_maps.npz`, `b_maps.npz`; 50, 25 and 12.5 m only), and a strict threshold would make their zones smaller by up to about 0.8 % of the zone area (counted on the whole stored map, not the evaluation window). Their curves are still drawn with F ≥ t. The maps (DS Figs 1 and 3) and the transient sub-steps (DS Fig. 5) stay in the supporting information (Fig. S1).

## Fig. 4. Transient tracking (tutorial 08)

`fig4_transient_tracking.png` / `.pdf`. The Neversink figures follow as Figs 5–9 and Figs S2–S3 (`README.md`).

Made by `studies/paper/transient_figure.py` (`main()`; about 8 s: builds and runs the model, runs four forward tracking runs and one backward run, and one MF6 GWT model; workspaces in `studies/paper/output/`, git-ignored).

*Figure 4. Snapshots and transient tracking on the 30-year seasonal model of tutorial 08: one layer of 1,600 cells (50 m), specific yield 0.2, porosity 0.3, a 1-day steady period and then four seasons a year (121 time steps of about 91 days). Recharge is 1.8 times its mean in winter and 0.1 times in summer; pumping is 1.4 times its mean in summer (1,120 m³/d). The well pumps throughout, so the simulation starts from a pumped steady state. A river (dashed) runs 100 m east of the supply well (triangle), and constant heads on the west edge, 1.4 km from the well, bring regional inflow. (a) Snapshots: each time step is traced backward on its own flows, with storage release as a source term. The well's water follows the seasons from the first step: on average 60 % recharge, 12 % west-edge inflow and 15 % storage release, which reaches 50 % in summer. Snapshots have no travel lag, and they cannot say where water released from storage came from. (b) Transient tracking forward, with recharge, river leakage, west-edge inflow and initial water (the water in the aquifer at the start of the simulation) tagged and carried through storage from step to step. In the last season of year 1 the well's water is 95 % initial water, 3 % river leakage and under 2 % recharge; in year 30 it is 52 % initial water, 33 % recharge and 16 % river leakage. No west-edge inflow reaches the well within 30 years. The dashed line is the initial water's share of all water pumped so far: 98 % after 1 year, 81 % after 10 years and 68 % after 30 years. (c) Backward fraction F to the well for the flows of the steady first step, with no time limit; the zone reaches the west edge. (d–f) Backward tracking in time from the end of the simulation: for the water in each cell at the start, the backward fraction in time w (the fraction that leaves through the well within 30 years), the fraction to the river (the only other sink) and the fraction still stored after 30 years. They add up to 1 in every cell. The 30-year zone (d) does not reach the west edge. With every source tagged, the well's pumping over the 30 years, 8,766,800 m³, splits into 1.62 million m³ of recharge, 1.21 million m³ of river leakage and 5.94 million m³ of initial water. The initial-water share, 67.7 %, equals the forward cumulative share after 30 years, as the forward–backward check requires. Transport uses four sub-steps per flow step (about 23 days); 16 sub-steps change the year-1 shares by at most 0.2 percentage points. Panels use the default water volumes, carried by the storage flows. With MF6 GWT's volumes (porosity × saturated volume at the end of each step) the tracked recharge fraction matches a GWT unit-concentration run on the same flows to 9.8 × 10⁻¹⁴ (round-off accumulated over 121 steps); the two options differ by less than 2 × 10⁻⁴.*

Sources: the model is `tutorials/herebedragons.py::build_transient` (30 years, one flow step per season), as in tutorial 08, and the tables are tutorial 08's helpers (`tutorials/08_transient_tracking/helpers.py`: `step_summary` for the snapshot shares, `origin_shares` for the tracked shares). The well factor is 1.0 in the steady first period, so initial water is the water in the aquifer at the start of the simulation, not water from before pumping began. The seasonal factors are `SEASON_RECHARGE` and `SEASON_PUMPING` of `herebedragons.py`, whose means are 1. Every panel uses the default "flow" volumes and four transport sub-steps per flow step, so the forward and backward runs pair to round-off in the forward–backward check (relative error ≤ 4.3 × 10⁻¹⁶ per tag, asserted below 1e-12; backward closure error 0). One transport step per 91-day flow step makes early arrivals too early (tutorial 08 §9): the year-1 river leakage share is 3.6 % with one step, 3.1 % with 4 and 3.0 % with 16, and the year-1 cumulative initial-water share 96.9 %, 97.6 % and 97.8 %. Two extra forward runs at one transport step per flow step, GWT's own stepping, give the check numbers: "gwt" volumes against tutorial 08's GWT recharge tracer (§8, `run_gwt_tracer`), and "flow" volumes for the largest difference between the two options over all tags, cells and steps (1.9 × 10⁻⁴). The snapshot means are weighted by step length over the seasonal steps; the summer storage peak is 49.7 %. The year-1 and year-30 shares are those of the step ending nearest each year. The three backward fractions add up to 1 within 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ (asserted below 1e-9). "Reaches the west edge": the steady F reaches 1 in the west-edge column, while w in that column is below 10⁻⁵ (asserted below 0.01). Every number in the caption, and each statement above, is asserted in `QUOTED`, `BELOW` and `check_quoted()` before plotting. The GWT verification on test models 6 and 7 is in Fig. 2. A steady-limit panel was left out: holding the first-step flows long enough to converge needs a run of about 4,000 steps (about 100 s to build), and model 10 already covers that limit (Table S1, Fig. 2).
