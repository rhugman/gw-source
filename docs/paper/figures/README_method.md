## Fig. 1. Method schematic

`fig1_method_schematic.png` / `.pdf`

*Figure 1. Full-mixing flow tracing. (a) Cell i receives intercell flows q_ji and source terms (here recharge) and passes water to neighbours (q_ik) and to sink terms (here a well, d_i). I_i and O_i are its total inflow and outflow; the cell budget error r_i = I_i − O_i is reported, not forced to zero. Because the cell is fully mixed, its outflows carry the same water, so the backward fraction F_i (the share of the cell's water that reaches the destination) is the flow-weighted mean of its downstream neighbours' fractions plus the share leaving directly through the destination sink. (b) Backward fractions F to a 6 m³/d well on an eight-cell example with boundary inflow (west), recharge of 1 on every cell and a river (east); arrows show flows. Computed with gwsource (gross accounting). The well takes 6 of the 11 units passing through its cell, so F = 6/11 = 0.55 there and in the cells upstream of it that drain only through it. (c) Gross and net accounting on the 5 m³/d weak-sink well of tutorial 02, whose cell carries 33.7 m³/d. Gross accounting, in which each sink takes its share of the cell's outflow, gives the well F = 0.15 in its cell and attributes 5.0 m³/d to it, its pumping. Net accounting, as in the reference method [FB13], fixes F = 1 in the well cell and attributes the whole throughflow, 33.7 m³/d, 6.7 times the pumping.*

Numbers in (b) and (c) are computed on each run: (b) from the hand-built `FlowStep` in `graph_step()`, whose backward closure is checked; (c) by building tutorial 02's layered model (`tutorials/herebedragons.py::build_layered`) and tracing the weak well in both modes.

## Table 2 and Fig. 2. Verification

`verification_table.csv` (long format: model, check, error, tolerance, measure, number of compared values, status, seconds, note), `verification_table.md` (models × checks, plus the largest error per check), `fig2_verification.png` / `.pdf`.

*Figure 2. Errors reached by the verification checks on the synthetic test models (markers) against machine epsilon (dashed) and the tolerance each check is tested to (grey bars). The closure checks (the traced volumes add up to the destination or source inflow), the forward–backward check (backward and forward tracing give the same volume between a source and a destination) and the boundary-control check (in every cell the fractions to all sinks add up to 1) test exact identities of the method; their errors are round-off (≤ 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁵) on every model, including the transient model (13 steps, storage as source and sink terms) and the stream, multi-aquifer-well, lake and unsaturated-zone networks. DISU builds of model 1 give the DIS results exactly, and simulations split into 2 to 4 models with GWF-GWF exchanges agree with the single model to ≤ 7.4 × 10⁻¹⁰, the MF6 solver tolerance. Transient tracking matches MF6 GWT unit concentrations to ≤ 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴. The flow that flow-weighted, weight-losing PRT particles deliver to the well matches the well inflow to within 0.6 %, a release-point error (finite number of release points). Net accounting reproduces the worked example of [FB13] exactly. "Exact" marks errors of zero. Error measures are given in Table 2.*

Largest error per check (tolerance in brackets):

| check | largest error | model |
|---|---|---|
| backward closure | 1.3e-15 (1e-10) | 6 transient |
| forward closure | 1.1e-15 (1e-10) | 6 transient |
| forward–backward | 1.5e-15 (1e-10) | 7 SFR + MVR network |
| boundary control | 1.8e-15 (1e-12) | 6 transient |
| DISU vs DIS | 0 (1e-12) | 4, both variants |
| split vs single | 7.4e-10 (1e-8) | 1 DIS, three models |
| tracking vs GWT | 1.0e-14 (1e-8) | 7 SFR + MVR vs SFT + MVT |
| PRT flow to the well | 5.9e-3 (2e-2) | 2 DIS, 3 layers (402.6 of 405 m³/d) |
| [FB13] example | 0 (1e-14) | worked example, net accounting |

Every check ran; none was skipped. Error measures:
- closure and forward–backward: |lhs − cell budget error term − rhs| / max(|rhs|, 1 m³/d), one group per package, the largest over groups, all destination/source pairs and all steps;
- boundary control: max |Σ_g F_g − 1| over cells with outflow, one group per sink term;
- DISU vs DIS and split vs single: max |a − b| / max |a| per output, the largest over all backward, forward and (split) travel-time outputs;
- tracking vs GWT: max |c − C_GWT| over all steps and active cells (and reaches), the largest over the tagging cases of `tests/test_tracking_mf6.py`;
- PRT flow to the well: |particle flow reaching the well / destination inflow − 1|;
- [FB13] example: max |value − published| over fraction, volume through and volume from.

## Fig. 3. Numerical dispersion

`fig3_numerical_dispersion.png` / `.pdf`

Decision: the two convergence figures of `docs/dispersion_study.md` (Figs 2 and 4, `docs/figures/dispersion_analytic_convergence.png` and `dispersion_model1_convergence.png`) are merged into one paper figure, one row per problem, with the same three columns. Both problems carry the same three messages (band width ~h^0.5, F ≥ 0.1 too large but containing the zone, F ≥ 0.5 close in area), and one figure saves a slot. It is redrawn from the study's tables (`studies/dispersion/output/a_metrics.csv`, `b_metrics.csv`) with the study's axis helper (`studies/dispersion/figures.py::_size_axis`). The Jaccard panel of the old problem-B figure is replaced by the F ≥ 0.5 area error, so both rows have the same columns. The maps (DS Figs 1 and 3) and the transient sub-steps (DS Fig. 5) stay in the supporting information.

*Figure 3. Numerical dispersion of full mixing against cell size h. Top row: problem A, uniform flow to a well with the analytical capture zone [Javandel & Tsang 1986] as reference, with flow along the grid rows (solid) and at 30° (dotted). Bottom row: problem B, a model-1-like aquifer with MF6 PRT on the 12.5 m grid as reference. (a, d) Width of the band where 0.1 < F < 0.9. For full mixing it narrows as about h^0.5 (dashed guide); face-to-face routing narrows it but keeps the rate. The cell-averaged exact zone (A) and PRT on the same grid (B) set the floor of about one cell. (b, e) Relative area error of the zone F ≥ 0.1: the zone always contains the reference zone but is too large, by 30 to 90 % in problem A and 8 to 16 % in problem B for h = 12.5 to 50 m. (c, f) Relative area error of the zone F ≥ 0.5: within 5 % (grey band) for h ≤ 50 m.*

The ranges in the caption are those of DS §5 (problem A includes the DISV grids, whose mean cell size is slightly above the nominal h).

## Fig. 4. Transient tracking (tutorial 08)

`fig4_transient_tracking.png` / `.pdf`. The Neversink figures follow as Figs 5–10 (`README.md`).

Made by `studies/paper/transient_figure.py` (`main()`; about 8 s: builds and runs the model, runs four forward tracking runs and one backward run, and one MF6 GWT model; workspaces in `studies/paper/output/`, git-ignored).

*Figure 4. Snapshots and transient tracking on the 30-year seasonal model of tutorial 08: one layer of 1,600 cells (50 m), specific yield 0.2, porosity 0.3, a 1-day steady period and then four seasons a year (121 time steps of about 91 days). Recharge is 1.8 times its mean in winter and 0.1 times in summer; pumping is 1.4 times its mean in summer (1,120 m³/d). The well pumps throughout, so the simulation starts from a pumped steady state. A river (dashed) runs 100 m east of the supply well (triangle), and constant heads on the west edge, 1.4 km from the well, bring regional inflow. (a) Snapshots: each time step is traced backward on its own flows, with storage release as a source term. The well's water follows the seasons from the first step: on average 60 % recharge, 12 % west-edge inflow and 15 % storage release, which reaches 50 % in summer. Snapshots have no travel lag, and they cannot say where water released from storage came from. (b) Transient tracking forward, with recharge, river leakage, west-edge inflow and initial water (the water in the aquifer at the start of the simulation) tagged and carried through storage from step to step. In the last season of year 1 the well's water is 95 % initial water, 3 % river leakage and under 2 % recharge; in year 30 it is 52 % initial water, 33 % recharge and 16 % river leakage. No west-edge inflow reaches the well within 30 years. The dashed line is the initial water's share of all water pumped so far: 98 % after 1 year, 81 % after 10 years and 68 % after 30 years. (c) Backward fraction F to the well for the flows of the steady first step, with no time limit; the zone reaches the west edge. (d–f) Backward tracking in time from the end of the simulation: for the water in each cell at the start, the backward fraction in time w (the fraction that leaves through the well within 30 years), the fraction to the river (the only other sink) and the fraction still stored after 30 years. They add up to 1 in every cell. The 30-year zone (d) does not reach the west edge. With every source tagged, the well's pumping over the 30 years, 8,766,800 m³, splits into 1.62 million m³ of recharge, 1.21 million m³ of river leakage and 5.94 million m³ of initial water. The initial-water share, 67.7 %, equals the forward cumulative share after 30 years, as the forward–backward check requires. Transport uses four sub-steps per flow step (about 23 days); 16 sub-steps change the year-1 shares by at most 0.2 percentage points. Panels use the default water volumes, carried by the storage flows. With MF6 GWT's volumes (porosity × saturated volume at the end of each step) the tracked recharge fraction matches a GWT unit-concentration run on the same flows to 9.8 × 10⁻¹⁴ (round-off accumulated over 121 steps); the two options differ by less than 2 × 10⁻⁴.*

Sources: the model is `tutorials/herebedragons.py::build_transient` (30 years, one flow step per season), as in tutorial 08, and the tables are tutorial 08's helpers (`tutorials/08_transient_tracking/helpers.py`: `step_summary` for the snapshot shares, `origin_shares` for the tracked shares). The well factor is 1.0 in the steady first period, so initial water is the water in the aquifer at the start of the simulation, not water from before pumping began. The seasonal factors are `SEASON_RECHARGE` and `SEASON_PUMPING` of `herebedragons.py`, whose means are 1. Every panel uses the default "flow" volumes and four transport sub-steps per flow step, so the forward and backward runs pair to round-off in the forward–backward check (relative error ≤ 4.3 × 10⁻¹⁶ per tag, asserted below 1e-12; backward closure error 0). One transport step per 91-day flow step makes early arrivals too early (tutorial 08 §9): the year-1 river leakage share is 3.6 % with one step, 3.1 % with 4 and 3.0 % with 16, and the year-1 cumulative initial-water share 96.9 %, 97.6 % and 97.8 %. Two extra forward runs at one transport step per flow step, GWT's own stepping, give the check numbers: "gwt" volumes against tutorial 08's GWT recharge tracer (§8, `run_gwt_tracer`), and "flow" volumes for the largest difference between the two options over all tags, cells and steps (1.9 × 10⁻⁴). The snapshot means are weighted by step length over the seasonal steps; the summer storage peak is 49.7 %. The year-1 and year-30 shares are those of the step ending nearest each year. The three backward fractions add up to 1 within 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ (asserted below 1e-9). "Reaches the west edge": the steady F reaches 1 in the west-edge column, while w in that column is below 10⁻⁵ (asserted below 0.01). Every number in the caption, and each statement above, is asserted in `QUOTED`, `BELOW` and `check_quoted()` before plotting. The GWT verification on test models 6 and 7 is in Fig. 2. A steady-limit panel was left out: holding the first-step flows long enough to converge needs a run of about 4,000 steps (about 100 s to build), and model 10 already covers that limit in the test suite.
