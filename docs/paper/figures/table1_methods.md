# Table 1. Methods compared

*Table 1. Four ways to compute, from a groundwater flow model's flows, the share of the water in each cell that reaches a destination or came from a source: the reference method [FB13, BF13; BFW21 and DAL22 for its 2020 version], this formulation, unit-concentration transport in MODFLOW 6 GWT [BED24, LAN22] and hydraulic mixing cells as published in [PAR11]. F is the backward fraction (the share of the water leaving a cell that leaves the model through the destination) and G the forward fraction (the share of the water entering a cell that came from the source); in transient tracking, c is the tagged fraction and w the backward fraction in time. Gross accounting: each sink term in a cell takes its share of the cell's total outflow. Net accounting: a destination cell is credited with all the water passing through it. Years in brackets date a statement to the 2013 papers or to the 2020 version of the reference method. [check] marks a cell that rests on less than the full text of its source; only the GWT backward-in-time cell, which rests on the [BED24] abstract, is so marked. MODALL [POT08] has no column because its solution method could not be established (footnote 10).*

| | The reference method [FB13, BF13] | This formulation | GWT unit concentration [BED24, LAN22] | Hydraulic mixing cells [PAR11] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What is solved, and how | Fractions cell by cell, in flow order after sorting the cells; one pass per time step | One set of linear equations per direction, in flow order¹; tracking: one implicit step at a time | Transport equation in implicit time steps, iterative solver; "does not have an option to calculate steady-state transport solutions" [LAN22]² | Explicit update from the previous step's fractions, on the flow model's own steps; stable only if a step's inflow or outflow volume ≤ the cell's storage (§3.1, Eq. 9)³ |
| Mixing in a cell | Full mixing | Full mixing | Full mixing with upstream weighting and no dispersion⁴ | "Modified mixing cell" (Campana & Simpson 1984): water leaving over a step carries the previous step's mix; water entering stays until the next step. Tends to full mixing as the step goes to 0 (§3.1–3.2, Eqs 9–10) |
| Accounting at weak sinks | Net: destination cell credited with all its throughflow⁵ | Gross (default); net as an option | Gross: sinks remove water at the cell's concentration | Outflow leaves at the cell's previous-step mix (§3.2); gross in effect (our reading) |
| Flow loops | Smallest-flow connection in each loop deleted (2013) | Solved exactly; closed recirculation loops given F = 0 and reported; net accounting can instead delete the smallest-flow connection (`remove_cycles`) | None needed: one implicit solve over all cells (our inference) | Not discussed; fixed stream flow direction assumed (§3, §4) |
| Outputs | F, volume through, volume from (by budget term), fraction from; forward fraction (C/C0); particle travel time; statistics over steps | F, G and volumes by term; tracking: c and w⁶ | Concentration, read as the share of water from the tagged source | Fractions of K flow components in each stream cell (groundwater, unsaturated flow, overland flow, rainfall, …), plus a dummy fraction for initial water (§3.1–3.2, Eqs 5–7) |
| Transient models: water carried through storage? | No: each step traced as a steady state (snapshots) | Yes, transient tracking (needs porosity); snapshots also | Yes (needs porosity) | Yes: stream-cell water volumes carried step to step (Eqs 5–6); initial fractions assumed until the initial water is flushed out (§3.2) |
| Backward in time? | No | Yes, solved from the end of the simulation | No⁷ [check] | None |
| Destinations or sources per solve | One destination group (any number of cells) per calculation | Gross: all groups in one solve (in tracking, one per step, or per sub-step when volumes change); net: one group per solve | One tagged source per GWT model; several models per simulation | All K flow components together, summing to 1 (Eq. 7); flow components, not user-chosen terms |
| Built-in water-budget checks | None described; flow loops reported (2013); "error checking" mentioned without detail (2020) [DAL22] | Closure, forward–backward, travel-time closure, closed-loop report; tracking: forward–backward in time, water and tagged-water balance | Solute mass budget by term per step (for unit concentration, a tagged-water balance)⁸; no source-to-destination identity | Relative error e = 1 − Σf (Eq. 13); global volume error GVE (Eq. 14): groundwater volume in the stream cells against total exfiltration, valid on gaining reaches only, close to our closure check |
| Grids and packages | Structured MODFLOW grids (2013); MF6 and USG unstructured (2020); package networks not documented | All MF6 grid types, multi-model simulations, package networks⁹ | DIS, DISV, DISU; SFR, LAK, MAW, UZF and MVR transport | HydroGeoSphere stream cells of the surface domain (§3.2); said to apply to any code that reports surface–subsurface exchange (§1, §3) |
| Open implementation | No: commercial; VB.NET source for academic use on request (2013) | Yes (`gwsource`, MIT) | Yes (MODFLOW 6, USGS) | Visual Basic for Excel post-processor on HydroGeoSphere output (§3.2); availability not stated |

¹ Without flow loops the equations are solved cell by cell in flow order; with flow loops, loop by loop in flow order. Net accounting solves one destination group at a time. In tracking, each time step (or sub-step, when the water volumes change within a flow step) is one set of linear equations.

² The GWT Model needs the storage (MST) package: without it, MF6 (6.9.0.dev0) stops at input with "Mass storage and transfer (MST6) package not specified". With MST porosity set to 0, a use not described in the parts of [LAN22] read, one transport step on the flows of test model 1 gave the steady unit concentration equal to G to 9.5 × 10⁻¹³ (recharge tagged) and 9.7 × 10⁻¹³ (river tagged), with no warning in the list file (our test).

³ Later versions extend [PAR11]: an independent sub-time-step routine between the flow model's steps, to avoid instability (Partington et al. 2013, as cited by Nogueira et al. 2022, Sect. 2.2; Partington et al. 2013 not read), and fractions in every model cell, surface and subsurface, as applied to a riparian zone by Nogueira et al. (2022, Sect. 2.2, Eq. 1).

⁴ GWT also offers central-in-space and TVD advection, dispersion and reactions [LAN22]. With upstream weighting and no dispersion its steady limit equals G (Table 2).

⁵ [FB13] define all water starting in or passing through a destination cell as reaching the destination, so a weak-sink well is credited with all the water passing through its cell.

⁶ Also boundary-control maps (the fractions to all sinks, which add up to 1 in every cell), mean travel time, statistics over steps and, in tracking, the fractions leaving through other sinks and still stored.

⁷ [LAN22] documents no backward-in-time option. A backward steady run would need a flow field with reversed flows and swapped sources and sinks: possible, but awkward (`references/SUMMARY.md` §6.3). The abstract of [BED24] lists well capture analysis among the uses without saying how it is done.

⁸ The GWT budget file holds the solute mass flows of each cell by term (intercell, storage, source and sink mixing, advanced packages) [MF6IO], and the list file reports the mass budget of the whole model with its percent discrepancy at each saved step (seen in our runs).

⁹ DIS, DISV and DISU grids; GWF-GWF exchanges; SFR reaches, lakes, MAW wells and UZF cells added as extra cells and linked by MVR.

¹⁰ MODALL [POT08] computes, from MODFLOW cell-by-cell flows, the share of flow in each cell that came from a source or goes to a sink (abstract), for steady-state models, assuming full mixing and without the reference method's flow-order sort [CHO16 §2.3.3, p. 103; FB13]. How it solves, how it treats weak sinks and what it does with flow loops could not be established.

### The reference method

- **Solved, how:** [FB13] Method and Evaluation: cells sorted in flow order, then one arithmetic pass; earlier versions used recursive scans. Each time step processed on its own [FB13] Introduction.
- **Mixing:** [FB13] Description of Calculation (divergent cells split their water in proportion to outflow); stated as full mixing in [CHO16] §2.4.3.
- **Accounting:** [FB13] Description of Calculation: all water starting in or passing through a destination cell reaches the destination (cell A, 100 %); volume from uses each cell's excess of outflow over inflow. The outflows of destination cells are detached so that nothing downstream is counted [FB13] Method.
- **Flow loops:** [FB13] Method (the lowest-volume connection is removed; which one to remove is called an open research question) and Evaluation; [BF13] Introduction to Graphs ("lowest flow volume cell connection(s)"). Dated 2013 because [DAL22] lists unspecified improvements to the flow routing in the 2020 version.
- **Outputs:** [FB13] Introduction and Figs 1–4, 6–8 (volume and fraction through, volume and fraction from; volume from by recharge, storage and boundary term if other budget records are read); [BF13] Introduction (min, max, mean, ASN, probability within range; travel time from forward particles based on MODPATH 6); [BFW21] slide 10 and [DAL22] (forward mode as "an Eulerian transport tool", fraction = C/C0, volume to the receptor); [BFW21] slide 5 (fraction from summing to 100 %).
- **Transient:** [FB13] Introduction and [BF13] Introduction and Problem Description: each time step a "time-instant steady state" (Rushton 2003); particles for travel time tracked through each step's steady flows [BF13] Introduction.
- **Backward in time:** none in [FB13], [BF13], [BFW21] or [DAL22]; it follows from the snapshot treatment.
- **Per solve:** [FB13] Evaluation and [BF13] river-catchment and boundary-control examples: up to about 150,000 destination cells processed as one group in one calculation; the four boundary-control maps (Figs 18–21) were "processed individually" and sum to 1 in every cell.
- **Checks:** [FB13] Method (flow loops reported to the user as a sign of a model flaw); [DAL22] ("error checking" among minor changes, without detail). No closure or other budget check is described.
- **Grids and packages:** [FB13] Abstract and Introduction (MODFLOW-96 and MODFLOW-2005 CBC files; method said to apply to MODFLOW-USG and finite elements); [BF13] (MODFLOW-96); [BFW21] slides 6 and 11 and [DAL22] (MF6 and MODFLOW-USG; triangular, quadtree and Voronoi meshes). MF6 exchanges, MVR and package networks are not mentioned in any source (`references/SUMMARY.md` §2.9, §8).
- **Open implementation:** [FB13] and [BF13] end matter (available on request; VB.NET source for academic use on request); [GWS-web] (node-locked, dongle and shared licences sold).

### GWT unit concentration

- **Solved, how:** [LAN22] abstract (implicit, iterative methods; the quoted words on steady state) and p. 2–1 (the cell balances form a system of linear equations); p. 1–7, items 4 and 10 (central, upstream or TVD advection; "no option to automatically run the GWT Model to steady state using a single time step"; steady state found by running transient until concentrations stabilise). Footnote 2: our runs of test model 1's GWT on its saved flows with `tests/models/gwt.py` (`build_gwt_fmi`: ADV UPSTREAM, no DSP, FMI with flow-imbalance correction; recharge or river tagged; GWT outer/inner DVCLOSE 1e-12/1e-13, RCLOSE 1e-9) and MF6 6.9.0.dev0, in a scratch folder outside the repository. With MST left out the run stops at input. With one transport step of 1 day and MST porosity 0, max |C − G| over the 594 active cells is 9.5 × 10⁻¹³ with recharge tagged and 9.7 × 10⁻¹³ with the river tagged; with porosity 10⁻¹², 3.8 × 10⁻⁸ and 2.4 × 10⁻⁹.
- **Mixing:** [LAN22] pp. 4–1 to 4–2 (upstream weighting); dispersion is an option (chapter 4). The match to G is this paper's own check (Table 2: ≤ 2.8 × 10⁻¹¹ at equilibrium on models 1 and 2).
- **Accounting:** [LAN22] p. 5–3, eq. 5–7: by default water leaving a cell through a stress package leaves at the cell's concentration. Flow-imbalance correction, p. 2–6, eq. 2–14.
- **Flow loops:** not discussed in [LAN22]; the cell is our inference.
- **Outputs:** [BED24] abstract (concentration one for the source water assessed, zero for all other water; the concentration gives each source's contribution to sinks; uses: source allocation, well capture analysis, mixing and dilution). Agreement with forward tracking: Table 2.
- **Transient:** [LAN22] p. 3–1, eqs 3–2 and 3–3 (storage of dissolved mass in the cell's water volume, with porosity and saturation, implicit in time).
- **Backward in time:** no reverse or adjoint option in the parts of [LAN22] read; [BED24] abstract silent on how well capture analysis is done [check]; a backward steady run on reversed flows, `references/SUMMARY.md` §6.3.
- **Per solve:** [LAN22] p. 1–6, item 1 (one species per GWT model; several GWT models per simulation).
- **Checks:** [MF6IO] "Budget File Contents for the GWT Model" (solute mass flows per cell, in mass per time, by term: FLOW-JA-FACE, STORAGE-AQUEOUS, SOURCE-SINK MIX, CNC, SRC, LKT, SFT, MWT, UZT and others); the list-file "MASS BUDGET FOR ENTIRE MODEL" with "PERCENT DISCREPANCY", seen in the footnote 2 runs. With unit concentration the solute mass is tagged water volume, so the budget is a tagged-water balance. No check of traced volumes between a source and a destination is described in the parts of [LAN22] or [MF6IO] read.
- **Grids and packages:** [LAN22] pp. 1–6 to 1–7, items 3 and 11 (DIS, DISV, DISU; SFT, LKT, MWT, UZT and MVT, advection only between package features and with the aquifer).
- **Open implementation:** MODFLOW 6 is distributed by the USGS ([LAN22] front matter, MODFLOW web page).

### Hydraulic mixing cells

All cells rest on the [PAR11] manuscript, read in full. The column describes [PAR11] itself, in which the mixing cells are stream cells of the surface domain only (§3.1, "a stream cell i"; §3.2–3.3, pairs of adjacent HydroGeoSphere grid points across the stream). Later extensions are in footnote 3.

- **Solved, how:** §3.1, Eqs 8–9: the fraction of component k in cell i at step N from the cell's storage at N − 1 and N, the outflow volume over the step at the cell's own fraction at N − 1 and the inflow volumes at the neighbours' fractions at N − 1; no system of equations. Stability (§3.1, after Eq. 9; §4): the volume entering or leaving a cell over a step must not exceed its storage; small cells and long steps cause oscillations, so suitable time steps are needed. Volumes over each step come from the flow model's fluxes at the end of the step (Eq. 11); no sub-steps.
- **Mixing:** §3 and §4 (based on the modified mixing cell of Campana & Simpson 1984; "the modified mixing rule has to be used" unless mixing in the river is simulated); §3.2 (outflow over a step at the previous step's fractions; water entering stays until the next step); §3.1, Eqs 9–10 (Eq. 9 tends to the perfectly mixed cell, Eq. 10, as the step goes to 0).
- **Accounting:** §3.2 and Eq. 9: every outflow of the cell, downstream or to the subsurface, uses the cell's previous-step fractions. That this is gross accounting in effect is our reading; [PAR11] does not discuss weak sinks.
- **Flow loops:** not discussed. §3 and §4: constant stream width and fixed stream flow direction assumed in the coding.
- **Outputs:** §3.1, Eqs 5–7 (K components: groundwater, unsaturated flow, overland flow, preferential flow and direct rainfall; fractions sum to 1); §3.2 (a "dummy (or undefined) fraction" for the initial water until the stream water is turned over). Applied as the groundwater component of streamflow at each stream cell (§3.3.2, Figs 11–12).
- **Transient:** §3.1, Eqs 4–6 (cell volumes and component volumes carried from step N − 1 to N); §3.2 (initial fractions are subjective; the dummy fraction falls to zero once the stream water is turned over).
- **Backward in time:** none; the method steps forward from initial fractions (§3.1–3.2).
- **Per solve:** Eqs 5–8 (all K components updated together).
- **Checks:** §3.3.1, Eq. 13 (relative error e from the sum of fractions; maximum 1.5 × 10⁻³ % in test case 1, 8.7 × 10⁻³ % in test case 2) and Eq. 14 (global volume error: the summed groundwater volume in the stream cells at the end against the summed exfiltration over the run from the model's water balance; "can only be used along completely gaining sections" with no losses; Table 1 of [PAR11]). GVE compares a traced volume with a water-budget total, as our closure check does.
- **Grids and packages:** §3.2 (HydroGeoSphere output: surface water depth, contributing area, surface–subsurface exchange flux, and upstream and downstream fluxes); §1 and §3 (can be implemented in any code that reports surface–subsurface exchange in a spatially distributed way); §3.1 (any FD, FV or FE scheme if the cell water balance is mass conservative).
- **Open implementation:** §3.2 ("coded in Visual Basic for Excel and is used as a post-processing tool on HGS outputs"); no statement on availability.

### MODALL (no column)

[POT08] abstract (Europe PMC): MODALL uses the MODFLOW cell-by-cell flow terms to evaluate the flow balance between cells and gives the share of flow in each cell from a source or to a sink; compared with an analytical solution, MODPATH and MT3DMS. [CHO16] §2.3.3 (pp. 23–24) and §2.4: steady-state MODFLOW only; outputs equal the reference method's fraction through. [CHO16] p. 103: full mixing, numerical dispersion on coarse grids [MC09], a simpler calculation than the reference method, without its flow-order sort. [FB13] Introduction: MODALL does not use the reference method's flow-order sort and was used to validate its fraction through. [BF13] Problem Description: on a model with flow loops MODALL did not delineate the capture areas (personal communication cited there). None of these says whether MODALL solves a linear system, iterates or sweeps, or how it treats weak sinks.
