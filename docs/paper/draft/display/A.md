## Table 1

**Table 1.** Four ways to compute, from a groundwater flow model's flows, the share of the water in each cell that reaches a destination or came from a source: the reference method (Foley and Black 2013; Black and Foley 2013; for its 2020 version, Black et al. 2021 and Daley et al. 2022), this formulation, unit-concentration transport in MODFLOW 6 GWT (Bedekar et al. 2024; Langevin et al. 2022) and hydraulic mixing cells as published by Partington et al. (2011). F is the backward fraction (the share of the water leaving a cell that leaves the model through the destination) and G the forward fraction (the share of the water entering a cell that came from the source); in transient tracking, c is the tagged fraction and w the backward fraction in time. Gross accounting: each sink term in a cell takes its share of the cell's total outflow. Net accounting: a destination cell is credited with all the water passing through it. Years in parentheses date a statement to the 2013 papers or to the 2020 version of the reference method. [check] marks a cell that rests on less than the full text of its source; only the GWT backward-in-time cell, which rests on the abstract of Bedekar et al. (2024) and the authors' conference slides (Bedekar et al. 2023), is so marked. MODALL (Potter et al. 2008) has no column because its solution method could not be established (footnote 17).

| | The reference method (Foley and Black 2013; Black and Foley 2013) | This formulation | GWT unit concentration (Bedekar et al. 2024; Langevin et al. 2022) | Hydraulic mixing cells (Partington et al. 2011) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What is solved, and how | Fractions cell by cell in flow order; one pass per time step | Linear equations in flow order¹; tracking: implicit steps | Implicit time steps, iterative solver; no steady-state option (Langevin et al. 2022)² | Explicit, on the flow model's steps; stable only for short steps³ |
| Mixing in a cell | Full mixing | Full mixing | Full mixing with upstream weighting and no dispersion⁴ | Modified mixing cell; tends to full mixing as steps shorten⁵ |
| Accounting at weak sinks | Net: destination cell credited with all water passing through⁶ | Gross (default); net as an option | Gross: sinks remove water at the cell's concentration | Outflow at the previous step's mix; gross in effect (our reading) |
| Flow loops | Smallest-flow connection in each loop deleted (2013) | Solved exactly; closed recirculation loops set to F = 0⁷ | None needed: one implicit solve over all cells (our inference) | Not discussed; fixed stream flow direction assumed |
| Outputs | F, volumes through and from; forward fraction; particle travel time⁸ | F, G and volumes by term; tracking: c and w⁹ | Concentration, read as the share of water from the tagged source | Fractions by flow component per stream cell, plus initial water¹⁰ |
| Transient models: water carried through storage? | No: each step traced as a steady state (snapshots) | Yes, transient tracking (needs porosity); snapshots also | Yes (needs porosity) | Yes: stream-cell water volumes carried step to step |
| Backward in time? | No | Yes, solved from the end of the simulation | No¹¹ [check] | No |
| Destinations or sources per solve | One destination group (any number of cells) per calculation | Gross: all groups in one solve; net: one group per solve | One tagged source per GWT model; several models per simulation | All flow components together; not user-chosen terms |
| Built-in water-budget checks | None described; flow loops reported (2013); unspecified "error checking" (2020; Daley et al. 2022) | Closure, forward–backward, travel-time closure; more in tracking¹² | Mass budget, a tagged-water balance¹³; no source-to-destination check | Sum-of-fractions error; global volume error, gaining reaches only¹⁴ |
| Grids and packages | Structured MODFLOW grids (2013); MF6, USG unstructured (2020); package networks not documented | All MF6 grid types, multi-model simulations, package networks¹⁵ | DIS, DISV, DISU; SFR, LAK, MAW, UZF and MVR transport | HydroGeoSphere stream cells; said to apply to other codes¹⁶ |
| Open implementation | No: commercial; VB.NET source for academic use on request (2013) | Yes (`gwsource`, MIT) | Yes (MODFLOW 6, USGS) | Excel (Visual Basic) post-processor; availability not stated |

¹ One set of equations for the backward fraction F and one for the forward fraction G. Without flow loops the equations are solved cell by cell in flow order; with flow loops, loop by loop in flow order. Net accounting solves one destination group at a time. In tracking, each time step (or sub-step, when the water volumes change within a flow step) is one set of linear equations, implicit in time, solved for all tagged groups at once.

² Langevin et al. (2022): GWT "does not have an option to calculate steady-state transport solutions"; steady state is reached by running transient steps until the concentrations stabilise. The GWT Model needs the storage (MST) package: without it, MF6 (6.9.0.dev0) stops at input with "Mass storage and transfer (MST6) package not specified". With MST porosity set to 0, a use not described in the parts of Langevin et al. (2022) read, one transport step on the flows of test model 1 gave the steady unit concentration equal to G to 9.5 × 10⁻¹³ (recharge tagged) and 9.7 × 10⁻¹³ (river tagged), with no warning in the list file (our test). <!-- table1_methods.md, Sources, GWT unit concentration, 'Solved, how'; tests/models/gwt.py build_gwt_fmi -->

³ The fractions at each step are computed from the previous step's fractions and the volumes moved over the step; no system of equations is solved. The update is stable only if the volume entering or leaving a cell over a step does not exceed the cell's storage; small cells and long steps cause oscillations. Later versions: Partington et al. (2012, §2.2) apply the method of Partington et al. (2011) unchanged, built into a version of HydroGeoSphere. Partington et al. (2013, §3.3) add overland-flow fractions, sub-time-steps and cell resets. The HMC ratio, the outflow over a step divided by the cell's storage at the start of the step (Eq. 2), must stay below 1; where it exceeds 1 in any cell, each flow step is split into s = [max. HMC ratio] + 1 sub-steps, and the fluxes and storage changes are split among them (Eqs 3–9). A cell that meets any of five criteria, such as an HMC ratio above 10⁴ or |1 − Σf| > 0.5, is reset: its fractions are set to 0 and its water is assigned to a tracked "reset" fraction (§3.3.3). The 2013 mixing cells are still surface cells only, stream and overland nodes (§3.3.1); tracking in the subsurface is recommended as future work (§6, para. 73). <!-- table1_methods.md, footnote 3 and Sources, Hydraulic mixing cells; Partington et al. 2013 Eqs 2–9, §3.3.3 --> Fractions in aquifer cells came later: Schilling et al. (2017) [check: not read], as an Aquanty research summary describes it, and Nogueira et al. (2022, Sect. 2.2, Eq. 1), in every model cell, surface and subsurface, of a riparian zone. All versions read step forward in time only.

⁴ GWT also offers central-in-space and TVD advection, dispersion and reactions (Langevin et al. 2022). With upstream weighting and no dispersion its steady limit equals G (Table 2). The abstract of Bedekar et al. (2024) and the authors' slides (Bedekar et al. 2023) do not say which advection scheme or whether dispersion was used.

⁵ After the modified mixing cell of Campana and Simpson (1984): water leaving a cell over a step carries the previous step's mix, and water entering stays until the next step.

⁶ Foley and Black (2013) define all water starting in or passing through a destination cell as reaching the destination, so a weak-sink well is credited with all the water passing through its cell.

⁷ A closed recirculation loop has no outflow to any sink or to a cell outside the loop; it is given F = 0 and reported. With net accounting, the smallest-flow connection in each loop can instead be deleted, as in the reference method (`remove_cycles`).

⁸ Volume through, volume from (by budget term) and fraction from; the forward fraction as C/C0; statistics over time steps.

⁹ Also boundary-control maps (the fractions to all sinks, which add up to 1 in every cell), mean travel time, statistics over steps and, in tracking, the fractions leaving through other sinks and still stored.

¹⁰ K flow components: groundwater, unsaturated flow, overland flow, preferential flow and direct rainfall. Their fractions are updated together and sum to 1 in each cell. Initial fractions are assumed, and a dummy fraction holds the initial water until it is flushed out.

¹¹ Langevin et al. (2022) document no backward-in-time option. A backward steady run would need a flow field with reversed flows and swapped sources and sinks: possible, but awkward. The abstract of Bedekar et al. (2024) lists well capture analysis among the uses without saying how it is done. The authors' conference slides on the method (Bedekar et al. 2023) show only forward runs: four transient simulations, one per tagged source (lateral inflow, recharge, storage, stream), with the concentration at a pumping well read as each source's share of the pumped water over 25 years. Nothing in them runs backward or on reversed flows. The slides name no code and do not discuss weak sinks.

¹² Also a report of closed recirculation loops. In tracking: the forward–backward check in time and the water and tagged-water balances.

¹³ With unit concentration the solute mass is the tagged water volume, so the GWT mass budget, by term and step, is a tagged-water balance. The GWT budget file holds the solute mass flows of each cell by term (intercell, storage, source and sink mixing, advanced packages) (MODFLOW 6 input/output guide [check: author, year]), and the list file reports the mass budget of the whole model with its percent discrepancy at each saved step (seen in our runs).

¹⁴ Relative error e = 1 − Σf, from the fractions summing to 1. The global volume error compares the groundwater volume in the stream cells at the end with the exfiltration summed over the run, from the model's water balance; it holds only on reaches that gain all along. It compares a traced volume with a water-budget total, close to our closure check.

¹⁵ DIS, DISV and DISU grids; GWF-GWF exchanges; SFR reaches, lakes, MAW wells and UZF cells added as extra cells and linked by MVR.

¹⁶ Stream cells of the HydroGeoSphere surface domain. Partington et al. (2011) say the method can be implemented in any code that reports surface–subsurface exchange in a spatially distributed way.

¹⁷ MODALL (Potter et al. 2008) computes, from MODFLOW cell-by-cell flows, the share of flow in each cell that came from a source or goes to a sink (abstract), for steady-state models, assuming full mixing and without the reference method's flow-order sort (Chowdhury 2016, §2.3.3 and p. 103; Foley and Black 2013). How it solves, how it treats weak sinks and what it does with flow loops could not be established.
