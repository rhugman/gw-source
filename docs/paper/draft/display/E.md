## Figure S1

**Figure S1.** Gross and net accounting, and the full-mixing zones against PRT; Neversink calibrated model, streams as cells. (a) Water attributed to each priority well. Gross accounting (filled) credits a well with its pumping; net accounting (open), as in the reference method (Foley and Black 2013), sets F = 1 in the well cell and credits the well with all the water passing through that cell. The two differ most where the well takes a small share of its cell's outflow: 44 % at U___7 (net accounting gives 2.26 times the pumping) and 69 % at SV_487 (1.43 times). At SV_227 net accounting gives 2 % less than the pumping, because it leaves out the 3.7 m³/d source term of the well cell. For the eight wells together, net accounting attributes 3,944 m³/d against 3,671 m³/d pumped; the per-well net values add up to 4,621 m³/d, because water passing from one well cell to another counts for both wells. (b–d) Published start cells (uppermost active layer) in the F > 0.1 and F > 0.5 zones of gross accounting, with the outline of the zone where weight-losing PRT particles, released over the cell volume, deliver more than half of the released water to the wells (P > 0.5, 691 cells). Particles were released only in cells with F > 10⁻⁶ (29,201 start cells, including all 2,139 with F > 0.1); start cells without particles have P = 0. The F > 0.1 zone holds 87 % of the PRT zone. The 89 cells it misses (orange, open squares), 86 of them in a strip west of the U wells, include 74 published contributing cells. Of the F > 0.5 zone (445 cells), 92 % lies inside the PRT zone. Weight-losing particles lose weight at every sink in the same proportion as full mixing, so the PRT zone is not an independent advective zone. The flow field has no flow loops. White: no published start cell. Coordinates in km from the grid origin.
