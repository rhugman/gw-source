## Figure 13

**Figure 13.** Numerical dispersion of full mixing against cell size h. Top row: problem A, uniform flow to a well, with the analytical capture zone (Javandel and Tsang 1986) as reference. Flow runs along the grid rows (solid) or at 30° to them (dotted). Bottom row: problem B, an aquifer like test model 1 ({SI: example and test models}). The reference is PRT on the finest grid, compared west of the river. (a, d) Width of the transition band (0.1 < F < 0.9). Dashed guide: $h^{0.5}$. Face routing: an experimental 2D option that passes water between cell faces instead of mixing it (Chowdhury 2016; Section 6). The exact zone averaged onto the grid (A) and PRT on the same grid (B) show the narrowest band the grid allows. (b, e) Relative error in the size of the F > 0.1 area. (c, f) The same for the F > 0.5 area. Grey band, ±5 %. The test problems and the numbers behind the panels are given in {SI: numerical dispersion maps and time steps}.

## Figure 9

**Figure 9.** Snapshots and transient tracking on the seasonal example model (Section 4.8). Triangle: supply well. Dashed line in (c–f): river. (a) Snapshots: the well's water by source, each step traced on its own flows. (b) Forward transient tracking: the well's water by tagged source, including initial water. The dashed line is the initial water's share of all water pumped so far. Its labels give the cumulative share after 1, 10 and 30 years. (c) Steady-state F for the first step's flows. F extends east of the river because the river cells take only part of the water flowing through them. (d–f) Backward tracking in time: for the water in each cell at the start, the fractions that leave through the well (w) or the river within 30 years, and the fraction still stored. Numbers are in {SI: transient checks on the seasonal example model}.
