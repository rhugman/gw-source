# Full-mixing flow tracing for MODFLOW 6: contributing areas, budget checks and transient tracking

**Article Impact Statement:** Well contributing areas from MODFLOW 6 in seconds, checked against the water budget, including how much pumped water was already stored.

**Abstract:** Contributing areas of supply wells are usually delineated by particle tracking, which needs a stop-or-pass rule at every weak sink. Full-mixing flow tracing treats each cell as fully mixed and computes from the cell-by-cell flows the fraction of each cell's water that leaves through a destination (backward) or came from a source (forward). One full-mixing method, here called the reference method, is used for source protection zones in England but is described only in conference papers and has no open implementation. We write it as one sparse linear system per direction, solved exactly. Two exact identities check every trace against the water budget: traced volumes add up to each destination's or source's inflow, and backward and forward tracing agree. For transient models, tagged water is carried through storage; with the water volumes of MODFLOW 6 transport, forward tracking matches its dispersion-free unit-concentration runs to round-off. <!-- verification_table.md, transient tracking checks: tracking vs GWT ≤ 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ (model 6), 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ (model 7), "gwt" volumes --> We add backward tracking in time, run from the end of the simulation to its start: for the water in each cell at each time, it gives the fractions that leave through the destination or other sinks, or stay stored. On a seasonal test model, 81 % of the water pumped in the first ten years had been in the aquifer at the start. <!-- README_method.md, Fig. 4 caption (b); studies/paper/transient_figure.py --> The code reads every MODFLOW 6 grid type, multi-model simulations and package networks. On the USGS Neversink/Rondout model (1.68 million cells) a trace takes 2–3 s.
