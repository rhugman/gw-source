# Where does my well's water come from? Open-source flow tracing for MODFLOW 6

**Article Impact Statement:** Open-source code turns saved MODFLOW 6 flows into contributing areas, water sources and travel times for any well or stream, in steady-state or transient flow, fast enough for ensembles.

**Abstract:** Water managers ask which land supplies the water that a well pumps, to protect that land from pollution. This land is the well's contributing area. We present open-source code, `gwsource`, that maps this area from the flows a MODFLOW 6 groundwater model already saves. The code treats the water in each cell as fully mixed. It extends the full-mixing method of FlowSource to MODFLOW 6. It computes the share of each cell's water that ends at a well, a stream or another outlet. The same calculation splits a well's water by source and gives mean travel times. For transient models, the code carries tagged water through storage, forward and backward in time. Every result is checked against the water budget. In ten small worked examples, each output a practitioner would use is checked against another approach. One example reproduces a published unit-concentration study. Results agreed with MODFLOW 6 transport to within its solver tolerance with the same water volumes. They agreed with particle tracking in total volumes and mean travel times, but not cell by cell at the edges of areas. On a regional model of 1.68 million cells, one trace of nine well groups took 2–3 s. At this cost, the code can trace every model of an ensemble, before and after history matching. Full-mixing areas are wider and more diffuse than particle-tracked areas. Where to draw an area's edge is therefore a judgement. Particle tracking remains the better tool where a sharp edge or a first arrival matters.
