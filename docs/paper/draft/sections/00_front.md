# Full-mixing flow tracing for MODFLOW 6: contributing areas, budget checks and transient tracking

**Article Impact Statement:** Open full-mixing tracing for MODFLOW 6: contributing areas in seconds, checked against the water budget, also backward in time.

**Abstract:** Contributing areas of supply wells are usually delineated by particle tracking, which handles weak sinks, transient flow and many destinations with difficulty. Full-mixing flow tracing treats each cell as fully mixed and computes from the cell-by-cell flows the fraction of each cell's water that leaves through a destination (backward) or came from a source (forward). The reference method, used for source protection zones in England, lacks a peer-reviewed description and open code. We write it as one sparse linear system per direction, solved exactly with or without flow loops. Weak-sink wells are credited with their pumping by default; the reference method's accounting is an option. Two exact identities check every result against the water budget: traced volumes add up to each destination's or source's inflow, and backward and forward tracing agree. For transient models, tagged water is carried through storage; forward, this matches dispersion-free unit-concentration transport in MODFLOW 6 to round-off. <!-- verification_table.md, transient tracking checks: tracking vs GWT ≤ 2.2e-15 (model 6), 1.0e-14 (model 7) --> What is added is backward tracking in time: the fractions of each cell's water, at each time, that leave through the destination or other sinks or stay stored. On a seasonal test model, 81 % of the water pumped in the first ten years had been in the aquifer at the start. <!-- README_method.md, Fig. 4 caption (b); studies/paper/transient_figure.py --> The code reads every MODFLOW 6 grid type, multi-model simulations and package networks. On the USGS Neversink/Rondout model (1.68 million cells) a trace takes 2–3 s; we attribute its differences from published particle-tracked contributing areas to particle conventions, mixing and stream treatment. <!-- neversink_study.md, Summary "Model and benchmark" and "Cost"; §1 (1,683,680 cells) -->
