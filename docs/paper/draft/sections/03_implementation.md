# 3. Implementation

## 3.1 Reading MODFLOW 6 output

The code needs only MODFLOW 6 output, not the model's input files (Langevin et al. 2017). The binary grid file gives the cell connections of structured (DIS), vertex (DISV) and unstructured (DISU) grids. The flows come from the GWF budget files. The user saves them with SAVE_FLOWS in each package and SAVE BUDGET ALL in the OC file. A package that passes water to the water mover (MVR) writes two records per boundary, such as DRN and DRN-TO-MVR. Both are kept, since the cell budget closes only with both. An unread budget term shows up as a large cell budget error. <!-- moved from sections/02_methods.md:61 at 43a6c59 ("A budget term that was not read shows up instead as a large cell budget error."); src/gwsource/diagnostics.py docstring (r_i = I_i - O_i over the budget terms read, so an unread term enters r_i) --> SFR reaches, lakes (LAK), multi-aquifer wells (MAW) and unsaturated-zone cells (UZF) can be added as extra cells from their own budget files, so water is traced through stream and lake networks too. For each package–aquifer exchange, the code checks that the two budgets agree, by default to within 1 × 10⁻⁴ of the largest exchange. <!-- network.py, packages.py; PLAN Phase 4A notes (AQUIFER_RTOL = 1e-4) --> GWF models joined by exchanges are read as one system.

## 3.2 Selections, outputs and particle tracking

Destinations and sources can be packages, boundnames, cells or zones. <!-- src/gwsource/transient.py docstring (TransientConfig statistics: running statistics such as time-weighted means per cell for snapshots; cut from the text in round 5, r4_reader C2) --> With long time steps, tagged water arrives too early, because transient tracking solves each step implicitly. Splitting each flow step into equal transport sub-steps prevents this ({SI: transient checks on the seasonal example model}). <!-- src/gwsource/tracking.py docstring (Sub-steps: implicit upstream stepping adds numerical dispersion in time of order dt; volumes interpolated linearly between V_old and V_new) --> <!-- tutorial 08 §9; README_method.md Fig. 4 notes (one transport step per 91-day flow step makes early arrivals too early) -->

The code also writes release points for PRT, the particle-tracking model built into MODFLOW 6 (Langevin et al. 2026). PRT works like MODPATH (Pollock 2016) but runs inside MODFLOW 6. The two methods can then be compared on the same problem (Section 5.3). Particles start where water enters the model through a source term. Each particle carries a weight, which is the flow of water it represents. Particle weights then add up to flows. <!-- src/gwsource/prt.py docstring (release points, face terms: particles of a flow assigned to one cell face (IFLOWFACE) start on that face; sentence cut in round 5 for length) --> When a particle passes through a cell with a sink, it loses the share of its weight that the sink takes from the cell's water. It carries the rest on. The destination is credited with its part. We refer to these as weight-losing particles. <!-- src/gwsource/prt.py docstring ("Weak sinks and the destination": d_j / O_j x W to the destination, keeps (1 - Snk_j / O_j) x W, computed along the PRT tracks) --> These particles treat weak sinks as gross accounting does (Section 2.3). Any difference from full mixing therefore comes from how water spreads between sinks: along particle paths, or by mixing in every cell.

On the USGS Neversink/Rondout model (Fienen et al. 2022; Corson-Dosch et al. 2022; Section 5), with 1.68 million cells, a steady-state trace of nine destination groups took 2–3 s (Section 5.1).
