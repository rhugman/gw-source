# 3. Implementation

## 3.1 Reading MODFLOW 6 output

The code reads only MODFLOW 6 output (Langevin et al. 2017). The binary grid file gives the cell connections of structured (DIS), vertex (DISV) and unstructured (DISU) grids alike. The GWF budget files give the flows, saved with SAVE_FLOWS and, in the Output Control (OC) file, SAVE BUDGET ALL. A package that passes water to the water mover (MVR) writes two records per boundary, such as DRN and DRN-TO-MVR. Both are kept, since the cell budget closes only with both. SFR reaches, lakes (LAK), multi-aquifer wells (MAW) and unsaturated-zone cells (UZF) can be added as extra cells from their own budget files, so water is traced through stream and lake networks too. The GWF and package budgets must agree on each aquifer exchange, by default to 1 × 10⁻⁴ of the largest exchange. <!-- network.py, packages.py; PLAN Phase 4A notes (AQUIFER_RTOL = 1e-4) --> GWF models joined by exchanges are read as one system.

## 3.2 Selections, outputs and particle tracking

Destinations and sources are selected by package, boundname, cells or zone. For snapshots (Section 2.7), running statistics such as time-weighted means are kept per cell. <!-- src/gwsource/transient.py docstring (TransientConfig statistics) --> Each flow step can be split into equal transport sub-steps, which stop tagged water from arriving too early when flow steps are long ({SI: transient checks on the seasonal example model}). <!-- tutorial 08 §9; README_method.md Fig. 4 notes (one transport step per 91-day flow step makes early arrivals too early) -->

We also write release points for MODFLOW 6 particle tracking (PRT; Langevin et al. 2026), so that the same problem can be run with particles and the two methods compared (Section 5.3). Particles start from every source term, each with a weight, the share of that term's flow it represents. Particle sums are then volumes of water. Particles of a flow assigned to one cell face (IFLOWFACE) start on that face. <!-- src/gwsource/prt.py docstring (release points, face terms) --> In each cell a weight-losing particle passes, it loses the share of its weight that the cell's sinks take. The destination's part of that loss is credited to the destination. <!-- src/gwsource/prt.py docstring ("Weak sinks and the destination": d_j / O_j x W to the destination, keeps (1 - Snk_j / O_j) x W, computed along the PRT tracks) --> These particles apply gross accounting (Section 2.3) along a particle path. Compared with full mixing, they test only how water spreads between sinks.

## 3.3 Compute cost

Cost decides whether a trace can be repeated over every realisation of an ensemble. A group is a destination traced as one unit, such as one well. A steady trace of nine groups on the 1.68-million-cell Neversink model took 2–3 s (Section 5.1), of which about 1 s was reading the budget. <!-- NS Summary, §1; reviews/r4_memory.md (load 1.0 to 1.2 s, trace 1.4 to 1.5 s, load plus trace 2.4 to 2.7 s) --> It peaked at 2.5 GB of memory, of which 0.5 GB was the loaded budget (Apple M4 Max, 64 GB). Each further group added about 0.19 GB, or about 115 MB per million cells. One hundred wells traced separately would need about 20 GB, so wells are best grouped or traced in batches. <!-- reviews/r4_memory.md (peak RSS 2.52 GB, nine groups; load only 0.52 GB; one group 0.98 GB; (2.52 − 0.98) / 8 = 0.19 GB per group; 0.5 + 0.3 + 100 × 0.19 ≈ 20 GB, an estimate, not run) --> On one million synthetic cells, the solve for four groups took 0.55 s, or 0.42 s with 7,000 flow loops. <!-- PLAN Phase 2 notes (Benchmarks: macOS, 16 cores, 64 GB; assembly 0.24, components 0.10, closed-loop check 0.10, solve 0.55, metrics 0.11 s; with 7,000 injected cycles, largest 649 cells, block solve 0.42 s); benchmarks/bench_solver.py -->
