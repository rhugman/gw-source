# 8. Conclusions

FlowSource (Foley and Black 2013; Black and Foley 2013) traces groundwater model flows under full mixing to give the share of each cell's water that reaches a well. It is not open source. We extended this approach in an open-source code for MODFLOW 6. The code solves the tracing exactly, including flow loops, where water circulates between cells. Gross accounting, the default, credits a weak-sink well with what it pumps. Net accounting is kept as an option. Every trace carries water-budget checks that must hold exactly once the cell budget errors are included. The code reads every MODFLOW 6 grid type and boundary package, multi-model simulations and package networks. Transient tracking carries tagged water through storage from step to step. Run backward in time, it gives the fractions of each cell's water that reach the well, leave through other sinks or stay stored. Snapshots cannot give these. A steady trace on 1.68 million cells takes 2–3 s, fast enough for ensembles.

The checks held on every test model. Forward transient tracking matched the unit-concentration transport of MODFLOW 6 GWT. Tested against a published real-world regional model, full mixing gave contributing areas within the range that particle tracking gives under different particle settings on the same flow field. The particle settings changed the area as much as the choice of method did. The treatment of streams changed it too. A protection zone should therefore be reported with its method, its particle settings, its stream representation and its threshold.
