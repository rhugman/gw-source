# 8. Conclusions

We write the reference method of full-mixing flow tracing (Foley and Black 2013; Black and Foley 2013) as one sparse linear system per direction, solved exactly with or without flow loops, with gross accounting by default and the reference method's net accounting as an option. Every result carries its own check: the closure and forward–backward checks are exact identities once the cell budget errors are included, and they held on the synthetic test models and on the Neversink model. For transient models, transient tracking carries tagged water through storage: forward, it matches the dispersion-free unit-concentration transport of MF6 GWT, and backward in time it gives, for the water in each cell, the fractions that reach the destination, leave through other sinks or stay stored, which snapshots cannot give. The same code reads every MF6 grid type, multi-model simulations and package networks, and steady traces of a regional model are cheap enough for ensembles and pumping scenarios. On Neversink, full mixing lies within the spread of particle results under different conventions on the same flow field, particle conventions change the result as much as the choice of method, and the treatment of streams changes the contributing area; a protection zone should therefore be reported with its method, its conventions and its threshold.
