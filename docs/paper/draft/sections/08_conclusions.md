# 8. Conclusions

This work provides open-source code that traces MODFLOW 6 flows under full mixing, checks every trace against the water budget and carries water through storage in transient models. It builds on FlowSource (Foley and Black 2013; Black and Foley 2013), which is used to draw protection zones in England (Environment Agency 2019). <!-- EA 2019 manual, references/docs/text/2019_EnvironmentAgency_SPZ_manual.txt, pp. 76–78 (total capture zones checked against the full-mixing outputs) --> The code solves the tracing exactly, even where water circulates between cells in flow loops. By default it credits a weak-sink well with what it pumps. Net accounting is kept for reproducing FlowSource studies. The code reads every MODFLOW 6 grid type, multi-model simulations and package networks. Forward transient tracking gives the share of pumped water that was already stored, as a GWT transport run would. Backward tracking in time adds an area for a stated time horizon, which snapshots cannot give. A steady trace on 1.68 million cells takes 2–3 s, so all 358 realisations of a published ensemble could be traced (Section 5.5).

The checks held on every test model. Forward transient tracking matched MODFLOW 6 GWT transport. We tested the method against a published real-world regional model. Full mixing agreed less with its particle-tracked areas than particle runs did, mainly because mixing spreads each well's water over more cells. Particle settings alone moved the particle runs' overlap with those areas from 0.65 to 0.998. A protection zone should therefore be reported with its accounting, threshold, stream representation and closure check, and with the settings of any particle check (Section 6.4).
