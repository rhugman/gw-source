# 7. Conclusions

We present `gwsource`. It is open-source code that traces where the water in a MODFLOW 6 model comes from and where it goes. It works from the cell-by-cell flows the model already saves. For a well, a spring, a stream or any other outlet, it maps the contributing area, splits the water by source and gives mean travel times. From a source, such as recharge or a river, it follows the water to where it leaves the model. Ten worked examples, each a runnable notebook, show how to use it.

The code builds on the full-mixing method of FlowSource (Foley and Black 2013). It extends that method to every MODFLOW 6 grid type, to package networks such as streams, movers and multi-aquifer wells, and to simulations made of several linked models. At weak sinks it uses gross accounting by default. Each sink term then takes only its share of the cell's water, as in a transport model. In transient models, tagged water is carried through storage from step to step. Backward in time, the same tracking maps the area whose water reaches a well within a stated time. <!-- Sections 4.4, 4.7, 4.8 and 4.10 -->

Every result comes with water-budget checks that must hold exactly. Each example also compares an output a practitioner would use with another common approach. The code reproduced the worked example of Foley and Black (2013) and the unit-concentration study of Bedekar et al. (2024). With GWT's water volumes, its fractions matched MODFLOW 6 GWT to 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ or better in transient runs. Particles in MODFLOW 6 PRT delivered the same water to the well, with nearly the same mean travel time. Any method that conserves water should agree in these totals. Cell by cell, the two methods differ at the edge of a contributing area, where full mixing gives in-between values. <!-- Sections 4.1 (799.9 of 800 m³/d; edge cells), 4.2 (Foley and Black 2013 to 10⁻¹⁴), 4.3 (steady state, 4.2 × 10⁻¹³), 4.8 (9.8 × 10⁻¹⁴, GWT's water volumes) and 4.10 (1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰, GWT's water volumes; Bedekar et al. 2024), 4.6 (−0.5 %; the agreement in totals and means is not an independent test, review alt_r1 F2 and F4) -->

On small models the answers come in seconds. On a published regional model of 1.68 million cells, one trace of nine destination groups took 2–3 s. That is small next to the model run itself. Tracing is therefore cheap enough for ensembles of hundreds of realisations and for history matching. <!-- Sections 3.4 and 5 -->

Full mixing has costs. Its areas are wider and more diffuse than particle areas. How much wider depends on the cell size and on how the cell faces lie relative to the flow. The threshold that draws an area therefore needs judgement and should be reported with it. Particle tracking is the better tool where a sharp edge or a first arrival matters. Transport models are better where dispersion or reactions matter (Section 6). <!-- Sections 4.4 and 6 (edge band and grid orientation) -->
