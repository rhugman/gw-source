# 7. Conclusions

We present `gwsource`. It is open-source code that traces where the water in a MODFLOW 6 model comes from and where it goes. It works from the cell-by-cell flows the model already saves. For a well, a spring, a stream or any other outlet, it maps the contributing area, splits the water by source and gives mean travel times. From a source, such as recharge or a river, it follows the water to where it leaves the model. The code builds on the full-mixing method of FlowSource (Foley and Black 2013). It extends the method to MODFLOW 6, with every grid type, package networks such as streams, movers and multi-aquifer wells, and simulations made of several linked models. It also extends the method to transient flow, with tagged water carried through storage. Ten worked examples, each a runnable notebook, show how to use it.

Every result comes with checks against the flow model's water budget that must hold exactly. The worked examples check each output a practitioner would use against other commonly used approaches. The code reproduced the worked example of Foley and Black (2013) and the unit-concentration study of Bedekar et al. (2024). Its fractions matched MODFLOW 6 GWT to 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ or better, in transient runs when both used GWT's water volumes. Particles in MODFLOW 6 PRT delivered the same water to the well, with nearly the same mean travel time. Agreement in these totals is expected of any method that conserves water. Cell by cell, the two methods differ at the edge of a contributing area, where full mixing gives in-between values. <!-- Sections 4.1 (799.9 of 800 m³/d; edge cells), 4.2 (Foley and Black 2013 to 10⁻¹⁴), 4.3 (steady state, 4.2 × 10⁻¹³), 4.8 (9.8 × 10⁻¹⁴, GWT's water volumes) and 4.10 (1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰, GWT's water volumes; Bedekar et al. 2024), 4.6 (−0.5 %; the agreement in totals and means is not an independent test, review alt_r1 F2 and F4) -->

The answers come in seconds on small models. On a published regional model of 1.68 million cells, one trace of nine destination groups took 2–3 s. That is small next to the model run itself. Tracing is cheap enough to run on every realisation of an ensemble of hundreds. It is also cheap enough to include in history matching and similar analyses. <!-- Sections 3.4 and 5 -->

The code handles weak sinks. Each sink term takes its share of the cell's water. The rest passes on to the neighbouring cells. Transient tracking runs forward and backward in time. Backward tracking in time maps the area whose water reaches a well within a stated time. <!-- Sections 4.4, 4.7, 4.8 and 4.10 -->

Full mixing has costs. Its areas are wider and more diffuse than particle areas. The threshold that draws an area therefore needs judgement and should be reported with it. Particle tracking remains the better tool where a sharp edge or a first arrival matters. Transport models remain the better tool where dispersion or reactions matter (Section 6).
