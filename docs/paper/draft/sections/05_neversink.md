# 5. Neversink Demonstration and Benchmark

The Neversink/Rondout model (Fienen et al. 2022; Corson-Dosch et al. 2022) is a published real-world regional model in steady state, so only the steady-state method is tested here. <!-- NS §1, §6; outline §5.5 --> Its published contributing areas come from one choice of particle settings, so they are a benchmark, not the true answer. We rerun particles on the same flow field to show how far the particle settings alone move the contributing areas of the wells. Particles that lose water at each sink as full mixing does then isolate the effect of mixing (Section 5.3). <!-- NS §3, §5 ("The published mask is a convention") -->

## 5.1 Model and benchmark

The USGS model (Fienen et al. 2022; Corson-Dosch et al. 2022) has four layers of 680 × 619 cells of 50 m and 12,316 SFR reaches. <!-- NS §1 --> <!-- the rerun with MODFLOW 6 version 6.9 (heads within 8.6 mm) is in the Fig. 5 caption --> The benchmark is the published MODPATH 7 (Pollock 2016) delineation for eight priority wells. They pump 3,671 m³/d in three clusters (Fig. 5). <!-- NS §1; figures/README.md Fig. 5 --> In the published runs, one particle starts in the uppermost active cell of each column in the three regions, 122,021 particles in all. We refer to these cells as the start cells. <!-- NS §1, §2 --> A particle stops in the first weak-sink cell it enters. That cell's sink is credited with all of the particle's water. <!-- NS §1, §2 --> The published contributing cells are the 980 start cells whose particle ends in a priority well. <!-- NS §1, §2; mp7_status.csv -->

## 5.2 Full mixing against the published area

To measure how well two areas agree, we use an overlap score, the Jaccard index. It is the number of cells that lie in both areas, divided by the number of cells that lie in either. It is 1 for identical areas and 0 for areas with no cell in common. <!-- NS §2 -->

F is the share of a cell's water that ends at the wells (Section 2.2), so drawing an area needs a threshold on F. The F > 0.5 area holds the cells where more than half the water ends at the wells. The F > 0.1 area, where more than a tenth does, is wider and more cautious (Section 4.3). The two areas overlap the published contributing cells at 0.41 and 0.30 (Fig. 6a–c; per well, {SI: per-well gross and net}). <!-- NS §2, Tables 1–2; agreement_with_mp7.csv; the area-matched overlap, 0.41 for the 980 start cells with the largest F, is in the SI --> Adding up F over the start cells gives the expected area, 910 cells, against 980 published contributing cells. <!-- NS §2, Table 1 -->

[[Figure 6]]

The F > 0.5 area has 445 cells, of which 94 % are published contributing cells. It covers 43 % of the published area. <!-- NS §2, Table 1 (417 of 445) --> The F > 0.1 area has 2,139 start cells, 2.2 times the published area. It holds 73 % of the published cells (Fig. 7a–c). <!-- NS §2, Table 1 and text after it (711 of 980 published cells inside F > 0.1), §5 --> Part of the difference lies at weak-sink wells, where published particles stop but full mixing passes water on ({SI: end states of the published particles}). <!-- NS §2; net_vs_gross_wells.csv (U___7 takes 44 % of its cell's outflow; full mixing passes the other 56 % on) -->

The results above use gross accounting, the default of the code (Section 2.3). Gross accounting credits each well with the water it pumps. The water traced to the wells then adds up to their inflow, 3,671 m³/d, so no water is counted twice. The published particles work more like net accounting. A particle that enters a weak-sink well cell stops there. The well is then credited with all of the particle's water, including the part that would have flowed on. Net accounting credits the wells with 3,944 m³/d, 7 % more than they pump. It raises the expected area to 978 cells. It changes the overlaps little, from 0.41 to 0.44 for the F > 0.5 area and from 0.30 to 0.28 for the F > 0.1 area ({SI: delineations on one flow field}; {SI: net attribution and PRT-area maps}). <!-- si/D.md Table S3 (gross 3,671, net 3,944 m³/d; expected area 910 gross, 978 net; overlaps 0.41/0.44 and 0.30/0.28); NS §2, Tables 2a–2b; net_vs_gross_wells.csv, net_vs_gross_mp7.csv -->

## 5.3 Particle tracking on the same flow field

Section 5.2 judged full mixing against the published area. That area is not the true answer. It comes from one choice of particle settings. Other reasonable choices give other areas. Before reading the overlaps of Section 5.2 as a measure of error, a user needs to know how far the particle settings alone move the area. We therefore reran particles with PRT on the same flow field. Some runs vary the particle settings. Others use particles that lose water at each sink as full mixing does, so that any remaining difference comes from mixing itself.

On the same flow field, PRT (Section 3.2) gives overlaps with the published area from 0.29 to 0.998, depending on the particle settings ({SI: delineations on one flow field}). <!-- NS §3, Table 3 and reproduction table; prt_comparison.csv, mp7_reproduction.csv --> The two lowest values come from runs that do not conserve water: particles that lose weight only at the wells deliver 2.6 times the wells' inflow (overlap 0.29), and particles released at the water table deliver 66 % of it (2,413 m³/d; overlap 0.39; {SI: diagnosis of the first PRT runs}). <!-- NS §3, Table 3; prt_comparison.csv (9,555 and 2,413 of 3,671 m³/d); prt_diagnosis.csv; decisions_r4 item 3 allows one sentence for both --> The particle runs that conserve water give overlaps of 0.62–0.998. Full mixing gives 0.30–0.41, because it splits each cell's water over all its outflows. <!-- NS §2, §3, Table 3 and reproduction table; agreement_with_mp7.csv, prt_comparison.csv, mp7_reproduction.csv -->

PRT reproduces the published area only with all three published settings. First, recharge and stream flows enter through the top face of a cell where that face is open (IFLOWFACE). Second, particles stop at every weak sink. Third, a particle that starts in a dry cell is moved down to the first active cell below, which MODPATH calls draping (DrapeOption). With all three, the overlap is 0.998. With boundary flows spread through the cell, the MODFLOW 6 default, it is 0.62–0.65, with or without draping. <!-- NS §2, §3 reproduction table; mp7_reproduction.csv (0.998, with 99.0 % of particles ending in the same cell as published; 0.65 draped, 0.62 dropped), mp7_status.csv --> The weak sinks include buried stream cells. A buried stream cell holds a gaining SFR reach but has another active cell above it. Its stream takes only part of the water that flows through the cell. The rest flows on to other cells. <!-- terms.md (buried stream cells); NS §2; si/F_editor.md status 2 and 3 (every particle stopped in a stream cell has an active cell above it) --> With the published settings, a particle that enters such a cell stops there. A particle that reaches a stream cell with no active cell above ends at the stream instead. <!-- si/F_editor.md (status 2 against status 3); NS §2 (MPBAS IFACE 6) -->

The like-for-like test uses weight-losing particles (Section 3.2). They were released from the source terms of all cells with F > 10⁻⁶, each weighted by its flow. With release over the cell volume, they delivered 3,736 m³/d against a well inflow of 3,671 m³/d (+1.8 %). <!-- NS §3, Table 3; prt_comparison.csv --> Each cell's particle share P is, like F, the share of its water that reaches the wells, but along particle paths. The P > 0.5 area overlaps the published cells at 0.62. <!-- NS §3, Table 3; agreement_with_mp7.csv (0.62 for release over the cell volume and at mid-cell) -->

[[Figure 7]]

Full mixing gives wider and more diffuse areas than these particles, which each follow one path. The F > 0.1 area is therefore the counterpart of the P > 0.5 area, not the F > 0.5 area. The F > 0.1 area holds 87 % of the 691 cells of the P > 0.5 area (Fig. 7d–f). The F > 0.5 area holds 59 % of the P > 0.5 area, and 92 % of its cells lie inside it ({SI: net attribution and PRT-area maps}). <!-- NS §5, Table 3a; evidence: studies/neversink/output/envelope_prt.csv (all, P>0.5: F>0.1 coverage 0.871, 89 missed, 226.7 of 3,736 m³/d; F>0.5 coverage 0.590, f_zone_inside_prt 0.917) --> These shares are upper bounds, because particles were released only where F > 10⁻⁶, and they test only the spreading between sinks, because the particles lose weight at sinks as full mixing does. <!-- NS §5, Table 3a; envelope_prt_notes.csv --> We attribute the gap between full mixing and these particles to that spreading, and the further gap to the published area to the published particle settings.

## 5.4 Stream representation

With streams as cells, SFR leakage is a source term of the aquifer cell. In the calibrated model it supplies 43 % of the wells' water. We refer to this as the stream share. The area stops at the streams (Fig. 6a–c). <!-- NS §4; sources_cells.csv (1,571 of 3,671 m³/d) --> With the stream network, the SFR reaches become extra cells linked by stream routing. Leakage is traced up the stream to where its water entered. All the wells' water then traces to recharge, and the area extends upstream along the streams (Fig. 6d–f). <!-- NS §4 ("All 3,671 m³/d then traces to recharge, none to the stream's external inflow or to CHD"); evidence: studies/neversink/output/sources_sfr_network.csv (priority: RCHA 3,671.0, CHD_0 0, SFR_0:EXT-INFLOW 0) --> <!-- dropped for length: the network's eight flow loops were solved exactly (NS §4; checks_sfr_network.csv, trace 2.6 s) -->

## 5.5 Ensemble at base pumping

The published study also mapped a probability of contribution for each cell. It is the share of the published Monte Carlo contributing areas that contain the cell. There are 357 such areas, because the calibrated realisation has none ({SI: Monte Carlo offsets and scenario rule}). <!-- NS §6, Table 4; si/D.md (runs 0–356 have areas; run 357, the calibrated realisation, has none); ensemble/probability_comparison.csv --> We traced all 358 realisations of the published ensemble, drawn with the PEST++ iterative ensemble smoother (White et al. 2020). <!-- NS §6; ensemble/summary.csv --> <!-- the reproduction of the 60 paired published areas (median overlap 0.996) is in the Fig. 8 caption -->

Our counterpart is the mean F over the realisations. It is a share of water averaged over realisations, not a share of realisations. Mean F correlates with the published probability at r = 0.80. Its expected area is 90 % of the published one. The areas above 0.5 overlap at 0.48 (Fig. 8; {SI: ensemble statistics}). <!-- NS §6, Table 4; ensemble/probability_comparison.csv -->

The published map is patchy, while mean F is smooth (Fig. 8). In each published realisation, one particle decides whether a start cell is in the area (Section 5.2). Neighbouring start cells can send their particles to different sinks. One realisation's area therefore breaks into strips and isolated cells. The patches remain after averaging over 357 realisations. We expect this is because all realisations share one stream network, so the gaps fall in similar places. F instead is the flow-weighted average of F in the cells that a cell drains to. It therefore changes gradually from one cell to the next. <!-- Section 5.2 (one particle per start cell, binary membership); Section 2.2 (F as flow-weighted average of downstream F); visual reading of Fig. 8; the shared-network reason is not tested -->

[[Figure 8]]

The two differ most in the S region (Fig. 5). There, the published area varies between realisations by 32 % (coefficient of variation). Across 60 realisations, the PRT area with the published settings follows it closely (r = 0.997 between the two areas). The expected area (sum of F) does not follow it (r = 0.00). <!-- NS §6 --> We infer that the same particle settings act on each realisation's flows and change where particles stop. <!-- NS §6 ("the differences between realisations show up through the particle conventions, not in the full-mixing capture"); which setting drives it is not isolated by region: at base pumping, stops at buried stream cells remove captured weight equal to about 2 cells in the SV_S cluster (scenarios/mechanisms60/stream_stops.csv, mean of 61 realisations), so the text does not name buried streams as the cause here -->

## 5.6 Pumping scenarios

Planned pumping may require a redrawn zone, so it matters how the area grows with pumping. The published scenarios raise pumping at the priority wells only, by a rule we inferred ({SI: Monte Carlo offsets and scenario rule}). <!-- NS §7, §8; scenarios.py --> At every rate, the median expected area is within 10 % of the published median ({SI: scenario medians}). <!-- NS §7, Table 5; scenarios/area_table.csv --> Pumping grows 3.68 times from base to maximum. The full-mixing area grows 4.09 times and the published area 3.60 times (Fig. 9). With the stream network, the full-mixing area grows 3.42 times, a little slower than pumping. <!-- NS §7, Table 5 (stream network 1,534 to 5,247 cells); scenarios/area_table.csv -->

[[Figure 9]]

The full-mixing area grows faster than pumping mainly because the stream share falls. Its median over the realisations drops from 42 % at base pumping to 28 % ({SI: growth mechanism}). <!-- NS §7; scenarios/source_table.csv --> Most of the added pumping is recharge that discharged to streams at base pumping. The recharge reaching the wells grows 4.57 times, and the area grows with it. <!-- NS §7 ("Where the stream water changes": 8,790 of 9,830 m³/d added pumping is reduced discharge to gaining reaches; recharge reaching the wells grows 4.57 times); mechanisms60/stream_by_state.csv -->

The published area grows more slowly, mainly because published particles stop at buried stream cells (Section 5.3). The area reaches these cells only at higher pumping. Full mixing passes on all but the stream's share of their outflow, so its area keeps growing past them. <!-- NS §7 ("Why the published area grows more slowly"); stream_stops.csv --> PRT with the published settings reproduces the published growth (3.62 against 3.60, 61 realisations). Particles that pass these cells and lose weight there as full mixing does grow even faster than full mixing ({SI: one convention at a time}; {SI: faster growth of weight-losing particles}). <!-- NS §7, Table 6 and bullets after it; mechanisms60/growth_decomposition.csv (r1 3.622, r2_thinned 4.76, r2_streams_thinned 3.83), stream_stops.csv -->
