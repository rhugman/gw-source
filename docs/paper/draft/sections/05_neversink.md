The published study also ran particles on 357 of the realisations. The published probability of contribution of a cell is the share of these runs whose contributing area holds the cell. Our counterpart is F averaged over the realisations. We refer to it as the ensemble mean F. Cell by cell, mean F correlates with the published probability at r = 0.80 (Fig. 12d). As in Section 4.1, the expected area of a map is the sum over cells of the map times the cell area. Here the sum runs over the start cells. ## Why the areas differ

Our areas differ from the published ones. We measure the difference with an overlap score. It is the number of cells in both areas divided by the number in either. It is 1 for identical areas and 0 for areas with no cell in common. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 (Jaccard index) -->

The published area comes from one reasonable choice of particle settings. Recharge and stream flows enter through the top face of a cell. A particle stops in the first weak-sink cell it enters. A particle that starts in a dry cell is moved down to the first active cell below (draping). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §2, §3 reproduction table --> PRT on the same flow field reproduces the published area (overlap 0.998) only with all three settings. Among particle runs that conserve water, the settings alone move the overlap from 0.62 to 0.998 ({SI: delineations on one flow field}). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §3, Table 3 and reproduction table; mp7_reproduction.csv (0.998; 0.65 draped, 0.62 dropped); prt_comparison.csv; the two runs that do not conserve water (0.29, 0.39) are in the SI table -->

The full-mixing areas where F exceeds 0.5 and 0.1 agree less, with overlaps of 0.41 and 0.30 (Fig. 12a, b). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2, Table 1; agreement_with_mp7.csv --> We infer that the main reason is that full mixing splits each cell's water among all its outflows, while a particle follows one path. Full-mixing areas are therefore wider and more diffuse. On average, the published cells send 42 % of their water to the wells. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 ("Their mean F is 0.42"; F > 0.1 contains 711 of the 980 published cells; 417 of the 445 F > 0.5 cells are published cells, 43 % of the published area); figures/README.md Fig. 7 --> The F > 0.1 area holds 73 % of them, against 43 % for the F > 0.5 area. A like-for-like test uses the weight-losing particles of Section 4.1. They lose water at each sink as full mixing does. Their particle share exceeds 0.5 in 691 start cells. The F > 0.1 area holds 87 % of these cells (Fig. 12c). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §5, Table 3a; envelope_prt.csv (all, P>0.5: F>0.1 coverage 0.871, 691 PRT cells) -->

Weak sinks add to the difference. A buried stream cell has an SFR reach and another active cell above it. A particle cannot leave such a cell through its top face. Where the reach gains water, the stream is a weak sink. A published particle then stops in the cell and credits all its water to the stream. Published particles stop in the same way at weak-sink wells. Full mixing passes the rest of the cell's water on ({SI: end states of the published particles}). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 "End states" (status 3: 26,828 stopped in stream cells with an active cell above; 466 in the five weak-sink priority wells); net_vs_gross_wells.csv (U___7 takes 44 % of its cell's outflow). Moved to the SI for length (review alt_r1, §5 cuts): crediting weak-sink wells as a stopping particle does (net accounting) raises the F > 0.5 overlap only from 0.41 to 0.44 (si/D.md Table S3; NS §2, Tables 2a–2b; net_vs_gross_mp7.csv), {SI: net attribution and PRT-area maps} -->

With the stream network, the area extends upstream along the streams. The expected area of the ensemble mean F is then 1.5 times the published one. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §6, Table 4 and text after it (sum ratio 1.51 for the network, "because it includes the upstream area that feeds the losing reaches"); probability_comparison.csv (1.508); dropped for length: published particles that reach a stream cell with no active cell above leave through its top face (NS §2 End states, status 2) --> Section 6 turns these differences into advice on thresholds and particle checks.
