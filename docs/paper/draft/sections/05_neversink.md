# 5. A regional model: Neversink

This section shows the cost and outputs of the method on a published regional model. The USGS Neversink/Rondout model (Fienen et al. 2022; Corson-Dosch et al. 2022) is a steady-state model of 1.68 million cells, with 12,316 SFR reaches. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1 (4 layers of 680 × 619 cells of 50 m, 1,683,680 cells; SFR 12,316 reaches) --> Its 358 realisations come from history matching with the PEST++ iterative ensemble smoother (White et al. 2020). We refer to the realisation with the base parameters as the calibrated model. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §6 and line 202 (rebuilt base realisation equals the published calibrated input); si/D.md (run 357 is the calibrated realisation); ensemble/summary.csv (358 realisations) --> The published study mapped the contributing areas of eight priority wells with MODPATH 7 (Pollock 2016). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1; figures/README.md Fig. 5; dropped for length: the wells pump 3,671 m³/d in all --> One particle started in the uppermost active cell of each column near the wells. The published probability of contribution is the share of the published Monte Carlo contributing areas that contain a cell. Our counterpart is F averaged over the realisations (the ensemble mean F). ## Why the areas differ

Our areas differ from the published ones. We measure the difference with an overlap score. It is the number of cells in both areas divided by the number in either. It is 1 for identical areas and 0 for areas with no cell in common. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 (Jaccard index) -->

The published area comes from one reasonable choice of particle settings. Recharge and stream flows enter through the top face of a cell. A particle stops in the first weak-sink cell it enters. A particle that starts in a dry cell is moved down to the first active cell below (draping). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §2, §3 reproduction table --> PRT on the same flow field reproduces the published area (overlap 0.998) only with all three settings. Among particle runs that conserve water, the settings alone move the overlap from 0.62 to 0.998 ({SI: delineations on one flow field}). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §3, Table 3 and reproduction table; mp7_reproduction.csv (0.998; 0.65 draped, 0.62 dropped); prt_comparison.csv; the two runs that do not conserve water (0.29, 0.39) are in the SI table -->

The full-mixing areas where F exceeds 0.5 and 0.1 agree less, with overlaps of 0.41 and 0.30 (Fig. 12a, b). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2, Table 1; agreement_with_mp7.csv --> We infer that the main reason is that full mixing splits each cell's water among all its outflows, while a particle follows one path. Full-mixing areas are therefore wider and more diffuse. On average, the published cells send 42 % of their water to the wells. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 ("Their mean F is 0.42"; F > 0.1 contains 711 of the 980 published cells; 417 of the 445 F > 0.5 cells are published cells, 43 % of the published area); figures/README.md Fig. 7 --> The F > 0.1 area holds 73 % of them, against 43 % for the F > 0.5 area. A like-for-like test uses PRT particles that lose weight at each sink as full mixing does. The F > 0.1 area holds 87 % of the 691 start cells where these particles deliver more than half the water to the wells (the P > 0.5 area; Fig. 12c). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §5, Table 3a; envelope_prt.csv (all, P>0.5: F>0.1 coverage 0.871, 691 PRT cells) -->

Weak sinks add to the difference. A buried stream cell holds a gaining SFR reach under another active cell. Its stream takes only part of the cell's outflow. At such cells and at weak-sink wells, a published particle stops and credits all its water to the sink. Full mixing passes the rest on ({SI: end states of the published particles}). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 "End states" (status 3: 26,828 stopped in stream cells with an active cell above; 466 in the five weak-sink priority wells); net_vs_gross_wells.csv (U___7 takes 44 % of its cell's outflow) --> Crediting weak-sink wells as a stopping particle does (net accounting) raises the F > 0.5 overlap only from 0.41 to 0.44 ({SI: net attribution and PRT-area maps}). <!-- evidence: si/D.md Table S3 (overlaps 0.41/0.44 and 0.30/0.28); docs/neversink_study.md §2, Tables 2a–2b; net_vs_gross_mp7.csv -->

With the stream network, the area extends upstream along the streams. The expected area of the ensemble mean F is then 1.5 times the published one. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §6, Table 4 and text after it (sum ratio 1.51 for the network, "because it includes the upstream area that feeds the losing reaches"); probability_comparison.csv (1.508); dropped for length: published particles that reach a stream cell with no active cell above leave through its top face (NS §2 End states, status 2) -->
