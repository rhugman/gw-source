# 5. Neversink Demonstration and Benchmark

The Neversink/Rondout model is a published real-world regional model in steady state, so only the steady method is tested. <!-- NS §1, §6; outline §5.5 --> Its published contributing areas come from one set of particle settings, so they are a benchmark, not a reference. We rerun particles on the same flow field to show how far these settings alone move the area. Particles that lose water at sinks as full mixing does then test mixing like for like (Section 5.3). <!-- NS §3, §5 ("The published mask is a convention") -->

## 5.1 Model and benchmark

The USGS model (Fienen et al. 2022; Corson-Dosch et al. 2022) has four layers of 680 × 619 cells of 50 m and 12,316 SFR reaches. <!-- NS §1 --> Rerun with MODFLOW 6 version 6.9, it reproduces the published heads within 8.6 mm. <!-- NS §1 and Checks; mf6_runs.csv --> The benchmark is the published MODPATH 7 (Pollock 2016) delineation for eight priority wells. They pump 3,671 m³/d in three clusters (U, SV_W and SV_S) in three regions (NE, W and S; Fig. 5). <!-- NS §1; figures/README.md Fig. 5 --> One particle starts in each uppermost active cell of the regions (the start cells; 122,021 particles). <!-- NS §1, §2 --> A particle stops in the first weak-sink cell it enters, whose sink is credited with all of its water. <!-- NS §1, §2 --> The published contributing cells are the 980 start cells whose particle ends in a priority well. <!-- NS §1, §2; mp7_status.csv -->

[[Figure 5]]

Comparisons are made on the start cells, where 96 % of the wells' water enters the model. <!-- NS §2 Coverage; mp7_coverage.csv --> A trace of nine destination groups took 2–3 s, against 44 s for the MODFLOW 6 run. <!-- nine groups: each well and all eight; NS Summary, §1, §4; checks_cells.csv (2.3 s), checks_sfr_network.csv (2.6 s); mf6_runs.csv --> For every group, the traced source water matched the wells' inflow to 1 × 10⁻¹² m³/d or less (the closure check, Section 2.5). <!-- NS Summary, §1, §4; checks_cells.csv, checks_sfr_network.csv -->

## 5.2 Full mixing against the published area

We measure agreement with the overlap: the cells both areas share, as a share of all cells in either area (the Jaccard index). An overlap of 1 means identical areas. An overlap of 0 means no cell in common. <!-- NS §2 -->

An area drawn from F, the share of a cell's water that ends at the wells, needs a threshold. The F > 0.5 area holds the cells where more than half the water ends at the wells, the cells most certainly in the area. The F > 0.1 area, where more than a tenth does, is a wider, cautious area (Section 4.3). With gross accounting (Section 2.3), these two areas overlap the published contributing cells at 0.41 and 0.30 (Fig. 6a–c; per well, {SI: per-well gross and net}). <!-- NS §2, Tables 1–2; agreement_with_mp7.csv; the area-matched overlap, 0.41 for the 980 start cells with the largest F, is in Fig. 7 and the SI --> The expected area, the sum of F over the start cells, is 910 cells against 980 published. <!-- NS §2, Table 1 -->

[[Figure 6]]

The F > 0.5 area lies almost wholly inside the published area: 94 % of its 445 cells are published contributing cells. It covers 43 % of the published area. <!-- NS §2, Table 1 --> The F > 0.1 area, 2,139 start cells or 2.2 times the published area, still misses 27 % of it. <!-- NS §2, Table 1, §5 --> Part of the difference lies at weak sinks. A published particle stops in the cell of U___7, whose well takes 44 % of the cell's outflow. Full mixing passes the other 56 % downstream ({SI: end states of the published particles}). <!-- NS §2; net_vs_gross_wells.csv --> Net accounting (Section 2.3), which credits U___7 with 2.26 times its pumping, raises the expected area to 978 cells ({SI: net attribution and PRT-area maps}). <!-- NS §2, Tables 2a–2b; net_vs_gross_wells.csv, net_vs_gross_mp7.csv --> Whether overlaps of 0.30–0.41 are low depends on the published particle settings.

## 5.3 Particle tracking on the same flow field

Why not compare with the published area alone? Then every difference would count as an error of full mixing. Yet the published area itself depends on particle settings: where particles start, where boundary flows enter a cell and what a particle does at a weak sink. On the same flow field, PRT (Section 3.2) gives overlaps from 0.29 to 0.998, depending on these settings alone (Fig. 7; {SI: delineations on one flow field}). <!-- NS §3, Table 3 and reproduction table; prt_comparison.csv, mp7_reproduction.csv --> The published area is therefore one particle setting among several, not ground truth.

PRT reproduces the published area only with three published settings: recharge and stream flows on the top face of a cell where that face is open (IFLOWFACE), particles stopped at every weak sink, and draping (MODPATH DrapeOption), which moves a particle that starts in a dry cell down to the first active cell below. The weak sinks include buried stream cells: SFR reach cells with an active cell above, where a gaining reach is usually a weak sink. With all three, 99.0 % of particles end in the same cell as published (overlap 0.998). <!-- the MODFLOW 6 default (boundary flows spread through the cell) gives 0.65 even with draping: Fig. 7 and Table S3; NS §2, §3 reproduction table; mp7_reproduction.csv (0.998, 0.65 draped, 0.62 dropped), mp7_status.csv -->

To test mixing itself, weight-losing particles (Section 3.2) were released from the source terms of all cells with F > 10⁻⁶, each weighted by its flow. These particles delivered 3,736 m³/d against a well inflow of 3,671 m³/d (+1.8 %). <!-- NS §3, Table 3; prt_comparison.csv --> Their particle share P, like F, is the share of a cell's water that reaches the wells, but along particle paths instead of by full mixing. The P > 0.5 area overlaps the published cells at 0.39–0.62, depending on release height (Fig. 7). <!-- NS §3, Table 3; agreement_with_mp7.csv; the 0.29 of the range above is particles that keep their weight at sinks other than the wells, now only in Fig. 7 and the SI: prt_comparison.csv --> <!-- dropped for length in round 4: stream leakage is 43 % of the wells' water under full mixing and 51 % along the particle paths (NS §3; prt_sources_by_well.csv; prt_diagnosis.csv) -->

[[Figure 7]]

Which F area should match the P > 0.5 area? Full mixing spreads each cell's water over all its outflows. The water reaching the wells is therefore shared over more cells, each with a lower F. Along a particle path, the water stays together until a sink takes it. On Neversink the P > 0.5 area has 691 start cells, against 445 for F > 0.5. <!-- NS §5, Table 3a; envelope_prt.csv --> Covering it takes the lower threshold F > 0.1. PRT was seeded only where F > 10⁻⁶, so the coverage is an upper bound. The particles lose weight at sinks as full mixing does, so only the spreading between sinks is tested. <!-- NS §5, Table 3a; envelope_prt_notes.csv --> Within these caveats, F > 0.1 covers 87 % of the P > 0.5 area. The F > 0.5 area covers 59 % of the P > 0.5 area. Of the F > 0.5 cells, 92 % lie inside it ({SI: net attribution and PRT-area maps}). <!-- NS §5, Table 3a; evidence: studies/neversink/output/envelope_prt.csv (all, P>0.5: F>0.1 coverage 0.871, 89 missed, 226.7 of 3,736 m³/d, the 6 % now only in Fig. S2; F>0.5 coverage 0.590, f_zone_inside_prt 0.917) --> The gap to the published area therefore comes from both mixing and the published particle settings.

## 5.4 Stream representation

How streams are represented also changes the area. With streams as cells, SFR leakage is a source term of the aquifer cell. It supplies 43 % of the wells' water (the stream share). The area stops at the streams (Fig. 6a–c). <!-- NS §4; sources_cells.csv --> With the stream network, the SFR reaches become extra cells linked by stream routing. Leakage is then traced up the stream to where its water entered. All the wells' water then traces to recharge. The area extends upstream along the streams (Fig. 6d–f). <!-- NS §4 ("All 3,671 m³/d then traces to recharge, none to the stream's external inflow or to CHD"); evidence: studies/neversink/output/sources_sfr_network.csv (priority: RCHA 3,671.0, CHD_0 0, SFR_0:EXT-INFLOW 0) --> The network's eight flow loops were solved exactly. <!-- NS §4; checks_sfr_network.csv (trace 2.6 s) -->

## 5.5 Ensemble at base pumping

The published study also mapped a probability of contribution, an uncertainty product for protection zones. It is the share of the 357 published Monte Carlo contributing areas, one per realisation, that contain a cell. <!-- NS §6, Table 4; ensemble/probability_comparison.csv --> We traced the 358 realisations of the published ensemble, drawn from the posterior of the PEST++ iterative ensemble smoother (White et al. 2020). <!-- NS §6; ensemble/summary.csv --> <!-- the reproduction of the 60 paired published areas (median overlap 0.996) moved to the Fig. 8 caption for length -->

Our counterpart is the mean F over the realisations. It is a share of water averaged over realisations, not a share of realisations. Mean F correlates with the published probability at r = 0.80. Its expected area is 90 % of the published one. The areas above 0.5 overlap at 0.48 (Fig. 8; {SI: ensemble statistics}). <!-- NS §6, Table 4; ensemble/probability_comparison.csv -->

[[Figure 8]]

Why do the two differ? In the S region the published area varies between realisations (coefficient of variation 32 %). PRT with the published settings follows this variation (r = 0.997, 60 realisations). The sum of F does not (r = 0.00). <!-- NS §6 --> There, the published area changes with the particle settings, not with the water reaching the wells. <!-- NS §6 ("the differences between realisations show up through the particle conventions, not in the full-mixing capture"); which setting drives it is not isolated by region: at base pumping, stops at buried stream cells remove captured weight equal to about 2 cells in the SV_S cluster (scenarios/mechanisms60/stream_stops.csv, mean of 61 realisations), so the text does not name buried streams as the cause here -->

## 5.6 Pumping scenarios

How does the area grow as pumping rises? This matters when a zone must be redrawn for planned rates. The published scenarios raise pumping at the priority wells only, by a rule we inferred ({SI: Monte Carlo offsets and scenario rule}). <!-- NS §7, §8; scenarios.py --> At every rate, the median full-mixing area is within 10 % of the published median ({SI: scenario medians}). <!-- NS §7, Table 5; scenarios/area_table.csv --> From base to maximum pumping, pumping grows 3.68 times. The full-mixing area grows 4.09 times, the published area 3.60 times (Fig. 9). <!-- NS §7, Table 5; scenarios/area_table.csv -->

[[Figure 9]]

The full-mixing area grows faster than pumping mainly because the stream share falls, from 42 % to 28 % ({SI: growth mechanism}). <!-- NS §7; scenarios/source_table.csv --> Most of the added pumping is recharge that discharged to streams at base pumping. The recharge reaching the wells, and with it the area, therefore grows faster than pumping. <!-- NS §7 ("Where the stream water changes"; recharge reaching the wells grows 4.57 times); mechanisms60/stream_by_state.csv -->

The published area grows more slowly. As pumping rises, the area reaches buried stream cells. Every published particle that enters one stops there. Full mixing passes on all but the stream's share of the cell's outflow, so its area keeps growing past these cells. <!-- NS §7 ("Why the published area grows more slowly"); stream_stops.csv --> PRT with the published settings reproduces the published growth (3.62 against 3.60, 61 realisations). Changing only this setting, stopping at buried stream cells, lowers the growth of weight-losing particles from 4.76 to 3.83 times ({SI: one convention at a time}; {SI: faster growth of weight-losing particles}). <!-- NS §7, Table 6 and bullets after it; mechanisms60/growth_decomposition.csv (r1 3.622, r2_thinned 4.76, r2_streams_thinned 3.83), stream_stops.csv -->
