## 4.10 Does it reproduce a published unit-concentration study?

Bedekar et al. (2024) used a synthetic MODFLOW-2005 and MT3D-USGS model to show how the unit-concentration method splits a well's water by source over time (Section 1). Forward transient tracking solves the same transport equation without dispersion, as their example does. Their example therefore tests transient tracking against a published result made with other codes. <!-- evidence: Bedekar et al. 2024 pp. 305–306 (MODFLOW-2005, MT3D-USGS; four separate MT3D-USGS simulations); tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb introduction -->

We rebuilt the model in MODFLOW 6 from the paper's description. It has three layers of 10 m, each with 101 × 101 cells of 10 m. Water enters as lateral inflow along the west edge and as recharge. A stream bounds the east edge. A well 150 m from the stream pumps 700 m³/d for 25 years. The paper leaves some values open, such as the time steps and the layer of the well screen. Our choices are listed in {SI: example and test models}. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §1 table (assumptions: K in m/d, convertible layers, river bottom at the cell bottom, well in layer 2, 25 years in 300 steps of 30.4 days) and budget (INFLOW 454.5, RCH 306.0, PUMP 700.0 m³/d); Bedekar et al. 2024 p. 305 -->

One forward run with four tags gives the share of the pumped water from each source (Fig. 11a). The fourth tag is the stored water, which is the initial water of Section 2.7. Stream water arrives within the first year. Lateral inflow enters 850 m from the well and first appears after about 7 years. At year 25 the well's water is 48.2 % lateral inflow, 29.2 % recharge and 22.5 % stream water. The paper gives 48 %, 29 % and 23 %. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §2 table (stream 0.080 at 1 yr, 0.225 from 10 yr; lateral inflow 0.090 at 10 yr, 0.482 at 25 yr) and §2 text (first appears after about 7 years); Bedekar et al. 2024 p. 306 ("approximately 29%, 23%, and 48%") -->

Backward tracking in time adds the time-limited contributing area (Fig. 11b–e). Within 1 year, the well pumps more than half the water of about 840 cells per layer, between the well and the stream. Within 25 years the area covers about 6,800 cells per layer. That is nearly the steady-state F > 0.5 area of the final flows (Fig. 11f). <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §7 table (cells with w > 0.5 in layer 2: 841 within 1 yr, 6,825 within 25 yr; steady trace F > 0.5: 6,817, 6,829, 6,831 cells in layers 1 to 3) --> We expect that a forward unit-concentration model would need one run per zone of the aquifer to give such a map. <!-- tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §7 introduction (one transport run per zone of the aquifer); untested, hence "we expect" -->

Four MODFLOW 6 GWT runs, one per source, give the same fractions to 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ in every cell and step. For this comparison the tracking used GWT's water volumes. The two codes solve the same equations, so this is a consistency test. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §3 (max |c − C| 4.4 × 10⁻¹¹ to 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰, below the GWT solver tolerance of 10⁻⁹; volume_mode "gwt") --> The independent check is Figure 2 of the paper, read off to about one percentage point. Over 15 read-off times, the largest difference is 1.3 percentage points, on the lateral-inflow front at year 10. With 7.6-day transport steps instead of 30-day steps, it falls to 0.6 points over the first 15 years. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §4 (largest |here − paper|: lateral inflow 1.3, storage 1.2 points at year 10) and §6 (4 sub-steps, 7.6-day steps: 0.6 points up to year 15) --> At year 25 the tracked shares are within 0.2 points of a steady-state backward trace of the final flows. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §5 table (steady trace 0.483, 0.292, 0.225 against tracked 0.482, 0.292, 0.225, stored water 0.002) --> The timing of each front depends on the transport step.
