## 4.10 How does a new well's source mix change in its first years?

When a new well starts pumping, its water changes over the years from mostly stored water to water from the sources that feed it. Bedekar et al. (2024) showed this with the unit-concentration method on a synthetic MODFLOW-2005 and MT3D-USGS model (Section 1). Forward transient tracking solves the same transport equation without dispersion, as their example does. Their example therefore also tests transient tracking against a published result made with other codes. <!-- evidence: Bedekar et al. 2024 pp. 305–306 (MODFLOW-2005, MT3D-USGS; four separate MT3D-USGS simulations); tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb introduction and §7, cell 18 (period 1 steady with no pumping; the well pumps from year 0) -->

We rebuilt the model in MODFLOW 6 from the paper's description. It has three layers of 10 m, each with 101 × 101 cells of 10 m. Water enters as lateral inflow along the west edge and as recharge. A stream bounds the east edge. A well 150 m from the stream pumps 700 m³/d for 25 years. The paper leaves some values open, such as the time steps and the layer of the well screen. Our choices are listed in {SI: example and test models}. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §1 table (assumptions: K in m/d, convertible layers, river bottom at the cell bottom, well in layer 2, 25 years in 300 steps of 30.4 days) and budget (INFLOW 454.5, RCH 306.0, PUMP 700.0 m³/d); Bedekar et al. 2024 p. 305 -->

One forward run with four tags gives the share of the pumped water from each source (Fig. 11a). The fourth tag is the stored water, which is the initial water of Section 2.7. Stream water arrives within the first year. Lateral inflow enters 850 m from the well and first appears after about 7 years. At year 25 the well's water is 48.2 % lateral inflow, 29.2 % recharge and 22.5 % stream water. The paper gives 48 %, 29 % and 23 %. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §2 table (stream 0.080 at 1 yr, 0.225 from 10 yr; lateral inflow 0.090 at 10 yr, 0.482 at 25 yr) and §2 text (first appears after about 7 years); Bedekar et al. 2024 p. 306 ("approximately 29%, 23%, and 48%") -->

Backward tracking in time adds the time-limited contributing area (Fig. 11b–e). The maps show layer 2, where the well is screened. Within 1 year, the well pumps more than half the water of 841 cells in that layer, between the well and the stream. Within 25 years the area covers 6,825 cells of the layer. That is nearly the 6,829 cells of the steady-state F > 0.5 area of the final flows (Fig. 11f). <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §7, cell 18 table (cells with w > 0.5 in layer 2: 841 within 1 yr, 6,825 within 25 yr; layers 1 and 3: 766 and 756 within 1 yr, review alt_r1 F5; steady trace F > 0.5: 6,817, 6,829, 6,831 cells in layers 1 to 3) --> We expect that a forward unit-concentration model would need one run per zone of the aquifer to give such a map. <!-- tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §7 introduction (one transport run per zone of the aquifer); untested, hence "we expect" -->

Four MODFLOW 6 GWT runs, one per source, give the same fractions to 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ in every cell and step, when the tracking uses GWT's water volumes. The two codes solve the same equations, so this is a consistency test. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §3 (max |c − C| 4.4 × 10⁻¹¹ to 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰, below the GWT solver tolerance of 10⁻⁹; volume_mode "gwt") --> The independent check is Figure 2 of the paper, read off to about one percentage point. Over 15 read-off times, the largest difference is 1.3 percentage points, on the lateral-inflow front at year 10. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §4 (largest |here − paper|: lateral inflow 1.3, storage 1.2 points at year 10). Moved to the SI for length (review alt_r1 P5): with 7.6-day transport steps the largest difference falls to 0.6 points up to year 15 (§6); at year 25 the tracked shares are within 0.2 points of a steady-state backward trace of the final flows (§5 table: steady 0.483, 0.292, 0.225 against tracked 0.482, 0.292, 0.225, stored water 0.002) -->

The timing of each front depends on the length of the transport step. With 30-day steps, the stream share after 6 months is 1.1 %. With transport sub-steps of 7.6 days it is 0.4 %. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §6 table (stream at 0.5 yr: 1.1 % with 30-day steps, 0.4 % with 7.6-day steps, 0.9 % in the paper) --> Where arrival times matter, the transport step should be checked by refining it (Section 4.8).
