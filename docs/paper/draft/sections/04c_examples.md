## 4.9 How sure can we be of the contributing area?

Every example so far used one set of parameters. Many sets can fit the measured heads about equally well. We refer to each set as a realisation. A set of realisations is an ensemble.

The ensemble here holds 50 realisations of the shared model (Section 4). Each has its own hydraulic conductivity (K) field. In each field, K is set at 100 pilot points, 200 m apart. Pilot points are values of K at fixed points. The model interpolates between them by kriging. The pilot-point values are drawn at random, with a spatial correlation. No measurements have shaped these fields. We refer to such an ensemble as a prior. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb Settings, cell 2 (N_REAL 50, PRIOR_SEED 3, SIGMA_LNK 0.8, CORRELATION_LENGTH 400 m, PP_SPACING 4) and §1, cells 3 and 4 (K = 5 exp(0.8 z) at 100 pilot points, simple kriging of ln K; recharge, river, west edge, well and grid unchanged; "These realisations are a prior: no data has shaped them") --> For each cell, the probability of contribution is the share of realisations in which F > 0.5. A protection zone can then be drawn at a stated risk. In the middle 90 % of realisations, the river supplies 0 to 33 % of the pumped water. The mean travel time lies between 36 and 52 years. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §8, cell 20 (5 to 95 %: river share 0.000 to 0.330; mean travel time 36 to 52 yr) --> The shared model gives 14 % and 46 years (Sections 4.1 and 4.6).

Full mixing spreads the edge of the area even in a single model (Section 4.1). In this prior, the unknown K blurs the edge more than full mixing does. The realisations disagree over 1.10 km², where the probability of contribution lies between 0.1 and 0.9. In the shared model, the cells with 0.1 < F < 0.9 cover 0.80 km². <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §8, cell 20 (1.10 km² for 0.1 < P(F > 0.5) < 0.9 over the 50 prior realisations; 0.80 km² for 0.1 < F < 0.9 in the homogeneous base case) and cell 21 -->

History matching adjusts a model's parameters until it fits measurements. PEST++ IES does this for every realisation of an ensemble (White 2018; White et al. 2020). Here it adjusts the 100 pilot-point values of each realisation of the prior. The history-matched ensemble is the posterior. A separate random field stands for the true aquifer. It is not built from pilot points. Its heads in 40 cells, with added noise, are the measurements. The set-up and the history-matching runs are described separately ({SI: ensemble and history-matching set-up}). <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §9 intro and §9.1, cells 21 and 22 (truth from hbd.build_realisation, not from pilot points; 40 head observations, noise sd 0.05 m) and §9.2, cell 24 (prior.csv = the prior of section 1, 100 adjustable parameters). The posterior band is narrower than the 0.80 km² band of full mixing in the shared model. The cells with a probability of at least 0.9 grow from 0.56 to 0.98 km². The middle 90 % of river shares narrows from 0–33 % to 5–13 %. Ensembles can also need a lot of memory. An exact percentile map needs every realisation's value in every cell. For 1,000 realisations of a model with 1 million cells, that is 4 GB for each output and each well. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §6, cell 16 (1,000 realisations, 1,000,000 cells, six series: 24 GB, so 4 GB per series) --> The code can instead sort each cell's values into a histogram as it goes. The memory then stays the same however many realisations there are.
