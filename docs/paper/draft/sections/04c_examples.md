## 4.9 How sure can we be of the contributing area?

Every example so far used one set of parameters. Many sets can fit the measured heads about equally well. A collection of such sets is an ensemble. Each set is a realisation.

The first ensemble is a prior. A prior uses no measurements. It holds 20 realisations of the shared model (Section 4). Each has its own random hydraulic conductivity (K) field. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb Settings, cell 2 (N_REAL 20, SIGMA_LNK 0.8) and §1, cell 3 (K = 5 exp(0.8 z), spatially correlated; recharge, river, west edge, well and grid unchanged) --> For each cell, the capture probability P(F > 0.5) is the share of realisations in which F > 0.5. F is the share of a cell's water that ends at the well (Section 4.1). A protection zone can then be drawn at a stated risk. In the middle 90 % of realisations, the river supplies 0.7 to 27 % of the pumped water. The mean travel time lies between 36 and 54 years. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §8, cell 20 (5 to 95 %: river share 0.007 to 0.273; mean travel time 36 to 54 yr) --> The shared model gives 14 % and 46 years (Sections 4.1 and 4.6).

Full mixing spreads the edge of the area even in a single model (Section 4.1). At the edge, this effect is about as large as that of the unknown K. The realisations disagree over 0.77 km², where 0.1 < P(F > 0.5) < 0.9. In the shared model, the cells with 0.1 < F < 0.9 cover 0.80 km². <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §8, cell 20 (0.77 km² for 0.1 < P(F > 0.5) < 0.9; 0.80 km² for 0.1 < F < 0.9 in the homogeneous base case) -->

History matching adjusts a model's parameters until it fits measurements. PEST++ IES does this for every realisation of an ensemble (White 2018). The adjusted ensemble is the posterior. In this synthetic test, another random field stands for the true aquifer. Its heads in 40 cells, plus noise of 0.05 m standard deviation, are the measurements. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §9.1, cell 22 (40 head observations, noise sd 0.05 m) --> K comes from 100 pilot points. Pilot points are values of K at fixed points, between which the model interpolates by kriging. Three iterations on a prior of 50 realisations took 320 model runs, or 66 s on 8 workers. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §9.2, cell 24 (100 pilot points, 50 prior realisations) and §9.4, cell 28 (3 iterations, 320 model runs, 8 workers, 66 s) --> The mean weighted sum of squared head residuals fell from 3,492 to 55. A fit within the noise would give about 40. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §9.4, cell 28 (mean phi 3492 for the prior, 55 at iteration 3) and cell 29 (40 observations, so about 40 from noise) -->

History matching narrows the capture probability (Fig. 10). The area where the realisations disagree shrinks from 1.098 to 0.350 km². The cells with P(F > 0.5) ≥ 0.9 grow from 0.558 to 0.978 km². The middle 90 % of river shares narrows from 0–33 % to 5–13 %. The true share is 9.4 %. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §9.5, cell 30 (prior, posterior: band 0.1 < P < 0.9 1.098, 0.350 km²; area P ≥ 0.9 0.558, 0.978 km²; river share 5 % and 95 % 0.000–0.330, 0.047–0.134; truth river share 0.094) -->

A narrower posterior is not necessarily a correct one. The maps can be checked against the true aquifer's F > 0.5 area. The posterior gives P(F > 0.5) ≥ 0.5 to 93 % of that area, against 79 % for the prior. In both ensembles, 86 to 87 % of the cells with P(F > 0.5) ≥ 0.9 lie inside the true area. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §9.5, cell 30 (true zone with P ≥ 0.5: 79.268 and 93.415 %; area P ≥ 0.9 inside the true zone: 86.099 and 87.468 %) and cell 31 --> That share did not improve. One synthetic case cannot show how reliable posteriors are in general.

The limit this example shows is memory. Percentile maps of F need every realisation's values in memory. For 1,000 realisations of 1 million cells, that is 4 GB for each output of each destination. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §6, cell 16 (1,000 realisations, 1,000,000 cells, six series: 24 GB, so 4 GB per series) --> Histograms cap the memory at some loss of accuracy. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §6, cells 14 and 15 (histogram quantiles within one bin width of the exact ones, for values inside the histogram range); §10, cell 33 (check of the code: mean F > 0.5 area over the realisations equals the sum of P(F > 0.5) times cell area, 1.1321, 1.1307 and 1.1506 km²), left out of the text for length -->
