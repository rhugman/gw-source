### Common settings of the example models

Every example model is a MODFLOW 6 model built with FloPy. Each saves what the code needs: the binary grid file, the budget of every time step with SAVE_FLOWS in every package, the heads, the saturation and the specific discharge. Packages that are traced as extra cells (SFR, MAW and MVR) also write their own budget files. The flow solver (IMS) uses the simple complexity option, BiCGSTAB acceleration, a head change criterion of 10⁻⁹ m for the outer iterations and 10⁻¹⁰ m for the inner iterations, and a residual criterion of 10⁻⁸ m³/d. ### Particle tracking on the example models (Sections 4.1 and 4.6)

The particle comparisons use MODFLOW 6 PRT on the flows of the shared model, with a porosity of 0.3. Particles are released from every source term of every cell with $F > 10^{-6}$. Each source term gets 16 release points, spread over the cell in plan. Each point carries an equal share of the term's flow as its weight. In a one-layer model there is no vertical flow, so the points are released at one height. The particles lose weight at every sink they pass, in proportion to the flow that the sink takes from the cell's outflow. On the shared model this gives 18,208 particles from 1,133 cells. The particle share P of a cell is the weight that reaches the well divided by the weight released in the cell. <!-- src/gwsource/prt.py PrtConfig (fraction_threshold 1e-6, subdivisions 2: 4 k^2 = 16 points per DIS cell, z_levels: one level in a one-layer grid, thin_weak_sinks True); tutorials/01_quickstart/01_quickstart.ipynb §8 and tutorials/06_travel_time/06_travel_time.ipynb §5 (PrtConfig(porosity=0.3), "18208 particles from 1133 cells") -->

### The grids (Section 4.4)

**Voronoi grid (DISV).** The Python package vorflow (Hugman and Sanchez 2026), version 0.2.0, builds the Voronoi grid. It first meshes the 2 km × 2 km square with triangles. It then turns the triangle mesh into Voronoi cells. The mesh size is 50 m away from the well and the river, as in the DIS grid. It is 10 m at the well and 12.5 m along the river. Between these sizes the mesh grows by about 20 % from one cell to the next, the default of vorflow. The well point and the river line x = 1,525 m are part of the mesh. So the well point is the centre of the well's cell. The river cells are centred on the river line. The grid has 3,241 cells in one layer, with the top, bottom, hydraulic conductivity and recharge of the shared model. The cell centres are the points the Voronoi cells are built around. So each connection between two cells is perpendicular to the face they share. The stresses are placed by geometry. The constant heads fill every cell with a vertex on the west edge. These cells are centred on the edge itself. The river fills every cell that the line x = 1,525 m crosses. This line is the centre line of the river column of the DIS model. The river conductance per cell is scaled so that the total conductance equals that of the DIS model. The well is in the cell centred on x = 1,425 m and y = 975 m, the centre of the DIS well cell. <!-- tutorials/herebedragons.py (vorflow_cells: ConceptualMesh with the domain box, the river LineString at x = (riv_col + 0.5) delr with resolution 12.5 m and the well Point with resolution 10 m, MeshGenerator(background_lc=50), VoronoiTessellator with default settings, default growth_factor 1.2; disv_gridprops: cell centre = Voronoi generator; voronoi_stress_cells: west-edge cells by vertex x < 1e-6, river cells crossed by the line over a length > 0, well by point; build_voronoi: cond = riv_cond × nrow / n_riv_cells); tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb (DISV (1, 3241); 41 constant-head cells centred at x = 0.0 m; 161 river terms, 80 north and 81 south) -->

**Unstructured grids (DISU).** Two DISU models hold the 1,600 cells of the shared model and their connections, written as an unstructured grid. One includes the cell vertices and one does not. **XT3D model.** This model is the shared model with the XT3D option of the flow package and an anisotropic, heterogeneous hydraulic conductivity. ### The seasonal model for snapshots (Section 4.5)

This model is the shared model with storage. The layer is convertible, with a specific yield of 0.2 and a specific storage of 10⁻⁵ m⁻¹. ### The rebuilt model of Bedekar et al. (2024) (Section 4.10)

Bedekar et al. (2024, pp. 305–306 and Fig. 1) describe their model in MODFLOW-2005. We rebuilt it in MODFLOW 6 from that description. The grid has 101 × 101 cells of 10 m and three layers of 10 m, with the top at 30 m. The horizontal hydraulic conductivity is 10 and the vertical conductivity 1. Lateral inflow of 1.5 m³/d enters every cell of the west column in all three layers, as a well package, 454.5 m³/d in all. Recharge of 3 × 10⁻⁴ m/d falls on every cell of layer 1, 306.0 m³/d in all. A stream (RIV) fills the east column of all three layers, with a stage of 30 m and a conductance of 1,000 m²/d per cell. The pumping well takes 700 m³/d. The first stress period is a steady state of 1 day without pumping. The paper leaves some values open. We made the following choices.

- **Units.** The paper gives the hydraulic conductivity in feet per day and every other input in metric units. We read the conductivity as 10 and 1 m/d. A rerun with the conductivity in feet per day lowered the head in the well cell from 26.8 to 19.1 m. It moved the long-run shares of the sources by at most 0.1 percentage points, because the inflow, recharge and pumping are fixed rates. <!-- tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §1 table (assumption) and §5 cell 14 output (26.76 and 19.07 m; steady shares 0.483/0.292/0.225 against 0.482/0.291/0.226) -->
- **Layer type.** All three layers are convertible. Only the cells near the well fall below the top of their layer.
- **Stream bottom.** The river bottom is at the bottom of each cell, so the exchange with the stream stays linear in head.
- **Well screen.** The well is in layer 2, where Fig. 1 of the paper draws its screen. It is at row 51, column 86, on the centre row and 150 m west of the stream cell centres.
- **Time steps.** The paper gives neither the length of the pumping period nor its time steps. The pumping period is 25 years, the time axis of the paper's Fig. 2, in 300 equal steps of 30.4 days. Section 4.10 also reruns the first 15 years with four transport sub-steps, 7.6-day transport steps. With these shorter steps, the largest difference from the read-off curves of the paper's Fig. 2 over the first 15 years falls from 1.3 to 0.6 percentage points.
- **Stored water.** The paper fixes the concentration of the stored water at 1 during the first period. We tag all the water in the model at the start of the first period as initial water. In that 1-day period, about 760 m³ of water enters from the sources, against about 3.06 million m³ of water in the aquifer.

## {SI: ensemble and history-matching set-up}

Section 4.9 traces the well in every realisation of an ensemble. It then history-matches the ensemble to head measurements with the PEST++ iterative ensemble smoother (PEST++ IES) and traces the well again. This text gives the set-up, so that the synthetic test can be repeated. <!-- tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §1 and §9 -->

**Pilot points and kriging.** The hydraulic conductivity of each realisation is set at 100 pilot points. They form a regular grid of 10 × 10 points, 200 m apart, in rows and columns 3, 7, …, 39. Between the pilot points, $\ln K$ is interpolated by simple kriging around $\ln 5$. The correlation between two points a distance $r$ apart is $\exp(-(r/400\ \mathrm{m})^2)$, with a nugget of 10⁻⁴ that keeps the kriging equations well conditioned. The kriging weights are computed once and saved. <!-- tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb settings, cell 2 (CORRELATION_LENGTH 400.0, PP_SPACING 4, NUGGET 1e-4) and §1, cells 3 and 4 (simple kriging of ln K around ln 5); tutorials/09_ensembles/helpers.py (pilot_point_cells: first = spacing // 2, every 4 cells; gaussian_correlation; pilot_point_covariance; kriging_weights) -->

**The prior.** The prior ensemble holds 50 realisations. Each draws the 100 pilot-point values as $K = 5 \exp(0.8\, z)$, where $z$ comes from a multivariate normal distribution with the correlation above (seed 3). Over the 50 prior fields, $\ln K$ has a mean of 1.60 and a standard deviation of 0.79, against targets of $\ln 5 = 1.61$ and 0.8. <!-- tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb settings, cell 2 (N_REAL 50, PRIOR_SEED 3) and cell 4 output ("100 pilot points; ln K of the prior fields: mean 1.60 (ln 5 = 1.61), standard deviation 0.79 (target 0.8)"); helpers.py draw_prior -->

**The synthetic truth and its measurements.** The true aquifer is a random field with the same statistics as the prior, made in another way. It is not built from pilot points. Its conductivity is $K = 5 \exp(0.8\, z)$. Here $z$ is white noise (seed 2024) smoothed by a Gaussian filter with a width of four cells (200 m), then shifted and scaled to a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1. A Gaussian filter of this width gives the correlation $\exp(-(r/400\ \mathrm{m})^2)$ of the prior. A kriged field is smoother between the pilot points than the truth. So no realisation can reproduce the truth exactly. The truth's F > 0.5 area is 1.025 km². The river supplies 9.4 % of its pumped water. Its steady-state heads are measured in 40 cells, drawn at random (seed 11) from all cells except the constant heads, the river and the well. Gaussian noise with a standard deviation of 0.05 m (seed 12) is added to each head. The measured heads range from 6.18 to 10.05 m. <!-- tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb settings, cell 2 (TRUTH_SEED 2024, N_OBS 40, OBS_SEED 11, NOISE_SD 0.05, NOISE_SEED 12) and §9.1, cells 21 and 22 (hbd.build_realisation with smoothing width CORRELATION_LENGTH / (2 delr) = 4 cells; correlation exp(-(r/2s)^2); "40 head observations from 6.18 to 10.05 m"; "truth: area F > 0.5 = 1.025 km2, river share = 0.094"); tutorials/herebedragons.py random_k_field (gaussian_filter of standard normal noise, mode reflect, standardised, K = mean K × exp(sigma_lnk z)); tutorials/09_ensembles/helpers.py observation_cells (CHD column, river column and well cells excluded) -->

**The PEST++ run.** Each model run of PEST++ writes the kriged conductivity from the pilot-point values, runs MODFLOW 6 and reads the heads at the 40 measurement cells. The 100 parameters are log-transformed. Their bounds are four prior standard deviations either side of the mean, from 0.20 to 123 m/d. Each measurement has the weight 1/0.05 m. PEST++ IES also adds noise of the same size to the measurements of each realisation. The run uses no extra realisation at the prior mean. It stops after three iterations (NOPTMAX = 3). On eight local workers, the run took 320 model runs and 61 s. <!-- tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §9.2 (forward_run.py: write_k_array, run_mf6, write_head_observations; log-transformed parameters, bounds mean_k exp(±4 × 0.8); weights 1/sigma; IES adds noise) and cell 24 (prior.csv = the prior of §1; ies_include_base False; noptmax 3, npar_adj 100, nnz_obs 40) and cell 26 output ("3 iterations, 320 model runs, 8 workers, 61 s") -->
