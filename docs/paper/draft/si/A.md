### Notes on the cells

- **What is solved, and how; this formulation.** One set of equations for the backward fraction F and one for the forward fraction G. Without flow loops the equations are solved cell by cell in flow order; with flow loops, loop by loop in flow order. Net accounting solves one destination group at a time. In transient tracking, each time step (or sub-step, when the water volumes change within a flow step) is one set of linear equations, implicit in time, solved for all tagged groups at once.
- **What is solved, and how; GWT unit concentration.** Langevin et al. (2022): GWT "does not have an option to calculate steady-state transport solutions"; steady state is reached by running transient steps until the concentrations stabilise. The GWT Model needs the storage (MST) package: without it, MODFLOW 6 (6.9.0.dev0) stops at input with "Mass storage and transfer (MST6) package not specified". With MST porosity set to 0, a use not described in the parts of Langevin et al. (2022) read, one transport step on the flows of test model 1 gave the steady unit concentration equal to G to 9.5 × 10⁻¹³ (recharge tagged) and 9.7 × 10⁻¹³ (river tagged), with no warning in the list file (our test). <!-- table1_methods.md, Sources, GWT unit concentration, 'Solved, how'; tests/models/gwt.py build_gwt_fmi -->
- **What is solved, and how; hydraulic mixing cells.** The fractions at each step are computed from the previous step's fractions and the volumes moved over the step; no system of equations is solved. The update is stable only if the volume entering or leaving a cell over a step does not exceed the cell's storage; small cells and long steps cause oscillations. Later versions: Partington et al. (2012, §2.2) apply the method of Partington et al. (2011) unchanged, built into a version of HydroGeoSphere. Partington et al. (2013, §3.3) add overland-flow fractions, sub-time-steps and cell resets. The HMC ratio, the outflow over a step divided by the cell's storage at the start of the step (Eq. 2), must stay below 1; where it exceeds 1 in any cell, each flow step is split into s = [max. HMC ratio] + 1 sub-steps, and the fluxes and storage changes are split among them (Eqs 3–9). A cell that meets any of five criteria, such as an HMC ratio above 10⁴ or |1 − Σf| > 0.5, is reset: its fractions are set to 0 and its water is assigned to a tracked "reset" fraction (§3.3.3). The 2013 mixing cells are still surface cells only, stream and overland cells (§3.3.1); tracking in the subsurface is recommended as future work (§6, para. 73). <!-- table1_methods.md, footnote 3 and Sources, Hydraulic mixing cells; Partington et al. 2013 Eqs 2–9, §3.3.3 --> Fractions in aquifer cells came later: Schilling et al. (2017) [check: not read], as an Aquanty research summary describes it, and Nogueira et al. (2022, Sect. 2.2, Eq. 1), in every model cell, surface and subsurface, of a riparian zone. All versions read step forward in time only.
- **Mixing in a cell; GWT unit concentration.** GWT also offers central-in-space and TVD advection, dispersion and reactions (Langevin et al. 2022). With upstream weighting and no dispersion its steady limit equals G (§4.1; Fig. 2). The abstract of Bedekar et al. (2024) and the authors' slides (Bedekar et al. 2023) do not say which advection scheme or whether dispersion was used.
- **Mixing in a cell; hydraulic mixing cells.** After the modified mixing cell of Campana and Simpson (1984): water leaving a cell over a step carries the previous step's mix, and water entering stays until the next step.
- **Accounting at weak sinks; the reference method.** Foley and Black (2013) define all water starting in or passing through a destination cell as reaching the destination, so a weak-sink well is credited with all the water passing through its cell.
- **Flow loops; this formulation.** A closed recirculation loop has no outflow to any sink or to a cell outside the loop; it is given F = 0 and reported. With net accounting, the smallest-flow connection in each loop can instead be deleted, as in the reference method (`remove_cycles`).
- **Outputs; the reference method.** Volume through, volume from (by budget term) and fraction from; the forward fraction as C/C0; statistics over time steps.
- **Outputs; this formulation.** Also boundary-control maps (the fractions to all sinks, which add up to 1 in every cell), mean travel time, statistics over steps and, in transient tracking, the fractions leaving through other sinks and still stored.
- **Outputs; hydraulic mixing cells.** K flow components: groundwater, unsaturated flow, overland flow, preferential flow and direct rainfall. Their fractions are updated together and sum to 1 in each cell. Initial fractions are assumed, and a dummy fraction holds the initial water until it is flushed out.
- **Backward in time; GWT unit concentration.** Langevin et al. (2022) document no backward-in-time option. A backward steady run would need a flow field with reversed flows and swapped sources and sinks: possible, but awkward. The abstract of Bedekar et al. (2024) lists well capture analysis among the uses without saying how it is done. The authors' conference slides on the method (Bedekar et al. 2023) show only forward runs: four transient simulations, one per tagged source (lateral inflow, recharge, storage, stream), with the concentration at a pumping well read as each source's share of the pumped water over 25 years. Nothing in them runs backward or on reversed flows. The slides name no code and do not discuss weak sinks.
- **Built-in water-budget checks; this formulation.** Also a report of closed recirculation loops. In transient tracking: the forward–backward check in time and the water and tagged-water balances.
- **Built-in water-budget checks; GWT unit concentration.** With unit concentration the solute mass is the tagged water volume, so the GWT mass budget, by term and step, is a tagged-water balance. The GWT budget file holds the solute mass flows of each cell by term (intercell, storage, source and sink mixing, advanced packages) (MODFLOW 6 input/output guide [check: author, year]), and the list file reports the mass budget of the whole model with its percent discrepancy at each saved step (seen in our runs).
- **Built-in water-budget checks; hydraulic mixing cells.** Relative error e = 1 − Σf, from the fractions summing to 1. The global volume error compares the groundwater volume in the stream cells at the end with the exfiltration summed over the run, from the model's water balance; it holds only on reaches that gain all along. It compares a traced volume with a water-budget total, close to our closure check.
- **Grids and packages; this formulation.** DIS, DISV and DISU grids; GWF-GWF exchanges; SFR reaches, lakes, MAW wells and UZF cells added as extra cells and linked by MVR.
- **Grids and packages; hydraulic mixing cells.** Stream cells of the HydroGeoSphere surface domain. Partington et al. (2011) say the method can be implemented in any code that reports surface–subsurface exchange in a spatially distributed way.
- **MODALL (no column).** MODALL (Potter et al. 2008) computes, from MODFLOW cell-by-cell flows, the share of flow in each cell that came from a source or goes to a sink (abstract), for steady-state models, assuming full mixing and without the reference method's flow-order sort (Chowdhury 2016, §2.3.3 and p. 103; Foley and Black 2013). How it solves, how it treats weak sinks and what it does with flow loops could not be established.

### The reference method

- **Solved, how:** Foley and Black (2013) Method and Evaluation: cells sorted in flow order, then one arithmetic pass; earlier versions used recursive scans. Each time step processed on its own Foley and Black (2013) Introduction.
- **Mixing:** Foley and Black (2013) Description of Calculation (divergent cells split their water in proportion to outflow); stated as full mixing in Chowdhury (2016) §2.4.3.
- **Accounting:** Foley and Black (2013) Description of Calculation: all water starting in or passing through a destination cell reaches the destination (cell A, 100 %); volume from uses each cell's excess of outflow over inflow. The outflows of destination cells are detached so that nothing downstream is counted Foley and Black (2013) Method.
- **Flow loops:** Foley and Black (2013) Method (the lowest-volume connection is removed; which one to remove is called an open research question) and Evaluation; Black and Foley (2013) Introduction to Graphs ("lowest flow volume cell connection(s)"). Dated 2013 because Daley et al. (2022) lists unspecified improvements to the flow routing in the 2020 version.
- **Outputs:** Foley and Black (2013) Introduction and Figs 1–4, 6–8 (volume and fraction through, volume and fraction from; volume from by recharge, storage and boundary term if other budget records are read); Black and Foley (2013) Introduction (min, max, mean, ASN, probability within range; travel time from forward particles based on MODPATH 6); Black et al. (2021) slide 10 and Daley et al. (2022) (forward mode as "an Eulerian transport tool", fraction = C/C0, volume to the receptor); Black et al. (2021) slide 5 (fraction from summing to 100 %).
- **Transient:** Foley and Black (2013) Introduction and Black and Foley (2013) Introduction and Problem Description: each time step a "time-instant steady state" (Rushton 2003); particles for travel time tracked through each step's steady flows Black and Foley (2013) Introduction.
- **Backward in time:** none in Foley and Black (2013), Black and Foley (2013), Black et al. (2021) or Daley et al. (2022); it follows from the snapshot treatment.
- **Per solve:** Foley and Black (2013) Evaluation and Black and Foley (2013) river-catchment and boundary-control examples: up to about 150,000 destination cells processed as one group in one calculation; the four boundary-control maps (Figs 18–21) were "processed individually" and sum to 1 in every cell.
- **Checks:** Foley and Black (2013) Method (flow loops reported to the user as a sign of a model flaw); Daley et al. (2022) ("error checking" among minor changes, without detail). No closure or other budget check is described.
- **Grids and packages:** Foley and Black (2013) Abstract and Introduction (MODFLOW-96 and MODFLOW-2005 CBC files; method said to apply to MODFLOW-USG and finite elements); Black and Foley (2013) (MODFLOW-96); Black et al. (2021) slides 6 and 11 and Daley et al. (2022) (MODFLOW 6 and MODFLOW-USG; triangular, quadtree and Voronoi meshes). MODFLOW 6 exchanges, MVR and package networks are not mentioned in any source. <!-- references/SUMMARY.md §2.9, §8 -->
- **Open implementation:** Foley and Black (2013) and Black and Foley (2013) end matter (available on request; VB.NET source for academic use on request); the vendor's web pages (as listed on 2026-10-01) (machine-locked, dongle and shared licences sold).

### GWT unit concentration

- **Solved, how:** Langevin et al. (2022) abstract (implicit, iterative methods; the quoted words on steady state) and p. 2–1 (the cell balances form a system of linear equations); p. 1–7, items 4 and 10 (central, upstream or TVD advection; "no option to automatically run the GWT Model to steady state using a single time step"; steady state found by running transient until concentrations stabilise). Note on GWT steady state: our runs of a GWT model on the saved flows of test model 1 (upstream advection, no dispersion, flow-imbalance correction on; recharge or river tagged; GWT outer/inner DVCLOSE 1 × 10⁻¹²/1 × 10⁻¹³, RCLOSE 1 × 10⁻⁹) with MODFLOW 6 6.9.0.dev0. <!-- tests/models/gwt.py build_gwt_fmi; run in a scratch folder outside the repository --> With MST left out the run stops at input. With one transport step of 1 day and MST porosity 0, max |C − G| over the 594 active cells is 9.5 × 10⁻¹³ with recharge tagged and 9.7 × 10⁻¹³ with the river tagged; with porosity 10⁻¹², 3.8 × 10⁻⁸ and 2.4 × 10⁻⁹.
- **Mixing:** Langevin et al. (2022) pp. 4–1 to 4–2 (upstream weighting); dispersion is an option (chapter 4). The match to G is this paper's own check (§4.1 and Fig. 2: ≤ 2.8 × 10⁻¹¹ at equilibrium on models 1 and 2).
- **Accounting:** Langevin et al. (2022) p. 5–3, eq. 5–7: by default water leaving a cell through a stress package leaves at the cell's concentration. Flow-imbalance correction, p. 2–6, eq. 2–14.
- **Flow loops:** not discussed in Langevin et al. (2022); the cell is our inference.
- **Outputs:** Bedekar et al. (2024) abstract (concentration one for the source water assessed, zero for all other water; the concentration gives each source's contribution to sinks; uses: source allocation, well capture analysis, mixing and dilution); 2023 slides 4–11 (C = 1 at the source, concentration at the pumping well read as the fraction of its water from that source; one run per source, storage among them). Agreement with forward tracking: §4.2 and Fig. 2.
- **Transient:** Langevin et al. (2022) p. 3–1, eqs 3–2 and 3–3 (storage of dissolved mass in the cell's water volume, with porosity and saturation, implicit in time).
- **Backward in time:** no reverse or adjoint option in the parts of Langevin et al. (2022) read; Bedekar et al. (2024) abstract silent on how well capture analysis is done; the 2023 slides show only forward transient runs, one per source (slides 9–11) [check: abstract only]; a backward steady run would need reversed flows. <!-- references/SUMMARY.md §6.3 -->
- **Per solve:** Langevin et al. (2022) p. 1–6, item 1 (one species per GWT model; several GWT models per simulation).
- **Checks:** the MODFLOW 6 input/output guide [check: author, year] "Budget File Contents for the GWT Model" (solute mass flows per cell, in mass per time, by term: FLOW-JA-FACE, STORAGE-AQUEOUS, SOURCE-SINK MIX, CNC, SRC, LKT, SFT, MWT, UZT and others); the list-file "MASS BUDGET FOR ENTIRE MODEL" with "PERCENT DISCREPANCY", seen in the runs of the note on GWT steady state. With unit concentration the solute mass is tagged water volume, so the budget is a tagged-water balance. No check of traced volumes between a source and a destination is described in the parts of Langevin et al. (2022) or the MODFLOW 6 input/output guide [check: author, year] read.
- **Grids and packages:** Langevin et al. (2022) pp. 1–6 to 1–7, items 3 and 11 (DIS, DISV, DISU; SFT, LKT, MWT, UZT and MVT, advection only between package features and with the aquifer).
- **Open implementation:** MODFLOW 6 is distributed by the USGS (Langevin et al. (2022) front matter, MODFLOW web page).

### Hydraulic mixing cells

All cells rest on the Partington et al. (2011) manuscript, read in full. The column describes Partington et al. (2011) itself, in which the mixing cells are stream cells of the surface domain only (§3.1, "a stream cell i"; §3.2–3.3, pairs of adjacent HydroGeoSphere grid points across the stream). Later versions, from the full texts of Partington et al. (2012, 2013) and from Nogueira et al. (2022), are in the note on hydraulic mixing cells (What is solved, and how).

- **Solved, how:** §3.1, Eqs 8–9: the fraction of component k in cell i at step N from the cell's storage at N − 1 and N, the outflow volume over the step at the cell's own fraction at N − 1 and the inflow volumes at the neighbours' fractions at N − 1; no system of equations. Stability (§3.1, after Eq. 9; §4): the volume entering or leaving a cell over a step must not exceed its storage; small cells and long steps cause oscillations, so suitable time steps are needed. Volumes over each step come from the flow model's fluxes at the end of the step (Eq. 11); no sub-steps.
- **Mixing:** §3 and §4 (based on the modified mixing cell of Campana and Simpson 1984; "the modified mixing rule has to be used" unless mixing in the river is simulated); §3.2 (outflow over a step at the previous step's fractions; water entering stays until the next step); §3.1, Eqs 9–10 (Eq. 9 tends to the perfectly mixed cell, Eq. 10, as the step goes to 0).
- **Accounting:** §3.2 and Eq. 9: every outflow of the cell, downstream or to the subsurface, uses the cell's previous-step fractions. That this is gross accounting in effect is our reading; Partington et al. (2011) does not discuss weak sinks.
- **Flow loops:** not discussed. §3 and §4: constant stream width and fixed stream flow direction assumed in the coding.
- **Outputs:** §3.1, Eqs 5–7 (K components: groundwater, unsaturated flow, overland flow, preferential flow and direct rainfall; fractions sum to 1); §3.2 (a "dummy (or undefined) fraction" for the initial water until the stream water is turned over). Applied as the groundwater component of streamflow at each stream cell (§3.3.2, Figs 11–12).
- **Transient:** §3.1, Eqs 4–6 (cell volumes and component volumes carried from step N − 1 to N); §3.2 (initial fractions are subjective; the dummy fraction falls to zero once the stream water is turned over).
- **Backward in time:** none; the method steps forward from initial fractions (§3.1–3.2).
- **Per solve:** Eqs 5–8 (all K components updated together).
- **Checks:** §3.3.1, Eq. 13 (relative error e from the sum of fractions; maximum 1.5 × 10⁻³ % in test case 1, 8.7 × 10⁻³ % in test case 2) and Eq. 14 (global volume error: the summed groundwater volume in the stream cells at the end against the summed exfiltration over the run from the model's water balance; "can only be used along completely gaining sections" with no losses; Table 1 of Partington et al. (2011)). GVE compares a traced volume with a water-budget total, as our closure check does.
- **Grids and packages:** §3.2 (HydroGeoSphere output: surface water depth, contributing area, surface–subsurface exchange flux, and upstream and downstream fluxes); §1 and §3 (can be implemented in any code that reports surface–subsurface exchange in a spatially distributed way); §3.1 (any FD, FV or FE scheme if the cell water balance is mass conservative).
- **Open implementation:** §3.2 ("coded in Visual Basic for Excel and is used as a post-processing tool on HGS outputs"); no statement on availability.

### MODALL (no column)

Potter et al. (2008) abstract (Europe PMC): MODALL uses the MODFLOW cell-by-cell flow terms to evaluate the flow balance between cells and gives the share of flow in each cell from a source or to a sink; compared with an analytical solution, MODPATH and MT3DMS. Chowdhury (2016) §2.3.3 (pp. 23–24) and §2.4: steady-state MODFLOW only; outputs equal the reference method's fraction through. Chowdhury (2016) p. 103: full mixing, numerical dispersion on coarse grids McLane and Cecan (2009) [check: not read], a simpler calculation than the reference method, without its flow-order sort. Foley and Black (2013) Introduction: MODALL does not use the reference method's flow-order sort and was used to validate its fraction through. Black and Foley (2013) Problem Description: on a model with flow loops MODALL did not delineate the capture areas (personal communication cited there). Tosco et al. (2010, p. 46) list MODALL among tools for choosing wells and pumping rates, without detail. None of these says whether MODALL solves a linear system, iterates or sweeps, or how it treats weak sinks.
