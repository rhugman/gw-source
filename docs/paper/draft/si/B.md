## {SI: net accounting, fixed cells}

Net accounting works from the cell-to-cell flows only. Each cell's source and sink terms are replaced by one net term, equal to its cell-to-cell outflow minus its cell-to-cell inflow. <!-- moved from §2.3 in round 5; PLAN §2.4 --> The destination cells are fixed at F = 1 (forward: the source cells at G = 1). Which cells are fixed depends on how the group was selected. A pure cell or zone selection fixes all its cells, as FlowSource defines groups (Foley and Black 2013). A selection with any term criterion (package, boundary name, term), also when combined with cells or a zone, fixes only the cells where a selected term flows in the traced direction: a sink in a backward trace, a source in a forward trace. A cell with selected terms of both signs is fixed in both directions. A group with no such term fixes no cell, so every fraction is 0. A well listed at zero rate, a drain above the water table, or a losing river cell in a backward trace is therefore not fixed. On a stream network, a package selection fixes only the reaches whose records flow in the traced direction. To fix a whole stream, select its reaches as cells. <!-- PLAN §2.4; src/gwsource/trace.py::net_fixed_cells -->

Net accounting has no cell budget error term. The water it attributes to a group counts each passage once: the throughflow of the fixed cells, less the water passing from one fixed cell to another and less the water that leaves the group and reaches it again. Its closure check (volume from the sources equals this net inflow) tests the sum over the cells outside the group, not each cell. Its error is exactly the sum of those cells' equation errors. <!-- PLAN §2.4; src/gwsource/assemble.py and diagnostics.py docstrings -->

## {SI: travel-time identity}

Multiplying each cell's travel-time equation (Eq. 8) by its outflow and adding over all cells gives, with the cell budget errors $r_i$,

$$\sum_i \mathrm{Src}_i\, m_i - \sum_i r_i\, m_i = \sum_i V_i F_i .$$

Divided by the destination inflow $\sum_i d_i$, the right-hand side is the turnover time: the volume of water that ends at the destination divided by the destination's inflow. By the backward closure check (Eq. 5), the left-hand side divided by the same inflow is the flow-weighted mean travel time of all water reaching the destination. The two are therefore equal once the cell budget errors are included. <!-- src/gwsource/traveltime.py docstring, "Exact identity (backward)" -->

The mean travel time $m_i/F_i$ of Section 2.6 includes the residence in the cell where the water leaves, for a well its own cell. The time until the water enters that cell is $(m_i - x_i)/F_i$, where $x$ solves $(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{P})\,\mathbf{x} = \boldsymbol{\tau} \circ (\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{D})$, the expected residence in the cell of exit, weighted by the share leaving there through the destination. Particle trackers that stop particles on entering a sink cell (MODFLOW 6 PRT, MODPATH) measure this second time. It is the full-mixing time compared with particles under Table S1 ({SI: verification errors per check and model}). <!-- src/gwsource/traveltime.py docstring (mean_time_to_exit_cell) --> On model 1, the water reaching the well takes 9,607 d on average including its time in the well cell, and 9,574 d to enter that cell. <!-- PLAN Phase 3 notes, PRT (model 1: Eulerian 9574 d to the exit cell, 9607 d including it) -->

## {SI: solution of the linear systems}

Equations (3) and (4) of the main text are solved in flow order. Where the cell-to-cell flows contain no loop, the matrix is permuted to triangular form, with every cell after the cells it depends on, and solved by substitution. This is the flow-order algorithm of FlowSource (Foley and Black 2013) in matrix form. Where there are loops, the matrix is permuted to block-triangular form. Each block is one strongly connected component of the flow graph, that is, one flow loop or one single cell, and the blocks are in flow order. The matrix is then factorised once by sparse LU in that order. Fill-in stays inside the loop blocks. <!-- src/gwsource/solve.py module docstring (topological and block solvers) -->

Each cell's outflow shares add up to at most 1, so $\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{P}$ is a weakly row-diagonally dominant M-matrix. Its factorisation therefore needs no pivoting, the row swaps that otherwise guard against round-off growth. The matrix is singular only on a closed recirculation loop, a set of cells with no outflow to any sink or to a cell outside the set. Water in such a loop never reaches a sink. These loops are found before the solve, given $F = 0$ and reported. Under gross accounting, one factorisation serves every destination group, and it is reused for the travel-time system of Eq. (8). In transient tracking, each step is solved in the same way, with one matrix for all tagged groups. <!-- src/gwsource/solve.py module docstring ("Pivoting is not needed: I - P is a (weakly) row-diagonally dominant M-matrix"; isolate_closed_loops; factorize() reuse for gwsource.traveltime); moved from §2.4 and §2.7 in round 5 -->

## {SI: water volume options}

Transient tracking needs the water volume of every cell at the start and end of each step. The code offers two options. In the default, called "flow", each volume starts at porosity times saturated volume and then changes only by the storage flows of the flow model. Every cell's water balance and tagged-water balance then close exactly. In the "gwt" option, the volume is reset to porosity times saturated volume at the end of each step, as in MODFLOW 6 GWT. Forward transient tracking with this option is unit-concentration transport in GWT (Bedekar et al. 2024; Langevin et al. 2022). Where porosity differs from specific yield, the reset volume does not match the storage flows. Tagged water then appears or disappears at step boundaries. The size of this effect on the seasonal example model is given in {SI: transient checks on the seasonal example model}. <!-- moved from §2.7 in round 5 (v6 §2.8 text); README_method.md Fig. 4 caption ("the two options differ by less than 2 × 10⁻⁴") -->
