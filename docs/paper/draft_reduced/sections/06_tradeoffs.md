# 6. Trade-offs and conclusions

`gwsource` builds on the full-mixing method of FlowSource (Foley and Black 2013). It extends that method to every MODFLOW 6 grid type, to package networks and linked models, and through storage in transient models. <!-- Sections 3, 4.2 and 4.4; full draft Sections 4.4, 4.7, 4.8 and 4.10 -->

But full-mixing areas are wider and more diffuse than particle areas (Section 5). Their edge is a band of cells where F lies between 0.1 and 0.9. In two simple test problems, halving the cell size made the band only about 30 % narrower ({SI: numerical dispersion}; {SI: numerical dispersion against cell size}). <!-- evidence: docs/dispersion_study.md Summary and §2 (band 1.2 to 12 cells wide; width narrows as about h^0.5, fitted rates 0.4 to 0.6) --> Potter et al. (2008) also found wider spreading on coarser grids. <!-- Potter p. 338; McLane and Cecan (2009, p. 172 and Fig. 1) say the same, cited in the SI comparison of methods --> On squares turned by 45° to the flow, the band was 1.8 times as wide as on squares aligned with it ({SI: the same trace on different grids}). <!-- evidence: tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb §4, cells 20, 22 and 25 (edge width F 0.5 to 0.1 at x = 0.5 km: DIS 121 m, rotated 45° squares 215 m) --> Where the edge of an area matters, finer cells along it and a grid aligned with the main flow narrow the band.

No single threshold on F therefore reproduces a particle area everywhere. The threshold is a judgement about the purpose of the area. Both the threshold and the accounting should be reported with the area. In the test problems, on cells of 50 m or less, the F > 0.5 area was within 5 % of the size of the reference area. The F > 0.1 area missed at most 0.1 % of the reference area. It was 8 to 90 % larger. <!-- evidence: docs/dispersion_study.md Summary and §7 (F ≥ 0.5 within 5 % at h ≤ 50 m; F ≥ 0.1 missed ≤ 0.1 % and was 30 to 90 % too large for problem A and 8 to 16 % for problem B at h = 12.5 to 50 m); si/C.md (strict and non-strict thresholds select the same full-mixing cells) --> On Neversink, the F > 0.1 area still missed cells that weight-losing particles assign to the wells (Section 5). <!-- evidence: envelope_prt.csv (all, P > 0.5: F > 0.1 coverage 0.871, 89 of 691 cells missed) --> So a low threshold does not guarantee a cautious area on a regional model.

Transient tracking has so far been tested on synthetic models only. Its memory grows with the number of time steps. Movers between models (the MVE package) are not implemented yet. DISU grids without cell vertices give no maps. PRT does not run on DISU grids. <!-- tutorials/07_networks_multimodel/07_networks_multimodel.ipynb §6 "Current limitations" (MVE not read); tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb (DISU without vertices: no maps); previous/07_limitations.md (PRT supports DIS and DISV only); src/gwsource/transient.py TransientConfig.keep_series (one value per cell and time step by default); full draft Section 6 -->

Totals depend much less on mixing than edges do (Sections 4.1 and 4.4). We would therefore use particles where a sharp edge or a first arrival matters, such as a short time-of-travel zone. We would use GWT where dispersion, sorption or decay matter, because transient tracking moves water by advection only (Langevin et al. 2022). Full mixing suits questions about many sinks, unstructured grids, package networks or many realisations. <!-- references/docs/text/2019_EnvironmentAgency_SPZ_manual.txt, pp. 76–78 (MODPATH time-of-travel zones for SPZ1 and SPZ2; total catchments checked against the full-mixing outputs) -->
