# 5. Trade-offs and conclusions

From the flows that a MODFLOW 6 model saves, `gwsource` maps where the water of any sink comes from and where the water of any source goes. The same trace splits a sink's water by source and gives mean travel times. The code extends the full-mixing method of FlowSource (Foley and Black 2013) to every MODFLOW 6 grid type, to stream networks, to linked models and to transient flow. Every result is checked against the water budget (Section 2). The outputs agree with GWT and with a published unit-concentration study (Section 3). On a published regional model, a trace took less time than the fastest particle-tracking run (Section 4). <!-- evidence: Sections 2–4 of this draft; weak-sink accounting left to Sections 1 and 2 for length; GWT to round-off with the same water volumes (Table 1 rows 03 and 08); Bedekar et al. The main trade-off is that full-mixing areas are wider and more diffuse than particle areas (Section 2). Their edge is a band of cells where F lies between 0 and 1. A sink's overall mean travel time and its split by source depend less on mixing than the edge of its area does. Any method that conserves water gives the same overall mean travel time (Section 3). In a test problem, particles gave the same split by source ({SI: numerical dispersion test problems}). On the regional model, with its more complex flow system, the split by source differed between the two methods (Section 4). <!-- evidence: Section 3 "How the outputs compare with other methods" (any method that conserves water gives the same overall mean; Table 1 row 06, all water within 0.5 %, not an independent test); docs/dispersion_study.md §2 "Shares of the well water" (problem B, RIV and RCH, shares the same to three digits under full mixing, face-to-face routing and particles); Neversink 57/43 under full mixing against 49/51 along weight-losing particle paths (Section 4; docs/neversink_study.md §3); scoping per reviews/r2_examples_facts.md finding 10 -->

We would therefore use particles where a sharp edge or a first arrival matters, such as a short time-of-travel zone. We would use GWT where dispersion, sorption or decay matter, because transient tracking moves water by advection only (Langevin et al. 2022). Full mixing suits many sinks, unstructured grids, package networks and repeated runs, such as one trace for every realisation of an ensemble. <!-- references/docs/text/2019_EnvironmentAgency_SPZ_manual.txt, pp. 76–78 (MODPATH time-of-travel zones for SPZ1 and SPZ2; total catchments checked against the full-mixing outputs); repeated runs: docs/neversink_study.md §6 "Runs" (two traces per realisation in about 9 s, 358 realisations; Section 4); Table 1 row 09 -->

The method also has practical limits. Transient tracking has been tested on synthetic models only. Its memory grows with the number of time steps. Movers between models (the MVE package) are not implemented yet. Without cell vertices, the code cannot map results on a DISU grid.
