# 1. Introduction

Practitioners who ask where groundwater comes from and where it goes also need to know how the answers change through the year and how sure they can be of them. <!-- evidence: docs/paper/outline.md §3.1 (the practitioner's questions) -->

Methods that answer these questions from a groundwater flow model fall into four families. Particle tracking follows particles of water along flow paths. It is the standard tool for mapping a well's contributing area. This is the land where water enters the aquifer and later reaches the well. In MODFLOW models it runs in MODPATH (Pollock 2016) or in the particle-tracking model of MODFLOW 6 (PRT; Langevin et al. 2026). Transport with tagged water gives the water from one source a concentration of 1 and all other water a concentration of 0. A cell's concentration is then the share of its water that came from that source (Bedekar et al. 2024). <!-- Bedekar p. 304: C = 1 for the source assessed, C = 0 for all other sources; the concentration distribution "signifies the fraction of water emanating from the particular source(s)" --> In MODFLOW 6 it runs in the Groundwater Transport (GWT) Model, with one model for each source (Langevin et al. 2022). <!-- display/A.md, Table 1 row "Destinations or sources per run" (one tagged source per GWT model) --> As documented, the unit-concentration method steps forward in time only. It gives the share of each cell's water that came from a source, but not the share that will later reach a well. <!-- Bedekar p. 306: well capture is read forward, from the concentration simulated at the pumping well over 25 years (Fig. 2); pp. 303–308 describe no backward-in-time or reversed-flow run --> Adjoint and age methods solve transport equations of their own (Neupauer and Wilson 1999; Goode 1996). Full-mixing flow tracing splits each cell's mixed water among its outflows.

Particle tracking has one difficulty at weak sinks. Sink terms are the boundaries that remove water from a cell, such as a pumping well or a gaining stream. A weak sink is a cell whose sink terms take only part of the cell's outflow. A particle cannot split. It must either stop at a weak sink or pass on. If it stops, all its water is counted as reaching the sink. If it passes, none of it is. The rule chosen changes the contributing area (Section 5).

Under full mixing, a weak sink can take just its share of the cell's water. The rest passes on to the neighbouring cells. Two published full-mixing programs apply different rules at a weak-sink well. FlowSource (Foley and Black 2013) is used to draw source protection zones in England (Environment Agency 2019). These zones restrict activities that could pollute the water a supply well pumps. <!-- references/docs/text/2019_EnvironmentAgency_SPZ_manual.txt, Section 1 ("the use of FlowSource as a post-processing tool from the models to help produce the hydraulic capture zones"); Appendix C4.4 --> FlowSource counts all the water passing through a weak-sink well's cell as the well's water. We refer to this as net accounting. For transient models, it traces each time step as a steady-state flow field. We refer to such a trace as a snapshot. <!-- references/docs/text/2019_EnvironmentAgency_SPZ_manual.txt, Appendix C4.4 ("For each stress period, FlowSource identifies how much of the water in each model cell is ultimately removed"); display/A.md, Table 1 rows "Accounting at weak sinks" and "Transient models"; si/A.md --> MODALL (Potter et al. 2008) applies full mixing to steady-state MODFLOW-88 models. <!-- Potter p. 336: "complete mix theory (Gelhar and Wilson 1974)"; step 1, "A steady-state MODFLOW-88 simulation is completed" --> At a weak-sink well it counts only the water the well pumps. We refer to this as gross accounting. <!-- Potter Fig. 1, p. 336: at the sink cell CF = Q_sink / total inflow to the cell, drawn for a weak sink --> All these methods are compared in {SI: comparison of methods}.

This paper presents open-source code, `gwsource`, that extends the full-mixing method of FlowSource to MODFLOW 6. It reads every MODFLOW 6 grid type, stream networks and linked models. <!-- src/gwsource (network.py, packages.py); display/A.md, Table 1 row "Grids and packages" --> It uses gross accounting by default. It checks every result against the water budget. <!-- previous/01_introduction.md:23 (gross default, budget checks) --> In transient models, it carries tagged water through storage, forward and backward in time. Risk-based studies run an ensemble of model versions that fit the measurements about equally well (Fienen et al. 2022). The code is fast enough to trace every version. <!-- docs/neversink_study.md Summary "Cost" (2 to 3 s for nine destination groups) and "Ensemble" (358 realisations, two traces each) -->
