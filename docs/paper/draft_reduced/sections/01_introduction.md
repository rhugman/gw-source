# 1. Introduction

Groundwater studies often ask where water comes from and where it goes. Which land feeds a supply well, a spring or a stream? How much of a well's water comes from a river? Practitioners also need to know how the answers change through the year and how sure they can be of them. <!-- evidence: docs/paper/outline.md §3.1 (the practitioner's questions) -->

Methods that answer these questions from a groundwater flow model fall into four families. Particle tracking follows particles of water along flow paths. It is the standard tool for mapping a well's contributing area. This is the land where water enters the aquifer and later reaches the well. In MODFLOW models it runs in MODPATH (Pollock 2016) or in the particle-tracking model of MODFLOW 6 (PRT; Langevin et al. 2026). Transport with tagged water gives the water from one source a concentration of 1 and all other water a concentration of 0 (Bedekar et al. 2024). <!-- Bedekar p. 304: C = 1 for the source assessed, C = 0 for all other sources --> In MODFLOW 6 it runs in the Groundwater Transport (GWT) Model, with one model for each source (Langevin et al. 2022). <!-- display/A.md, Table 1 row "Destinations or sources per run" (one tagged source per GWT model) --> Adjoint and age methods solve transport equations of their own (Neupauer and Wilson 1999; Goode 1996). Full-mixing flow tracing splits each cell's mixed water among its outflows.

But particles need a rule at weak sinks. Sink terms are the boundaries that remove water from a cell, such as a pumping well or a gaining stream. A weak sink is a cell whose sink terms take only part of the cell's outflow. A particle cannot split. It must either stop at a weak sink or pass on. If it stops, all its water is counted as reaching the sink. If it passes, none of it is. The rule chosen changes the contributing area (Section 5).

Under full mixing, a weak sink takes its share of the cell's water. The rest passes on to the neighbouring cells. FlowSource (Foley and Black 2013; Black and Foley 2013) uses this method to draw source protection zones in England (Environment Agency 2019). <!-- references/docs/text/2019_EnvironmentAgency_SPZ_manual.txt, Section 1 ("the use of FlowSource as a post-processing tool from the models to help produce the hydraulic capture zones"); Appendix C4.4 --> It counts all the water passing through a weak-sink well's cell as the well's water. We refer to this as net accounting. For transient models, it traces each time step as a steady-state flow field. We refer to such a trace as a snapshot. <!-- references/docs/text/2019_EnvironmentAgency_SPZ_manual.txt, Appendix C4.4 ("For each stress period, FlowSource identifies how much of the water in each model cell is ultimately removed"); display/A.md, Table 1 rows "Accounting at weak sinks" and "Transient models"; si/A.md --> MODALL (Potter et al. 2008) applies full mixing to steady-state MODFLOW-88 models. <!-- Potter p. 336: "complete mix theory (Gelhar and Wilson 1974)"; step 1, "A steady-state MODFLOW-88 simulation is completed" --> At a weak-sink well it counts only the water the well pumps. We refer to this as gross accounting. <!-- Potter Fig. 1, p. 336: at the sink cell CF = Q_sink / total inflow to the cell, drawn for a weak sink --> As published, neither program reads MODFLOW 6 output. <!-- MODALL is for MODFLOW-88 (Potter p. 336); both papers predate MODFLOW 6 (Langevin et al. 2017) --> All these methods are compared in {SI: comparison of methods}.

This paper presents open-source code, `gwsource`, that extends the full-mixing method of FlowSource to MODFLOW 6. It reads every MODFLOW 6 grid type, stream networks and linked models. <!-- src/gwsource (network.py, packages.py); display/A.md, Table 1 row "Grids and packages" --> It uses gross accounting by default. It checks every result against the water budget. <!-- previous/01_introduction.md:23 (gross default, budget checks) --> In transient models, it carries tagged water through storage, forward and backward in time. It is fast enough for risk-based studies, which run an ensemble of model versions that fit the measurements about equally well (Fienen et al. 2022). <!-- docs/neversink_study.md Summary "Cost" (2 to 3 s for nine destination groups) and "Ensemble" (358 realisations, two traces each) -->
