# Where does it come from and where does it go? Fast, open-source flow tracing for MODFLOW 6 models

**Article Impact Statement:** Open-source code turns saved MODFLOW 6 flows into contributing areas, water sources and travel times for any well or stream, in steady-state or transient flow, fast enough for ensembles.

**Abstract:** Groundwater studies often ask where water comes from and where it goes. Which land feeds a well, spring or stream? How much of a well's water is river water? Where does recharge under a polluted field end up? We present open-source code, `gwsource`, that answers such questions from the flows a MODFLOW 6 model saves. The code treats the water in each cell as fully mixed, like earlier methods. It computes the share of each cell's water that ends at a well, a stream or another outlet. The same trace splits a well's water by source and gives mean travel times. The code extends the method to every MODFLOW 6 grid type, stream network and linked model. It handles weak sinks. It carries tagged water through storage between time steps, forward and backward in time. Every result is checked against the flow model's water budget. The paper presents ten worked examples. Each checks an output a practitioner would use against other commonly used approaches. Results matched MODFLOW 6 transport to its solver tolerance with the same water volumes. They matched particle tracking in totals and mean travel times, as any method that conserves water should. At the edges of areas the two differ cell by cell. On a regional model of 1.68 million cells, one trace of eight wells, alone and together, took 2–3 s. That is small next to the model run itself.
