# Where does it come from and where does it go? Open-source flow tracing for MODFLOW 6 models

**Article Impact Statement:** Open-source code turns saved MODFLOW 6 flows into contributing areas, water sources and travel times for any well or stream, in steady-state or transient flow.

**Abstract:** Groundwater studies often ask where water comes from and where it goes. Which land feeds a well, spring or stream? How much of a well's water is river water? Where does recharge under a polluted field end up? Protection zones around water supplies rest on such answers. We present open-source code, `gwsource`, that answers such questions from the flows a MODFLOW 6 model saves. The code treats the water in each model cell as fully mixed. It computes the share of each cell's water that ends at a well, a stream or another outlet. The same trace splits that outlet's water by source and gives mean travel times. The code extends the full-mixing method of FlowSource (Foley and Black 2013) to every MODFLOW 6 grid type, stream networks and linked models. In transient models it carries tagged water through storage, forward and backward in time. Every result is checked against the flow model's water budget. The outputs matched MODFLOW 6 transport of tagged water to round-off when both used the same water volumes. They also reproduced a published transport study. Particle tracking gave the same overall mean travel times, as any method that conserves water should. On a published regional model of 1.68 million cells, a trace of eight wells took 2–3 s. A trace is therefore cheap enough to repeat for every realisation of an ensemble. Full-mixing areas are wider and more diffuse than particle areas, so the threshold that defines an area is a judgement.
