# 5. A regional model: Neversink

This section tests the method on a published regional model. The USGS Neversink/Rondout model (Fienen et al. 2022) is a steady-state model of 1.68 million cells, with 12,316 reaches of the Streamflow Routing (SFR) package. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1 (4 layers of 680 × 619 cells of 50 m, 1,683,680 cells; SFR 12,316 reaches) --> Its 358 realisations come from history matching with PEST++ IES. We refer to the realisation with the base parameters as the calibrated model. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §6 and line 202 (rebuilt base realisation equals the published calibrated input); si/D.md (run 357 is the calibrated realisation); ensemble/summary.csv (358 realisations) --> The published study mapped the contributing areas of eight priority wells with MODPATH 7 (Pollock 2016). One particle started in the uppermost active cell of each column near the wells. We refer to these cells as the start cells. The published contributing cells are the 980 start cells whose particle ends in a priority well. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §2 (three regions, 122,021 particles); mp7_status.csv -->

We traced the eight wells in the calibrated model with gross accounting ({SI: Neversink full-mixing maps}). The stream reaches were left as source and sink terms of the aquifer cells. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §2 (streams-as-cells trace of the calibrated model, gross accounting; checks_cells.csv) --> These full-mixing areas differ from the published ones. We measure the difference with an overlap score. It is the number of cells in both areas divided by the number in either. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 (Jaccard index) --> The published area comes from one reasonable choice of particle settings. These settings fix where recharge enters a cell, where particles stop and how particles that start in dry cells are handled. PRT on the same flow field reproduces the published area (overlap 0.998) only with all three published settings. Among particle runs that conserve water, the settings alone move the overlap from 0.62 to 0.998 ({SI: delineations on one flow field}). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §2, §3, Table 3 and reproduction table; mp7_reproduction.csv (0.998; 0.65 draped, 0.62 dropped); prt_comparison.csv -->

The full-mixing areas where F exceeds 0.5 and 0.1 agree less, with overlaps of 0.41 and 0.30. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2, Table 1; agreement_with_mp7.csv --> We infer that the main reason is that full mixing splits each cell's water among all its outflows, while a particle follows one path. On average, the published cells send 42 % of their water to the wells. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 ("Their mean F is 0.42") --> So under full mixing many published cells fall below F = 0.5. The F > 0.1 area holds 73 % of them (Fig. 3). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 (F > 0.1 contains 711 of the 980 published cells) --> Weight-losing particles lose water at each sink as full mixing does. The F > 0.1 area holds 87 % of the 691 start cells where these particles deliver more than half their water to the wells. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §5, Table 3a; envelope_prt.csv (all, P>0.5: F>0.1 coverage 0.871, 691 PRT cells) -->

Weak sinks add to the difference. A buried stream cell has an SFR reach and another active cell above it. A particle cannot leave such a cell through its top face. Where the reach gains water, the stream is a weak sink. A published particle then stops in the cell. All its water counts as reaching the stream. Published particles stop in the same way at weak-sink wells. Full mixing passes the rest of the cell's water on ({SI: end states of the published particles}). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 "End states" (status 3: 26,828 stopped in stream cells with an active cell above; 466 in the five weak-sink priority wells) --> Net accounting treats weak-sink wells as stopping particles do. It raises the F > 0.5 overlap only from 0.41 to 0.44 ({SI: delineations on one flow field}). <!-- evidence: si/D.md Table S3; docs/neversink_study.md §2, Tables 2a–2b; net_vs_gross_mp7.csv --> For a practitioner, an overlap of 0.41 should be read against the range of 0.62 to 0.998 that particle settings alone produce. <!-- evidence: agreement_with_mp7.csv (0.41); mp7_reproduction.csv (0.62 dropped, 0.998 published settings) -->
