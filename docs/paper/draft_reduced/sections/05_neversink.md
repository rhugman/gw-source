# 5. A regional model: Neversink

This section shows that a trace works and stays fast on a published regional model. It also explains why full-mixing areas differ from the published particle areas, so that a practitioner can read one next to the other. The USGS Neversink/Rondout model (Fienen et al. 2022; Corson-Dosch et al. 2022) is a steady-state MODFLOW 6 model of 1.68 million cells. Eight priority wells pump 3,671 m³/d. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1 (4 layers of 680 × 619 cells of 50 m, 1,683,680 cells; SFR 12,316 reaches; total pumping of the eight priority wells 3,671 m³/d) --> Its 358 realisations come from history matching with PEST++ IES (White 2018; White et al. 2020). We refer to the realisation with the base parameters as the calibrated model. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §6 and line 202 (rebuilt base realisation equals the published calibrated input); si/D.md (run 357 is the calibrated realisation); ensemble/summary.csv (358 realisations) --> The published study mapped the wells' contributing area in the calibrated model with MODPATH 7 (Pollock 2016). One particle started in the uppermost active cell of each column near the wells. We refer to these cells as the start cells. The published contributing cells are the 980 start cells whose particle ends in a priority well. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §2 (three regions, 122,021 particles); mp7_status.csv --> We traced the wells in the calibrated model with gross accounting. The stream reaches stayed source and sink terms of the aquifer cells. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §2 (trace of the calibrated model with SFR leakage as a source and sink term of the aquifer cells, called "streams as cells" in NS; gross accounting; checks_cells.csv) -->

One trace of nine destination groups (each well alone and all eight together) took 2–3 s and peaked at 2.5 GB of memory. Each further group added about 0.19 GB ({SI: the Neversink comparison}). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md Summary "Cost" (2 to 3 s, nine destination groups); reviews/r4_memory.md (load plus trace 2.4 to 2.7 s; peak RSS 2.52 GB for nine groups; (2.52 − 0.98) / 8 = 0.19 GB per further group; Apple M4 Max, 64 GB) --> The closure check held to 1 × 10⁻¹² m³/d or better for every group. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1 "Checks" (backward closure 1e-12 m³/d or smaller for every group, with streams as cells and as nodes) --> PRT on the same flows took about 30 s when it wrote only where each particle ends. With full particle tracks it took about 5 min for 122,112 particles and about 13 min for 488,448 particles. Their track files were about 1.5 and 6 GB. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md "Reproducing" (reproduction of the published mask: PRT five times, about 30 s each, only termination records written; single-height runs, 122,112 particles, about 5 minutes and 1.5 GB; four-height runs, 488,448 particles, about 13 minutes each and about 6 GB); §3 (122,112 and 488,448 particles). PRT memory was not measured and is left out --> These times do not compare like with like, because one PRT run follows one particle convention for all wells, while one trace gives F, the volumes and the checks for every group. The weight-losing PRT runs also needed the trace first, because they were released only from cells with F > 10⁻⁶. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §3 (particles released from the 30,180 cells with F > 1e-6, flux-weighted, in all layers) --> Across the ensemble, two traces of each realisation took about 9 s. Closure held to 3 × 10⁻¹² m³/d in every realisation. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §6 "Runs" (per realisation about 9 s for both traces, one with streams as cells and one with the stream network; closure in every realisation to 3e-12 m³/d) -->

The full-mixing areas differ from the published area. We measure the difference with an overlap score. It is the number of cells in both areas divided by the number in either. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 (Jaccard index) --> The published area comes from one reasonable choice of particle settings. Recharge and stream flows enter a cell through its top face. A particle stops in the first weak-sink cell it enters. A particle that starts in a dry cell is moved down to the first active cell. PRT on the same flows reproduces the published area (overlap 0.998) only with all three settings. Among particle runs that conserve water, the settings alone move the overlap from 0.62 to 0.998 ({SI: the Neversink comparison}). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §2, §3, Table 3 and reproduction table; mp7_reproduction.csv (0.998; 0.65 draped, 0.62 dropped); prt_comparison.csv -->

Full mixing lies below that range. The areas where F exceeds 0.5 and 0.1 have overlaps of 0.41 and 0.30. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2, Table 1; agreement_with_mp7.csv --> We infer that the main reason is that full mixing splits each cell's water among all its outflows, while a particle follows one path. On average, the published cells send 42 % of their water to the wells. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 ("Their mean F is 0.42") --> Many of them therefore fall below F = 0.5 (Fig. 4a). The F > 0.1 area holds 73 % of them (711 of 980; Fig. 4b). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 (F > 0.1 contains 711 of the 980 published cells) --> Weight-losing particles (Section 4) give a closer comparison, because they lose water at each sink as full mixing does. The F > 0.1 area holds 87 % of the 691 start cells where these particles deliver more than half their water to the wells. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §5, Table 3a; envelope_prt.csv (all, P>0.5: F>0.1 coverage 0.871, 691 PRT cells) --> The 89 cells it misses supply 6 % of the particles' water to the wells (227 m³/d). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §5, Table 3a and text below it (89 missed cells; particles deliver 227 m³/d of their source water, 6 % of the 3,736 m³/d they deliver); envelope_prt.csv (226.7 of 3,736 m³/d) -->

Weak sinks add to the difference. A buried stream cell holds a stream reach and has another active cell above it. Where such a reach gains water, it is a weak sink that stops published particles. In the published runs, 26,828 particles stopped in buried stream cells and 466 in the five priority wells that take only part of their cell's outflow. Each stopped particle counts in full as reaching that sink, while full mixing passes the rest of the cell's water on ({SI: the Neversink comparison}). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 "End states" (status 3: 26,828 stopped in stream cells with an active cell above; 466 in the five weak-sink priority wells) --> Net accounting counts a weak-sink well's water much as a stopping particle does. It raises the F > 0.5 overlap only from 0.41 to 0.44. <!-- evidence: si/D.md, table of delineations; docs/neversink_study.md §2, Tables 2a–2b; net_vs_gross_mp7.csv -->

Example C (Section 4) showed that a sink's split by source depends on how stream reaches are traced. On this model the split also depends on the method. Full mixing attributes 57 % of the wells' water to recharge and 43 % to stream leakage. Along weight-losing particle paths the split is 49 % and 51 %, although both methods deliver the wells' total inflow to within 2 %. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §3 (prt_sources_by_well.csv, one PRT run evaluated for every well: 57/43 under full mixing, 49/51 along the particle paths); §4 (2,100 m³/d, 57 %, recharge; 1,571 m³/d, 43 %, SFR leakage); §3, Table 3 (weight-losing particles over the cell volume 3,736 m³/d, 1.8 % above the wells' inflow of 3,671 m³/d) --> We infer, without tracing it cell by cell, that leaked stream water near the wells stays on particle paths that end at a well, while full mixing gives it the average fate of the water leaving the cells it passes. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §7 "Why thinned particles grow faster" (inferred, not traced cell by cell) and §8 --> With the reaches traced as the stream network, all of the wells' water traces back to recharge. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md Summary "Streams" and §4 (all 3,671 m³/d traces to recharge with the SFR reaches as cells of the traced system) -->
