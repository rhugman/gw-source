# 5. A regional model: Neversink

This section tests the method on a published regional model. The USGS Neversink/Rondout model (Fienen et al. 2022; Corson-Dosch et al. 2022) is a steady-state model of 1.68 million cells, with 12,316 SFR reaches. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1 (4 layers of 680 × 619 cells of 50 m, 1,683,680 cells; SFR 12,316 reaches) --> The published study mapped the contributing areas of eight priority wells with MODPATH 7 (Pollock 2016). One particle started in the uppermost active cell of each column near the wells. The published contributing cells are the 980 of these start cells whose particle ends in a priority well. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §2 (three regions, 122,021 particles); mp7_status.csv --> Maps of F around the wells are given in {SI: Neversink full-mixing maps}.

Our areas differ from the published ones. We measure the difference with an overlap score. It is the number of cells in both areas divided by the number in either. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 (Jaccard index) --> The published area comes from one reasonable choice of particle settings. These settings fix where recharge enters a cell, where particles stop and how particles that start in dry cells are handled. PRT on the same flow field reproduces the published area (overlap 0.998) only with all three published settings. Among particle runs that conserve water, the settings alone move the overlap from 0.62 to 0.998 ({SI: delineations on one flow field}). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §2, §3, Table 3 and reproduction table; mp7_reproduction.csv (0.998; 0.65 draped, 0.62 dropped); prt_comparison.csv -->

The full-mixing areas where F exceeds 0.5 and 0.1 agree less, with overlaps of 0.41 and 0.30. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2, Table 1; agreement_with_mp7.csv --> We infer that the main reason is that full mixing splits each cell's water among all its outflows, while a particle follows one path. On average, the published cells send 42 % of their water to the wells. The F > 0.1 area holds 73 % of them. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 ("Their mean F is 0.42"; F > 0.1 contains 711 of the 980 published cells) --> Weight-losing particles lose water at each sink as full mixing does. The F > 0.1 area holds 87 % of the 691 start cells where these particles deliver more than half their water to the wells. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §5, Table 3a; envelope_prt.csv (all, P>0.5: F>0.1 coverage 0.871, 691 PRT cells) -->

Weak sinks add to the difference. A buried stream cell has an SFR reach and another active cell above it. Where the reach gains water, a published particle stops in the cell. All its water then counts as reaching the stream. Published particles stop in the same way at weak-sink wells. Full mixing passes the rest of the cell's water on ({SI: end states of the published particles}). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 "End states" (status 3: 26,828 stopped in stream cells with an active cell above; 466 in the five weak-sink priority wells) -->
