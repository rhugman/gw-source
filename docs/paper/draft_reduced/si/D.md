## {SI: the Neversink comparison}

Section 5 compares full mixing with the published particle tracking of the Neversink/Rondout model (Fienen et al. 2022; Corson-Dosch et al. 2022). Section 5 also defines the calibrated model, the start cells, the published contributing cells and the overlap score. This text gives the cost of the trace and of the particle runs, the delineations behind the overlaps and how the published particles end. The model is in a USGS data release (Fienen and Corson-Dosch 2021). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §6 and line 202 (rebuilt base realisation equals the published calibrated input); run 357 is the calibrated realisation; ensemble/summary.csv (358 realisations) -->

The 122,021 start cells are the uppermost active cell of each column in three regions around the wells. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1 (three regions NE, S, W; 122,021 particles) --> The expected area of a map of F is the sum of F over the start cells. It is a size of the contributing area that needs no threshold. All Neversink cells are 50 m squares, so expected areas are given as numbers of cells. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1, §2 (three regions, 122,021 particles; Jaccard index); full draft Section 4.1 (expected area) and Section 5 (sum over the start cells; all cells are 50 m) -->

### Cost of the trace and of the particle runs

The trace was timed on an Apple M4 Max with 16 cores and 64 GB of memory. One trace of the nine destination groups took 2.4 to 2.7 s in all. Reading the budget file took 1.0 to 1.2 s of this. The trace itself took 1.4 to 1.5 s. <!-- evidence: reviews/r4_memory.md (Machine; Results: load 1.0 to 1.2 s, trace 1.4 to 1.5 s, load plus trace 2.4 to 2.7 s) --> Memory peaked at 2.52 GB, of which 0.52 GB was the loaded budget. A trace of the eight wells together, as one group, peaked at 0.98 GB. Each further group therefore adds about 0.19 GB on this model of 1.68 million cells. <!-- evidence: reviews/r4_memory.md (peak RSS 0.52 GB load only, 2.52 GB nine groups, 0.98 GB one group; (2.52 − 0.98) / 8 = 0.19 GB) --> The closure check held to 1 × 10⁻¹² m³/d or better for every group. This was so with the stream reaches left as source and sink terms and with them traced as the stream network. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §1 "Checks" -->

The PRT runs of Section 5 differ in what they release and what they write. The runs that reproduce the published area release one unweighted particle at each published start and write only where each particle ends. Each took about 30 s. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md "Reproducing" (PRT five times, about 30 s each, only termination records written) and §3 "Reproducing the published mask with PRT" --> The flow-weighted runs release particles from every source term of the 30,180 cells with F > 10⁻⁶, in all layers, and write full tracks. With four release points in plan at one height, 122,112 particles took about 5 min and wrote a track file of about 1.5 GB. With the same points at four heights, 488,448 particles took about 13 min and wrote about 6 GB. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §3 (30,180 cells, one plan sub-division per cell, 4 points, four heights 488,448 particles or one 122,112) and "Reproducing" (about 13 minutes and about 6 GB; about 5 minutes and 1.5 GB) --> The peak memory of PRT was not measured.

Thresholds are strict. The area-matched delineation takes the 980 start cells with the largest F. For flow-weighted PRT, the expected area is the particle weight that reaches the wells, in cells. For published starts, it is the number of start cells whose particle ends in a priority well. The flow-weighted particles were released from the source terms of the 30,180 cells with F > 10⁻⁶, in all layers. P is the share of the water released in a start cell that the particles deliver to the wells. The published-start runs use one unweighted particle per published start, stopped at every weak sink. "MODFLOW 6 default" spreads recharge and stream flows through the cell. "Top face where open" puts them on the top face through IFLOWFACE, the MODFLOW 6 setting that assigns a boundary flow to one cell face. <!-- NS §3 (30,180 cells with F > 1e-6, flux-weighted release in all layers; reproduction with IFLOWFACE −1 and draping); note shortened in SI round 1 -->

The wells take 3,671 m³/d. Gross accounting attributes exactly this flow to them. Net accounting attributes 3,944 m³/d, because it also counts the water that passes on from the well cells. Weight-losing particles deliver within 2 % of the wells' inflow. Particles that lose weight only at the wells deliver 9,555 m³/d. That is 2.6 times the wells' inflow. They keep the weight that streams and other sinks take on the way. Their delivery is therefore water the particles assign to the wells, not water that flows into them. <!-- NS §2, Table 2a (net 3,943.5 m³/d); §3, Table 3 (9,555 m³/d, factor 2.6); prt_diagnosis.csv -->

The 87 % of Section 5 is 602 of the 691 start cells where weight-losing particles released over the cell volume deliver more than half their water to the wells. It is an upper bound, because the particles were released only from cells with F > 10⁻⁶. A cell left out that would have delivered more than half its water would be one more missed cell. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §5, Table 3a caveats (PRT seeded only from cells with F > 1e-6; all 2,139 start cells with F > 0.1 seeded; the 87 % coverage is an upper bound) --> The weight-losing particles also lose weight at each sink by the full-mixing shares. For this reason they are not an independent particle area. The comparison measures only the spreading that full mixing adds between sinks. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §5, Table 3a caveats (the thinned particles lose weight at every weak sink by the full-mixing shares; not an independent advective zone) -->

With the stream reaches left as source and sink terms, full mixing traces 2,100 m³/d of the wells' water to recharge and 1,571 m³/d to stream leakage. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §4 (2,100 m³/d, 57 %; 1,571 m³/d, 43 %) --> Along the paths of the weight-losing particles, the wells receive 344 m³/d more stream water and 279 m³/d less recharge. Most of the extra stream water leaks from reaches 0.4 to 1.3 km south of the U wells and 0.5 to 1.2 km from SV_487. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §3, source split bullets (stream water +344 m³/d; recharge −279 m³/d; reaches 0.4 to 1.3 km south of the U wells and 0.5 to 1.2 km from SV_487; prt_sources_by_well.csv) --> In a cell that receives both sources, the two reach the wells in the same proportion under either method. The difference therefore comes from where each source enters the aquifer. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §3 ("In a cell that receives both, the two sources reach the wells in the same proportion under either method, so the difference comes from where each source enters") --> <!-- NS §2 (711 of 980 published cells inside F > 0.1); NS §5, Table 3a; envelope_prt.csv (all, P>0.5, F>0.1: prt_cells 691, missed_cells 89, coverage 0.871 = 602 / 691; 226.7 of 3,736 m³/d) -->

### End states of the published particles

Each published MODPATH 7 particle on the calibrated model ends in one of three ways. We classed each end cell on the MODFLOW 6 budget. A strong sink sends all of its outflow to boundary sinks. A weak sink sends only part of it. <!-- evidence: studies/neversink/output/mp7_status.csv; docs/neversink_study.md §2, "End states of the published particles" -->

| end state (MODPATH status) | particles | end cells | particles ending in a priority well |
|----------------------------|-----------|---------------------------------------------|----------------|
| leaves the model through a cell face (2) | 89,920 | top face of a stream cell with no active cell above: 88,781; side face next to a dry cell: 1,139 | 0 |
| stopped on entering a weak-sink cell (3) | 27,631 | buried stream cells: 26,828; wells: 612; constant heads: 191 | 466 |
| ends in a cell with no exit face (5) | 4,470 | wells: 2,570; buried stream cells: 1,472; constant heads: 428 | 514 |

<!-- evidence: studies/neversink/output/mp7_status.csv (particles, top_face, end_SFR_0, end_WEL_0, end_CHD_0 and per-well columns by status); per-well counts (SV_407 32, SV_487 97, U__55 116, U___7 58, U___8 163; SV_193 48, SV_227 115, U_917 351) in docs/neversink_study.md §2 -->

The 980 published contributing cells are the 514 particles that end in the three priority wells that take all of their cell's outflow. They also include the 466 particles stopped in the five priority wells that take only part of it. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2; mp7_status.csv -->

A buried stream cell is a cell with an SFR reach and another active cell above it. The published runs put recharge and stream flows on the top face of the cell. MODPATH 7 does this only where the top face has no active neighbour. Elsewhere the flow stays spread through the cell. A particle that reaches a stream cell with no active cell above therefore leaves through the top face (status 2). A particle that reaches a buried stream cell is stopped there as at a weak sink (status 3). Every particle that stops in a stream cell has an active cell above it. None of those that leave through a stream cell's top face has. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 (MPBAS IFACE 6; ModpathCellData.f90) -->

At the weak-sink wells, the published particles and full mixing treat the cell's water differently. The well of U\_\_\_7 takes 44 % of its cell's outflow. All the water of a published particle that enters the cell still counts as reaching U\_\_\_7. Under full mixing, the other 56 % of the cell's outflow passes on, most of it to U\_\_\_8 and U\_917. So 97 % of the water in the cell reaches a priority well (F = 0.97). <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §2 and §3, "Why the first PRT runs captured too little"; net_vs_gross_wells.csv (well_share_of_outflow 0.442) -->

### The ensemble

Section 5 gives the cost of the two traces of each realisation. One trace left the stream reaches as source and sink terms, and the other traced them as the stream network. The whole ensemble, with its MODFLOW 6 runs, took 76 min in six parallel processes. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md Summary ("The ensemble (358 realisations, two traces each) took 76 min on six processes") and §6, "Runs"; moved from the note under the former Table S6 in SI round 1 --> The published study maps a probability of contribution. It is the share of 357 published Monte Carlo contributing areas that contain a cell. Mean F over the realisations, with the stream reaches left as source and sink terms, is the full-mixing counterpart ({SI: Neversink particle area and ensemble mean F}b). It correlates with the published probability at r = 0.80. Its expected area is 0.90 times the published one. The stored published areas lie one row south of their cells, so we moved them one row north before this comparison. The archived code release (Section 7) explains this offset in docs/neversink_study.md. <!-- evidence: docs/neversink_study.md §6, Table 4 and "Pairing the published masks with the realisations"; studies/neversink/output/ensemble/probability_comparison.csv (correlation 0.799; sum_ratio 0.905) -->
