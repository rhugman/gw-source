**Weight-losing particles.** Section 3 compares the contributing area of example A with MODFLOW 6 PRT on the same flows. Particles start from every source term in cells with F > 10⁻⁶. Each particle carries a share of its term's flow as a weight. It loses part of that weight at every other sink it passes, in proportion to the flow that sink takes from the cell. These particles are the particle counterpart of gross accounting. Similar weighting rules at sinks appear in the travel-time method of Visser et al. (2009) and the solute-transport method of Winston et al. (2018). <!-- evidence: src/gwsource/prt.py track_particles (seed from cells with F > threshold; thin_weak_sinks); moved from main-text example A in round 2 --> Because the particles lose weight in this way, they deliver what the well pumps whatever the shape of the area (799.9 of 800 m³/d). The particle share of a cell is the share of the weight released there that reaches the well (main-text Fig. 3a). Cells with a particle share above 0.5 cover 1.168 km². The F > 0.5 area is 2.1 % smaller. The F > 0.1 area holds all of these cells. <!-- evidence: tutorials/01_quickstart/01_quickstart.ipynb §8, cell 24 (18,208 particles from 1,133 cells; 799.9 of 800.0 m³/d; P > 0.5: 467 cells, 1.168 km²; F > 0.5 −2.1 %, 14 particle cells outside; F > 0.1 +34.9 %, 0 outside) -->

**Ensembles.** Many sets of parameters can fit the measured heads about equally well. We refer to each set as a realisation and to a group of realisations as an ensemble. History matching with PEST++ IES, the iterative ensemble smoother of PEST++ (White 2018; White et al. 2020), adjusts the realisations until they fit the measurements. We refer to the ensemble before history matching as the prior and to the history-matched ensemble as the posterior. Table 1 row 09 uses 50 realisations of the hydraulic conductivity of the example model. The measured heads come from a synthetic true model, so the posterior can be compared with the truth. {SI: probability of contribution before and after history matching} maps the probability of contribution before and after history matching. The posterior assigns a probability of at least 0.5 to 93 % of the true F > 0.5 area, against 79 % for the prior. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb Settings, cell 2 (N_REAL 50) and §9 (PEST++ IES on the prior of §1); §9.1, cell 22 (truth, 40 head observations, noise sd 0.05 m); §9.4, cell 28 (true zone with P ≥ 0.5: 79.268 and 93.415 %) --> A narrower posterior is not necessarily a correct one. One synthetic case cannot show how reliable posteriors are in general.

## {SI: probability of contribution before and after history matching}

**Figure.** Probability of contribution before and after history matching on the example model (Section 3). The probability of contribution of a cell is the share of realisations in which more than half of the cell's water ends at the well (F > 0.5). (a) Probability of contribution over 50 prior realisations of the hydraulic conductivity field. (b) The same over the 50 realisations after history matching to heads with PEST++ IES. Black lines: probabilities of 0.1, 0.5 and 0.9. Red dashed line: F = 0.5 in the synthetic true model, which supplied the measured heads. White circles: the 40 cells with head measurements. (c) Share of the pumped water that comes from the river, in each prior and posterior realisation. Each dot is one realisation. Boxes span the 25 to 75 % range and whiskers the 5 to 95 % range. Red dashed line: the river share of the true model.
