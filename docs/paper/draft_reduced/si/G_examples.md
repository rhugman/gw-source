## {SI: accounting at a small well}

This text gives in full one of the worked examples summarised in Section 4 of the main text. The shared model is the example model of that section.

The two accountings of Section 2 differ only at weak sinks. A small well in strong regional flow is a weak sink. Its cell passes most of its water on to the neighbouring cells. This example shows how much the choice of accounting changes the water attributed to such a well. The shared model is split into three layers, of which the middle one is the least permeable ({SI: example and test models}). The supply well pumps 800 m³/d from layer 3. A second, small well pumps 5 m³/d from layer 3 at row 17, column 13, upgradient of the supply well. <!-- evidence: tutorials/herebedragons.py LAYER_BOTM (0, −12, −30 m), LAYER_K (10, 1, 15 m/d), k33_ratio 0.1, LAYERED_WELLS (supply_well layer 3, row 21, column 29, −800 m³/d; weak_well layer 3, row 17, column 13, −5 m³/d); tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §3, cell 13 -->

The small well's cell carries 33.7 m³/d. The well pumps 5 m³/d of it. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §3, cell 13 (inflow 33.67 m³/d, taken by the well 5.00, passed on 28.67) --> Gross accounting counts only these 5 m³/d as the well's water. F in its cell is then 5/33.7 = 0.149. Net accounting fixes F = 1 in the cell. It attributes all 33.7 m³/d to the well. This is 6.7 times what the well pumps. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §3, cell 15 (weak_well: F in cell 0.149 gross, 1.0 net; 5.0 against 33.667 m³/d; net / gross 6.733) --> Both accountings find the same flow path, a narrow strip along row 17 from the west edge to the well ({SI: the worked example of Foley and Black (2013) and a small well}b, c). Under net accounting, 93–99 % of the water in layer 3 along that strip is assigned to the small well. Under gross accounting, 82 % of the water in the small well's cell flows on to the supply well. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §3, cell 18 (net F along row 17 west of the well, layer 3: 0.93 to 0.99; gross F to the supply well in the weak well's cell 0.821) --> The supply well pumps all the water that flows into its cell. For this well the two accountings agree. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §3, cells 14 and 15 (supply_well: cell throughflow 800.0 m³/d, pumping 800.0; F in the well cell 1.0 gross and net) --> Gross accounting treats the well as a transport model does. Net accounting remains an option, for comparison with studies that used it.

Two checks test the code. The first is independent. It uses the worked example that Foley and Black (2013) give to explain FlowSource. It is a block of 3 × 3 cells with given flows and one destination cell ({SI: the worked example of Foley and Black (2013) and a small well}a). Net accounting reproduces their published fractions, volumes through and volumes from in every cell to 1 × 10⁻¹⁴. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §2, cells 7 and 9 (assert atol 1e-14 against Foley and Black 2013, Figs 5–8; numpy.linalg.solve of the 11 × 11 matrix gives the same fractions, left out of the text for length) --> The second check is internal. ## {SI: where each source's water goes}

This text gives in full one of the worked examples summarised in Section 4 of the main text. The shared model is the example model of that section.

Some questions start at a source rather than at a receptor, such as a well, a spring or a stream. A water-quality study may ask how much river water, or how much recharge from one field, reaches a receptor. A forward trace answers it. It gives G in every cell. G is the share of a cell's water that came from the source (Section 2). The model is the shared model, unchanged.

River water enters the aquifer only through the reaches in rows 19 to 23, where pumping draws the water table below the river stage. From there it spreads to the well ({SI: forward fractions of river water and field recharge}a). <!-- evidence: tutorials/03_forward/03_forward.ipynb §4, cell 12 (losing river reaches in rows 19 to 23) --> All 113 m³/d that the river loses reaches the well. Of the regional inflow at the west edge, 37 % reaches the well. Of the recharge, 29 % reaches the well and 71 % drains to the river ({SI: forward fractions of river water and field recharge}c). <!-- evidence: tutorials/03_forward/03_forward.ipynb §5, cell 14 (river 113.3 of 113.3 m³/d to the well; west edge 118.1 of 323.9, 0.365; recharge 568.6 of 1,950.0, 0.292; 1,381.4 to the river) --> G in the well cell gives the pumped water by source. ## {SI: the same trace on different grids}

This text gives in full one of the worked examples summarised in Section 4 of the main text. The shared model is the example model of that section.

MODFLOW 6 models are not limited to regular grids. Some use Voronoi grids refined around wells and rivers (DISV) or fully unstructured grids (DISU). Some use the XT3D option of MODFLOW 6 for anisotropy. This example builds the shared model on four grids. The first is the regular grid (DIS). The second is a Voronoi grid (DISV) made with vorflow (Hugman and Sanchez 2026), a Python package that builds Voronoi grids for MODFLOW 6. Away from the well and the river, its cells are about 50 m across, like the regular cells. They shrink to about 10 m at the well and to 12.5 m along the river. The grid has 3,241 cells. The last two grids hold the regular cells written as DISU, with and without cell vertices. The same lines of code trace the well on each grid. <!-- evidence: tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb settings cell 2 (VORONOI_BACKGROUND 50 m, VORONOI_WELL_SIZE 10 m, VORONOI_RIVER_SIZE 12.5 m; vorflow 0.2.0); §1, cell 4 and §2, cell 9 (1,600, 3,241, 1,600 and 1,600 cells; closure error at most 1.4e-16 on every grid); tutorials/herebedragons.py vorflow_cells -->

The two DISU models have the same cells and connections as the DIS model. Their F is identical to the DIS result in every cell. <!-- evidence: tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb §4, cell 15 (max |F − F_DIS| 0.0e+00 for both DISU models); §9, cell 53 (assert atol 1e-12) --> Without cell vertices, a DISU model gives fractions and volumes but no maps or GIS polygons. ## {SI: the contributing area through the seasons}

This text gives in full one of the worked examples summarised in Section 4 of the main text. The shared model is the example model of that section.

Recharge is highest in the wet season. Many supply wells pump most in the dry season. So the contributing area moves through the year. This example makes the shared model transient. It adds storage, with a specific yield of 0.2 and a specific storage of 1 × 10⁻⁵ m⁻¹. Recharge is scaled by 1.8 in the wet season, 1.0 in the wet-to-dry transition, 0.1 in the dry season and 1.1 in the dry-to-wet transition. The well pumps 640, 800, 1,120 and 640 m³/d in the same seasons. After a 1-day steady-state start, the model runs for 3 years, with three steps of about 30 days in each season. <!-- evidence: tutorials/herebedragons.py build_transient (sy 0.2, ss 1e-5), SEASON_RECHARGE (1.8, 1.0, 0.1, 1.1), SEASON_PUMPING (0.8, 1.0, 1.4, 0.8), seasonal_periods; tutorials/05_transient_snapshots/05_transient_snapshots.ipynb §1, cell 4 (640, 800, 1,120, 640 m³/d; 37 saved steps; step lengths 1.0 and 30.44 d) --> Each of the 36 seasonal steps is traced as a snapshot (Section 2).

## {SI: mean travel time to the well}

This text gives in full one of the worked examples summarised in Section 4 of the main text. The shared model is the example model of that section.

Protection zones are often drawn by travel time as well as by area. A spill 50 days upgradient of a well calls for a different response from a spill 50 years upgradient. This example adds the mean travel time of {SI: method in full} to the backward trace of the shared example model (Section 4). The mean travel time of all the water reaching the well is 46 years. <!-- evidence: tutorials/06_travel_time/06_travel_time.ipynb §5 (volume / pumping rate 16,791.3 d = 46.0 yr) --> River water reaches the well in about 2 years, because the losing river cells lie next to it. ## {SI: streams, drains, wells and linked models}

This text gives in full one of the worked examples summarised in Section 4 of the main text. The shared model is the example model of that section.

In many models water leaves the aquifer cells and comes back. A drain may pass its water to a stream through the water mover (MVR). The stream may then lose that water to the aquifer near a well. A trace on the aquifer cells alone stops at the stream. It reports the leakage as a source with no history. This example shows what changes when stream reaches and wells are traced as cells of their own (Section 3).

The river of the shared model becomes a stream of the SFR package, with one reach per row, flowing from north to south. The first reach receives 300 m³/d from upstream. Two drains lie 800 m west of the stream. Through the mover, one passes 80 % of its water to the first reach and the other all of it. The upper reaches lose water to the aquifer. The reaches below the well gain water from it. <!-- evidence: tutorials/07_networks_multimodel/07_networks_multimodel.ipynb §1; tutorials/herebedragons.py build_stream_mover, STREAM_DRAINS (rows 9 and 23, column 15, 1-based), DRAIN_TO_STREAM (0.8, 1.0); stream in column 31 -->

## {SI: a new well's source mix}

This text gives in full one of the worked examples summarised in Section 4 of the main text.

When a new well starts pumping, its water changes over the years from mostly stored water to water from the sources that feed it. Bedekar et al. (2024) showed this with the unit-concentration method on a synthetic MODFLOW-2005 and MT3D-USGS model (Section 1). Forward transient tracking solves the same transport equation without dispersion, as their example does. Their example therefore also tests transient tracking against a published result made with other codes. <!-- evidence: Bedekar et al. 2024 pp. 305–306 (MODFLOW-2005, MT3D-USGS; four separate MT3D-USGS simulations); tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb introduction and §7, cell 18 (period 1 steady with no pumping; the well pumps from year 0) -->

We rebuilt the model in MODFLOW 6 from the paper's description. It has three layers of 10 m, each with 101 × 101 cells of 10 m. Water enters as lateral inflow along the west edge and as recharge. A stream bounds the east edge. A well 150 m from the stream pumps 700 m³/d for 25 years. The paper leaves some values open, such as the time steps and the layer of the well screen. Our choices are listed in {SI: example and test models}. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §1 table (assumptions: K in m/d, convertible layers, river bottom at the cell bottom, well in layer 2, 25 years in 300 steps of 30.4 days) and budget (INFLOW 454.5, RCH 306.0, PUMP 700.0 m³/d); Bedekar et al. 2024 p. 305 -->
