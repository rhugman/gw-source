**Weight-losing particles.** Example A of Section 3 compares the contributing area with MODFLOW 6 PRT on the same flows. Particles start from every source term in cells with F > 10⁻⁶. Each particle carries a share of its term's flow as a weight. It loses part of that weight at every other sink it passes, in proportion to the flow that sink takes from the cell. These particles are the particle counterpart of gross accounting. Similar weighting rules at sinks appear in the travel-time method of Visser et al. (2009) and the solute-transport method of Winston et al. (2018). <!-- evidence: src/gwsource/prt.py track_particles (seed from cells with F > threshold; thin_weak_sinks); moved from main-text example A in round 2 --> A particle run conserves water when the weight its particles deliver to each sink equals the water that sink takes. These particles conserve water, so they deliver what the well pumps whatever the shape of the area (799.9 of 800 m³/d). The particle share of a cell is the share of the weight released there that reaches the well (main-text Fig. 3a). Cells with a particle share above 0.5 cover 1.168 km². The F > 0.5 area is 2.1 % smaller and leaves out 14 of these cells. The F > 0.1 area is 35 % larger and holds all of them. In the 372 cells with F between 0.05 and 0.95, the particle share and F differ by 0.164 on average. <!-- evidence: tutorials/01_quickstart/01_quickstart.ipynb §8, cell 24 (18,208 particles from 1,133 cells; 799.9 of 800.0 m³/d; P > 0.5: 467 cells, 1.168 km²; F > 0.5 −2.1 %, 14 particle cells outside; F > 0.1 +34.9 %, 0 outside; mean abs(P − F) 0.164 over 372 edge cells) -->

**Accounting at a small well.** The two accountings of Section 2 differ only at weak sinks. A small well in strong regional flow is a typical weak sink. This example shows how much the choice of accounting changes the water attributed to such a well. It uses the three-layer model, where a small well pumps 5 m³/d upgradient of the supply well. The small well's cell carries 33.7 m³/d. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §3, cell 13 (inflow 33.67 m³/d, taken by the well 5.00, passed on 28.67) --> Gross accounting counts only the 5 m³/d that the well pumps. F in the well's cell is then 5/33.7 = 0.149. Net accounting fixes F = 1 in the cell. It attributes all 33.7 m³/d to the well. This is 6.7 times what the well pumps. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §3, cell 15 (weak_well: F in cell 0.149 gross, 1.0 net; 5.0 against 33.667 m³/d; net / gross 6.733) --> Under gross accounting, 82 % of the water in the small well's cell flows on to the supply well. The supply well takes all the water that flows into its cell. Both accountings therefore give it the same water. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §3, cells 14, 15 and 18 (supply_well: cell throughflow 800.0 m³/d, pumping 800.0; F in the well cell 1.0 gross and net; gross F to the supply well in the weak well's cell 0.821) -->

Net accounting reproduces the worked example of Foley and Black (2013) in every cell to 1 × 10⁻¹⁴. This check is independent of the code, because the example's fractions, volumes through and volumes from are published. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §2, cells 7 and 9 (assert atol 1e-14 against Foley and Black 2013, Figs 5–8) --> On the three-layer model, the fractions to all sinks add up to 1 within 1 × 10⁻¹⁵, as the boundary-control check of Section 2 requires. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §5, cell 26 (sum over WEL and RIV: min 0.999999999999999, max 1.000000000000001) -->

**The same trace on different grids.** The two DISU models give the DIS result in every cell. <!-- evidence: tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb §4, cell 15 (max |F − F_DIS| 0.0e+00 for both DISU models) --> On the Voronoi grid, the contributing area has the same shape and reach. With the XT3D option, the flow across a face depends on the heads of the surrounding cells as well. Water can then go round in flow loops. The trace of the XT3D model found five groups of cells with flow loops, 40 cells in all. Together they circulate 4.4 m³/d. That is 0.016 % of the summed outflow of all cells in the model. The loop-by-loop solve of {SI: method in full} equals a solve of the whole system at once to 3.3 × 10⁻¹⁶. Under net accounting, deleting the smallest flow of each loop, as FlowSource does, changes F by up to 0.155 on this model. <!-- evidence: tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb §6, cells 31 and 35 (5 strongly connected sets of 4 to 15 cells; 40 of 1,600 cells; circulation 4.42 m³/d; model throughflow about 27,400 m³/d, so 0.016 %), cell 37 (block against plain sparse LU, max |dF| 3.3e-16) and cell 39 (net accounting, edges deleted against the block solve: max |dF| 0.155) -->

**The contributing area through the seasons.** Each of the 36 seasonal steps of the 3-year seasonal model is traced as a snapshot. The F > 0.5 area is smallest in the wet season, at 0.59 km². In that season the well pumps least. The rising water table also puts water into storage. Storage then acts as a sink that takes part of each cell's water. In the dry season the F > 0.5 area grows to 1.35 km². <!-- evidence: tutorials/05_transient_snapshots/05_transient_snapshots.ipynb §3, cell 6 (F > 0.5 area: wet 0.592, dry 1.350 km²) and §4, cell 9 (mean share by season: dry storage 0.525, cut from the text in SI round 1) --> Statistics over the 36 steps give nested zones (Fig. 2e). Cells with F > 0.5 in every step form an inner zone of 0.52 km². Cells with F > 0.5 in some steps add a seasonal zone of 0.93 km². Cells with F > 0.1 in at least one step add a further 0.41 km². These nested zones are one way to combine the maps. They are not a rule for drawing protection zones. <!-- evidence: tutorials/05_transient_snapshots/05_transient_snapshots.ipynb §7, cell 21 (inner 0.52, seasonal 0.93, ring 0.41, outer envelope 1.86 km²) --> In every snapshot, the traced sources add up to the pumping within 1 × 10⁻⁶ m³/d. <!-- evidence: tutorials/05_transient_snapshots/05_transient_snapshots.ipynb §4, cell 8 (largest |sum of sources − pumping| 7.2e-07 m³/d) -->
