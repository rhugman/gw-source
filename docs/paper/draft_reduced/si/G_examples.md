## {SI: the worked examples in brief}

This text gives, for each worked example of Section 4, what changes in the model, the main results and the checks. {SI: software, verification and models} describes the models. The figures of this text follow it.

**Sources and area.** {SI: contributing area against particle tracking} maps F and the particle share of weight-losing PRT particles on the example model. The F > 0.5 area covers 1.143 km². The area with a particle share above 0.5 covers 1.168 km². In the 372 cells with F between 0.05 and 0.95, the particle share and F differ by 0.164 on average. <!-- evidence: tutorials/01_quickstart/01_quickstart.ipynb §5, cell 16 (F > 0.5: 457 cells, 1.143 km²) and §8, cell 24 (P > 0.5: 467 cells, 1.168 km²; mean |P − F| 0.164 over 372 edge cells) -->

**Accounting at a small well.** The two accountings of Section 2 differ only at weak sinks. A small well in strong regional flow is a typical weak sink. This example shows how much the choice of accounting changes the water attributed to such a well. It uses the three-layer model, where a small well pumps 5 m³/d upgradient of the supply well. The small well's cell carries 33.7 m³/d. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §3, cell 13 (inflow 33.67 m³/d, taken by the well 5.00, passed on 28.67) --> Gross accounting counts only the 5 m³/d that the well pumps. F in the well's cell is then 5/33.7 = 0.149. Net accounting fixes F = 1 in the cell. It attributes all 33.7 m³/d to the well. This is 6.7 times what the well pumps. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §3, cell 15 (weak_well: F in cell 0.149 gross, 1.0 net; 5.0 against 33.667 m³/d; net / gross 6.733) --> Under gross accounting, 82 % of the water in the small well's cell flows on to the supply well. The supply well takes all the water that flows into its cell. Both accountings therefore give it the same water. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §3, cells 14, 15 and 18 (supply_well: cell throughflow 800.0 m³/d, pumping 800.0; F in the well cell 1.0 gross and net; gross F to the supply well in the weak well's cell 0.821) -->

Net accounting reproduces the worked example of Foley and Black (2013) in every cell to 1 × 10⁻¹⁴. This check is independent of the code, because the example's fractions, volumes through and volumes from are published. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §2, cells 7 and 9 (assert atol 1e-14 against Foley and Black 2013, Figs 5–8) --> On the three-layer model, the fractions to all sinks add up to 1 within 1 × 10⁻¹⁵, as the boundary-control check of Section 2 requires. <!-- evidence: tutorials/02_accounting/02_accounting.ipynb §5, cell 26 (sum over WEL and RIV: min 0.999999999999999, max 1.000000000000001) -->

**Transient tracking.** {SI: snapshots and transient tracking over 30 years} compares snapshots with transient tracking on the 30-year seasonal model. Over 30 years the well pumped 8.77 million m³. Backward tracking in time splits this into 13.8 % river leakage, 18.5 % recharge and 67.7 % initial water. Initial water made up 98 % of all the water pumped in the first year and 81 % of the water pumped in the first 10 years. Over 30 years its share fell to 68 %. This matches the 67.7 % of the backward split. <!-- studies/paper/transient_figure.py QUOTED ("cumulative initial, year 1" 0.98, "cumulative initial, year 10" 0.81, "cumulative initial, year 30" 0.68, "pumping, 30 years (m3)" 8,766,800, volumes 1.21, 1.62 and 5.94 million m³, "share initial, backward" 0.677); tutorials/08_transient_tracking/08_transient_tracking.ipynb §4 table (initial water, all pumped so far: 0.976, 0.813 and 0.677 after 1, 10 and 30 years); the 10-year share added in SI round 1, because Section 4 quotes it -->

The table lists the checks of transient tracking on this model. <!-- tutorial 08 §§5, 6, 8; README_method.md Fig. 4 notes -->

| check | largest error |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------|
| forward tracking against backward tracking in time, tagged volumes of every tag | 4.3 × 10⁻¹⁶ |
| tracked recharge fraction against a GWT run, "gwt" volumes, one transport step per flow step | 9.8 × 10⁻¹⁴ |
| fractions to the well, to the river and still stored, which must add up to 1 in every cell | 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ |
| backward closure | 0 |
| tagged-water balances, one transport step per flow step | 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ |

The two water volume options of {SI: method in full} give nearly the same result here. With "gwt" volumes, about 5,300 m³ of the 45.6 million m³ of initial water is lost over 30 years. The year-30 share of initial water is then 0.5166, against 0.5167 with the default "flow" volumes. <!-- tutorial 08 §7 --> With one transport step per flow step, the two options differ by up to 1.9 × 10⁻⁴ in the forward fractions and up to 3.7 × 10⁻³ in the backward fractions. <!-- tutorials/08_transient_tracking/08_transient_tracking.ipynb §7, cell 20 ("largest difference between the modes: 1.9e-04 in c, 3.7e-03 in w (any cell, any step)") -->

Long transport steps make tagged water arrive too early. With one transport step per flow step, new recharge made up 0.0078 of the well's water in the first step, against 0.0042 with 16 sub-steps. With one, four and 16 sub-steps, the river supplied 3.6, 3.1 and 3.0 % of the well's water in the last season of year 1. From four sub-steps to 16, the year-1 shares moved by at most 0.2 percentage points. The main runs therefore use four. <!-- tutorial 08 §9; evidence: docs/paper/figures/README_method.md Fig. 4 notes ("the year-1 river leakage share is 3.6 % with one step, 3.1 % with 4 and 3.0 % with 16, and the year-1 cumulative initial-water share 96.9 %, 97.6 % and 97.8 %") -->

**Ensembles.** {SI: probability of contribution before and after history matching} maps the probability of contribution before and after history matching. The area where it lies between 0.1 and 0.9 shrinks from 1.10 km² in the prior to 0.35 km² in the posterior. The posterior gives a probability of at least 0.5 to 93 % of the true F > 0.5 area, against 79 % for the prior. <!-- evidence: tutorials/09_ensembles/09_ensembles.ipynb §9.4, cell 28 (band 0.1 < P < 0.9 1.098, 0.350 km²; true zone with P ≥ 0.5: 79.268 and 93.415 %) -->

**Where each source's water goes.** A forward trace starts at a source rather than at a well. Of the 45 m³/d of recharge on the field, 39 % reaches the well. <!-- evidence: tutorials/03_forward/03_forward.ipynb §6, cell 16 (36 recharge cells, 45.0 m³/d; 17.7 m³/d to the well, 39 %) --> G in the well cell gives the pumped water by source. The shares are 14 % river water, 15 % regional inflow and 71 % recharge, as the backward trace of Section 4 found. <!-- evidence: tutorials/03_forward/03_forward.ipynb §3, cell 10 (G in the well cell: river 0.142, west edge 0.148, recharge 0.711) --> The forward and backward traces give the same volume from each source to the well within 1 × 10⁻⁶ m³/d. The difference equals the cell budget error term of Eq. (S8). <!-- evidence: tutorials/03_forward/03_forward.ipynb §7, cell 18 (differences 2.8e-9 to 4.7e-7 m³/d, each equal to the residual term) --> The GWT run reaches equilibrium after 10⁶ days. Its concentrations of river water then equal G in every cell to 4.2 × 10⁻¹³. <!-- evidence: tutorials/03_forward/03_forward.ipynb §9, cell 24 (1e6 days in 10 steps; largest change over the last step 5.6e-17; largest |G − C| over all 1,600 cells 4.2e-13) -->

**The same trace on different grids.** The two DISU models give the DIS result in every cell. <!-- evidence: tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb §4, cell 15 (max |F − F_DIS| 0.0e+00 for both DISU models) --> On the Voronoi grid, the contributing area has the same shape and reach. Its expected area is 1.178 km², against 1.173 km² on the regular grid. The expected area is the sum of F times the cell area over all cells. <!-- evidence: tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb §4, cell 15 (expected area 1.178 against 1.173 km², +0.4 %); full draft Section 4.1 (expected area, sum of F × cell area) --> North of the well, F falls from 0.5 to 0.1 over 186 m on the Voronoi grid, against 121 m on the regular grid. On the regular grid the water flows along the rows, so each cell passes nearly all its water to one neighbour. Each Voronoi cell splits its water among two or three downstream cells, because its faces lie at many angles to the flow. We read the wider edge as a sideways spread of F by these splits. {SI: numerical dispersion test problems} tests this reading on squares turned to the flow. <!-- evidence: tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb §4, cells 20 and 22 (width of the edge from F = 0.5 to F = 0.1 north of the well along x = 500 m, F interpolated linearly between cell centres: DIS 121 m, DISV 186 m); cell 25 (the check does not separate face angle from the varying cell size on the 50 m Voronoi grid) -->

With the XT3D option, the flow across a face depends on the heads of the surrounding cells as well. Water can then go round in flow loops. The trace of the XT3D model found five groups of cells with flow loops, 40 cells in all. Together they circulate 4.4 m³/d. That is 0.016 % of the summed outflow of all cells in the model. The block solve of {SI: method in full} equals a plain sparse solve of the whole system to 3.3 × 10⁻¹⁶. Under net accounting, deleting the smallest flow of each loop, as FlowSource does, changes F by up to 0.155 on this model. <!-- evidence: tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb §6, cells 31 and 35 (5 strongly connected sets of 4 to 15 cells; 40 of 1,600 cells; circulation 4.42 m³/d; model throughflow about 27,400 m³/d, so 0.016 %), cell 37 (block against plain sparse LU, max |dF| 3.3e-16) and cell 39 (net accounting, edges deleted against the block solve: max |dF| 0.155) -->

**The contributing area through the seasons.** Each of the 36 seasonal steps of the 3-year seasonal model is traced as a snapshot. The F > 0.5 area is smallest in the wet season, at 0.59 km². In that season the well pumps least. The rising water table also puts water into storage. Storage then acts as a sink that takes part of each cell's water. In the dry season the F > 0.5 area grows to 1.35 km². <!-- evidence: tutorials/05_transient_snapshots/05_transient_snapshots.ipynb §3, cell 6 (F > 0.5 area: wet 0.592, dry 1.350 km²) and §4, cell 9 (mean share by season: dry storage 0.525, cut from the text in SI round 1) --> Statistics over the 36 steps give nested zones (Fig. 2e). Cells with F > 0.5 in every step form an inner zone of 0.52 km². Cells with F > 0.5 in some steps add a seasonal zone of 0.93 km². Cells with F > 0.1 in at least one step add a further 0.41 km². These nested zones are one way to combine the maps. They are not a rule for drawing protection zones. <!-- evidence: tutorials/05_transient_snapshots/05_transient_snapshots.ipynb §7, cell 21 (inner 0.52, seasonal 0.93, ring 0.41, outer envelope 1.86 km²) --> In every snapshot, the traced sources add up to the pumping within 1 × 10⁻⁶ m³/d. <!-- evidence: tutorials/05_transient_snapshots/05_transient_snapshots.ipynb §4, cell 8 (largest |sum of sources − pumping| 7.2e-07 m³/d) -->

**Mean travel time.** The mean travel time of {SI: method in full} needs a porosity, here 0.3. Weight-losing PRT particles stop when they enter the well cell. The full-mixing times are therefore also taken to the point where the water enters the well cell. Over all the water reaching the well, the mean full-mixing time is then 45.9 years. The mean particle time is 45.7 years, 0.5 % shorter. <!-- evidence: tutorials/06_travel_time/06_travel_time.ipynb §6 "Overall means" (PRT 45.7, full mixing to the well cell 45.9 yr, −0.5 %); the 46.0 years of §5 (volume / pumping rate, 16,791.3 d) includes the 32.2 d spent in the well cell, so the former "46 years" and "45.9 years" differ by that residence; SI round 1 states the time to the well cell only --> This agreement is not an independent test. In both methods, the mean travel time of all the water is close to the volume of water that will end at the well divided by the pumping rate. That volume is nearly the same in both. <!-- evidence: tutorials/06_travel_time/06_travel_time.ipynb §5 (volume identity, relative error 0.0) and the text after "Overall means" --> The comparison cell by cell is the informative one. In the 456 cells with F > 0.5, apart from the well cell itself, the median ratio of particle time to full-mixing time is 0.96. In the 676 cells with F < 0.5, it is 5.7. A particle from a cell with small F follows one slow path around the edge of the area. Full mixing averages over all the paths that the cell's water takes. <!-- evidence: tutorials/06_travel_time/06_travel_time.ipynb §6 "Cell by cell", cell 22 (F ≥ 0.5: 456 cells, median 0.96; F < 0.5: 676 cells, median 5.72; the comparison keeps cells with a full-mixing time above 0, so the well cell drops out of the 457 cells with F > 0.5, review alt_r1 F8) -->

**Streams, drains and linked models.** On the aquifer cells alone, the trace stops at the stream. Stream leakage then supplies 32 % of the well's water. <!-- evidence: tutorials/07_networks_multimodel/07_networks_multimodel.ipynb §2 (SFR 255.2 m³/d, share 0.319) --> With the reaches traced as cells, that water splits into 129 m³/d of upstream stream inflow and 126 m³/d of recharge. That recharge reached the stream through the drains and the mover. <!-- evidence: tutorials/07_networks_multimodel/07_networks_multimodel.ipynb §3 "The well's water by original source" (SFR:EXT-INFLOW 129.4; RCH 670.6 against 544.8, +125.8 m³/d) --> The traced volumes add up to the pumping to round-off, with and without the reaches as cells. <!-- evidence: tutorials/07_networks_multimodel/07_networks_multimodel.ipynb §3 closure (relative error 1.4 × 10⁻¹⁶ cells only, 0.0 with SFR nodes) --> The split model gives F to within 2 × 10⁻¹³ of the single model. Both simulations solve the same flow equations, so this is a consistency test. It shows that no water is lost where the models meet. <!-- evidence: tutorials/07_networks_multimodel/07_networks_multimodel.ipynb §5 (max |split − single| of F 2.07 × 10⁻¹³) -->

**A new well's source mix.** Bedekar et al. (2024) showed with the unit-concentration method how a new well's water changes from stored water to water from its sources. Their synthetic example ran in MODFLOW-2005 and MT3D-USGS without dispersion. It therefore tests transient tracking against a published result made with other codes. <!-- evidence: Bedekar et al. 2024 pp. 305–306 (MODFLOW-2005, MT3D-USGS; four separate MT3D-USGS simulations) -->

One forward run with four tags gives the share of the pumped water from each source ({SI: the unit-concentration example rebuilt}a). Stream water arrives within the first year. Lateral inflow first appears after about 7 years. At year 25 the well's water is 48.2 % lateral inflow, 29.2 % recharge and 22.5 % stream water. The paper gives 48 %, 29 % and 23 %. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §2 table (stream 0.080 at 1 yr, 0.225 from 10 yr; lateral inflow 0.090 at 10 yr, 0.482 at 25 yr) and §2 text (first appears after about 7 years); Bedekar et al. 2024 p. 306 ("approximately 29%, 23%, and 48%") --> The independent check is Figure 2 of the paper, read off to about one percentage point. Over 15 read-off times, the largest difference is 1.3 percentage points, on the lateral-inflow front at year 10. With transport sub-steps of 7.6 days, the largest difference over the first 15 years falls to 0.6 percentage points. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §4 (largest |here − paper|: lateral inflow 1.3, storage 1.2 points at year 10) and §6, cell 16 output ("7.6-day steps: ... 0.6 percentage points") --> Four MODFLOW 6 GWT runs, one per source, give the same fractions to 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ in every cell and step. The two codes solve the same equations, so this is a consistency test. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §3 (max |c − C| 4.4 × 10⁻¹¹ to 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰, below the GWT solver tolerance of 10⁻⁹; volume_mode "gwt") -->

Backward tracking in time adds the time-limited contributing area ({SI: the unit-concentration example rebuilt}b–e). In layer 2, where the well is screened, the well pumps more than half the water of 841 cells within 1 year. Within 25 years the area covers 6,825 cells of that layer. That is nearly the 6,829 cells of the steady-state F > 0.5 area of the final flows. <!-- evidence: tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §7, cell 18 table (cells with w > 0.5 in layer 2: 841 within 1 yr, 6,825 within 25 yr; steady trace F > 0.5: 6,829 cells in layer 2) --> We expect that a forward unit-concentration model would need one run per zone of the aquifer to give such a map. <!-- tutorials/10_unit_concentration/10_unit_concentration.ipynb §7 introduction (one transport run per zone of the aquifer); untested, hence "we expect" -->
