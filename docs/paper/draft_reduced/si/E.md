## {SI: numerical dispersion test problems}

Full mixing spreads the edge of a contributing area over a band of cells, because each cell mixes the water it receives. This spreading comes from the grid, not from the aquifer. We refer to it as numerical dispersion. Two test problems measure how wide the band is and how it changes with cell size $h$. <!-- docs/dispersion_study.md §1 -->

Problem A is a confined aquifer, 2 km × 2 km, with a transmissivity of 500 m²/d, a regional hydraulic gradient of 0.002 and one well pumping 500 m³/d. The perimeter cells hold the analytical head of uniform flow plus a well. The reference is the analytical capture zone of Javandel and Tsang (1986). The regional flow runs along the grid rows or at 30° to them. The grids have square cells of 200 to 12.5 m (DIS) or Voronoi cells of about the same mean size (DISV). Problem B is test model 1 without its inactive cells ({SI: software, verification and models}). It is one unconfined layer of 1.5 km × 1 km, with recharge, a river, a constant-head west edge and a well pumping 800 m³/d. Its grids have cells of 100 to 12.5 m. Its reference is PRT on the 12.5 m grid, with about 94,000 flow-weighted particles released at the water table. East of the river, all water passes the gaining river. The river is a weak sink there. So the areas of problem B are compared west of the river only. <!-- docs/dispersion_study.md §1 (problem A: T = 500 m²/d, gradient 0.002, Q = 500 m³/d, 2000 m × 2000 m, perimeter cells at the analytical head, Javandel and Tsang (1986), 0° and 30°, DIS h = 200 to 12.5 m and Voronoi DISV; problem B: test model 1 without inactive cells, 1500 m × 1000 m, recharge 1e-3 m/d, river, west CHD, well 800 m³/d, DIS h = 100 to 12.5 m, PRT reference on the finest grid with 94,000 particles at the water table, compared west of the river only) -->

Across cell sizes, the band where F lies between 0.1 and 0.9 was 1.2–12 cells wide ({SI: numerical dispersion maps and cell size}). In metres, it narrowed only as about the square root of $h$, with fitted rates of 0.4–0.6. For cells of 50 m or less, the size of the F > 0.5 area was within 5 % of the reference area. The F > 0.1 area missed at most 0.1 % of the reference area in every run. For cells of 12.5–50 m, it was 30–90 % larger than the reference area in problem A and 8–16 % larger in problem B. Voronoi grids spread the edge no less than square cells. Face-to-face routing (Chowdhury 2016) is an experimental 2D option that passes water between cell faces instead of mixing it across the whole cell. It removed 35–70 % of the excess spreading. It did not change how fast the band narrows with $h$, because water still mixes across each cell face. <!-- docs/dispersion_study.md Summary and §5 (band 1.2 to 12 cells, h^0.5 with fitted rates 0.4 to 0.6; F ≥ 0.5 within 5 % at h ≤ 50 m; F ≥ 0.1 contained the whole reference zone in every run, missed ≤ 0.1 %, 30 to 90 % (A) and 8 to 16 % (B) too large at h = 12.5 to 50 m; DISV no less dispersion; face routing removes 35 to 70 % of the excess spreading and does not change the convergence rate) -->

The orientation of the grid matters as well. On the example model, F falls from 0.5 to 0.1 north of the well over 121 m on the regular grid, whose rows run along the flow. On squares of 47 m turned by 45° to the flow, the same band is 215 m wide. That is 1.8 times as wide. On a Voronoi grid with cells of about 25 m, it is 126 m wide. <!-- evidence: tutorials/04_grids/04_grids.ipynb §4, cells 20 and 22 (same model on squares of side 47.1 m rotated 45°, one centred on the well, 1,921 cells; and on the vorflow grid with a 25 m background, 8,256 cells; width of the edge from F = 0.5 to F = 0.1 north of the well along x = 500 m, F interpolated linearly between cell centres: DIS 121 m, rotated squares 215 m, DISV 25 m 126 m); 215 / 121 = 1.78; moved from the former Text S7 in SI round 1 -->

Totals depend much less on mixing. On the same grid, the well's shares by source were the same to three digits under full mixing, face routing and particles. For cells of 50 m or less, the flow-weighted mean travel time of all the water reaching the well was within 2 % of particle tracking in problem B and within 4 % of the analytical value in problem A. <!-- docs/dispersion_study.md Summary (shares by source identical to three digits; travel time within 2 % of PRT (B) and 4 % of analytical (A) for h ≤ 50 m) -->

The dispersion study draws areas with F ≥ 0.1 and F ≥ 0.5, while the main text writes F > 0.1 and F > 0.5. No full-mixing cell in these tests has F exactly 0.1 or 0.5, so both forms select the same full-mixing cells. <!-- studies/dispersion/output/a_maps.npz and b_maps.npz, counted in round 4: no full-mixing map value equal to 0.1 or 0.5 within 1e-12; the comparison zones (PRT, cell-averaged exact) hold cells at exactly 0.5 and are drawn with ≥ (a strict threshold would shrink them by at most about 0.8 % of their area); threshold paragraph shortened and the time-step paragraph moved out in SI round 1 (docs/dispersion_study.md §3) -->
