## Overall judgement

Methods is now readable without the SI for the steady-state outputs. F, G, the volumes, gross and net accounting and the mean travel time are each defined, given an equation where it helps and read in plain words. The eight-cell example ties F and gross against net to numbers a reader can check by hand. This answers the outside comment that "Methods is the big thing".

Four things still stop a practitioner from trusting every output of Sections 4 and 5 from the main text alone. The method never says how stream reaches, lakes and linked models enter the equations, although example C and Section 5 depend on it. The transient part leaves the two water volume options and transport sub-steps wholly to the SI, although example B's GWT match and its 0.004 difference depend on them. The consequence of full mixing (wider, more diffuse areas that depend on cell size) is not stated where the assumption is introduced. And the travel-time closure check, Eq. (6), is shown as an equation but its plain reading skips the step that makes it say what the text claims.

The order is mostly right. The one structural change I would make is to move gross and net accounting up, straight after F and Fig. 1a, because the worked example already uses gross accounting and the weak-sink rule is the paper's main difference from FlowSource.

At 2,605 words the section is 60 % over the outline's 1,630. Since the author has relaxed the cap, I propose a target of about 2,100 words. That is room for the additions below, paid for by cuts in the two transient subsections, the solver paragraph and Eq. (6), and by shorter readings of the equations. The author has since decided that an equation needs only one or two sentences that state its core idea, with no term-by-term derivation in words (finding 23). The lead-ins to Eqs. (2), (4) and (7) and the reading of Eq. (8) now walk through each term, and together they hold about 250 words that can drop to about 100.

## Findings

### Must-fix

**1. Stream reaches, lakes, multi-aquifer wells and linked models never enter the method.**
*Location:* "Full mixing and the cell budget", and the definition of a destination in "The backward fraction F".
*Problem:* The equations are written for "cells" and "neighbours". The introduction claims stream networks and linked models, Section 3 says reaches "can be traced as extra cells", and the outline's example C (point 3) turns on the difference between a stream reach as a source term of the aquifer cell and a reach traced as a cell of its own (32 % of the well's water moves from stream leakage to upstream inflow and recharge). Section 5 uses "streams as cells" throughout. A reader of Methods alone cannot tell what that choice means, or that the water mover and model exchanges are just more flows $q_{ij}$. Without this, the split by source in example C looks arbitrary.
*Fix:* Two or three sentences after the definition of $q_{ij}$. For example: "A stream reach, a lake or a multi-aquifer well can be traced in one of two ways. It can stay a source or sink term of the aquifer cells it exchanges water with. It can also be traced as a cell of its own, from its package budget file, so that its flows to the aquifer, to other reaches and through the water mover become flows $q_{ij}$ like any other. Cells of linked models joined by exchanges are treated as one set of cells. Which choice a trace makes changes the split of a sink's water by source (example C)." Point to Table S2 for the budget records needed.

**2. Gross and net accounting comes after the checks, although F and Fig. 1a already use gross accounting.**
*Location:* order of subsections. "Gross and net accounting at weak sinks" is the sixth block. Eq. (2), Fig. 1a ("gross accounting" in its caption) and "Eq. (3) therefore gives F = 6/11" come three blocks earlier.
*Problem:* The worked example already makes the gross choice, with 6 of 11 m³/d going to the well and 5 m³/d passing on, but the reader is told what that choice is only after G, volumes and checks. The paragraph on net accounting then has to send the reader back to Fig. 1a. Weak sinks are what the introduction builds up to (its paragraphs 3 and 4), so this is the place a practitioner expects the method to answer them. Example A's weight-losing particles are also the particle counterpart of gross accounting, so the concept is needed before Section 4 in any case.
*Fix:* Move "Gross and net accounting at weak sinks" to straight after the Fig. 1a paragraph and [[Figure 1]], before the solver paragraph or straight after it. Its first sentence then reads naturally from the example: "The well cell in Fig. 1a is a weak sink." The checks paragraph can then say once that net accounting has no cell budget error term. With this order, the remaining blocks (G, volumes and checks, travel time) all build on gross F without interruption. I rate this must-fix because it is the one change of order that the paper's main argument needs. Severity can drop to should-fix if the author prefers to keep the order and adds a one-sentence forward pointer at Eq. (2).

**3. The overview describes the section, not the questions it answers.**
*Location:* "Overview".
*Problem:* The overview says what a backward and a forward trace are and that "the subsections build up from the two fractions … to the volumes, checks and travel times". It does not tie the outputs to the practitioner's questions of the introduction (where a sink's water comes from, where a source's water goes, how old it is, how the answer changes through the year), nor to examples A, B and C. Outline §1.2 asks that the general view (any sink, any source, water by source, travel time) shows in every section. The overview is the one place in Methods where that map can be given in a few lines. It also does not say what a user must supply (the binary grid file and budget file, and a porosity for times), which a modeller wants to know first.
*Fix:* Replace the third and fourth sentences with a short map, for example: "The backward fraction F maps where a sink's water comes from. A contributing area follows from it (example A in Section 4). The forward fraction G maps where a source's water goes. Volumes split a sink's water by source, and with a porosity the trace also gives mean travel times (example C). For transient models the code either traces each time step on its own or carries tagged water through storage (example B). A trace needs only the grid and budget files that MODFLOW 6 saves, plus a porosity for travel times and transient tracking." Keep the sentence that the derivations are in the SI.

**4. The consequence of full mixing is not stated where full mixing is introduced.**
*Location:* "Full mixing and the cell budget", first paragraph; and the end of "The backward fraction F" (contributing area).
*Problem:* Outline §1.2 frames the tool as "open about its trade-offs", and key message 5 says areas are wider and more diffuse than particle areas. Methods states the assumption ("each active model cell as one well-mixed volume") but not what it costs. The only caveats are at the checks ("cannot show … that full mixing describes how water moves") and at travel time ("not a first-arrival time"). A modeller reading "contributing area is the set of cells where F exceeds a threshold" needs to know at that point why F takes in-between values at the edge and why the threshold matters. Otherwise example A's 0.164 mean difference from particles, and Section 6, arrive without preparation.
*Fix:* One or two sentences after the definition of full mixing: "Full mixing spreads a cell's water over all its outflows. At the edge of a contributing area, a cell's water then splits between the sink and other outlets, so F takes values between 0 and 1 where particles from the same cell would mostly either reach the sink or miss it. The coarser the grid, the wider this band ({SI: numerical dispersion})." Then the contributing-area sentence can say why a threshold is needed rather than only that it is a judgement.

### Should-fix

**5. Eq. (6) and its plain reading do not match without a missing step.**
*Location:* "Mean travel time", Eq. (6) and the two sentences after it.
*Problem:* The left-hand side is $\sum_i \mathrm{Src}_i m_i - \sum_i r_i m_i$. The text says the check states that the volume on the right "equals the destination's inflow times the flow-weighted mean travel time of all the water the destination takes". To see this the reader must write $m_i = F_i \cdot (m_i/F_i)$, recognise $\mathrm{Src}_i F_i$ as the volume from cell $i$, and recall from the closure check that these volumes add up to the destination's water. None of these steps is in the text, and the closure check itself is given only in words. A practitioner will either skip the equation or distrust it. It is also the only check given as an equation, while the main closure check, which example A quotes (800.0000 against 800.0000 m³/d), is not.
*Fix:* Prefer dropping Eq. (6) to the SI next to Eq. (S3) and stating the check in words: "Because $\mathrm{Src}_i F_i$ is the volume from cell $i$, the travel times obey a closure check of their own ({SI}). The volume of water in the model that will end at the destination equals the destination's inflow times the flow-weighted mean travel time of all its water." This keeps the sentence example C needs and saves one display equation and about 30 words. If the author keeps Eq. (6), add the step in one sentence.

**6. The water volume options and transport sub-steps are needed to read example B.**
*Location:* "Transient flow: snapshots and transient tracking", last paragraph ("The two volume options and the transport sub-steps are described in {SI}").
*Problem:* Example B (outline §3.4, points 9 and 10, and the current text) reports that transient tracking matches GWT to 9.8 × 10⁻¹⁴ "with GWT's volumes and one transport step per flow step", that "the default volumes change the fractions by less than 0.004", and that the main runs use four sub-steps because "long steps make new water arrive too early". The Bedekar et al. (2024) comparison also uses sub-steps. With Methods as it stands, a reader meets "GWT's volumes" and "sub-steps" for the first time in the results. This goes against the outline's own purpose for §3.2 ("understand every output in Sections 4 and 5 from this section alone").
*Fix:* Two sentences each, keeping the SI for the detail. Volumes: "By default the code starts each cell's water volume at porosity times saturated volume and then changes it only by the storage flows of the flow model, so every cell's water balance closes exactly. As an option, it resets the volume to porosity times saturated volume at each step, as GWT does, so that results can be compared with a GWT run." Sub-steps: "Long implicit time steps make tagged water arrive too early. The code can split each flow step into shorter transport sub-steps without changing the flow model." Name the options' keywords only in the SI.

**7. The cell budget error $r_i$ is said to be "kept in the equations below" but is not visible in them, and its meaning changes in Eq. (7).**
*Location:* "Full mixing and the cell budget", last paragraph; Eqs. (2)–(4); Eq. (7) and "where $O_i$ now leaves out the storage terms".
*Problem:* A reader looks for $r_i$ in Eqs. (2)–(4) and does not find it. It enters only because the backward fraction uses outflow and the forward fraction uses inflow, and it appears explicitly first in the checks (in words) and then in Eqs. (6)–(8). In Eq. (7) the storage terms are taken out of $O_i$, and $r_i$ silently becomes the budget error with the storage change included (`tracking.py`: "net inflow over all terms, storage included"), not $I_i - O_i$ of Eq. (1). A modeller who reads $r_i$ as defined in Eq. (1) will think the storage change leaves the cell at its own mix.
*Fix:* In the cell-budget paragraph, replace "keeps this error in the equations below" with a sentence that says where it shows: "Because F is written with each cell's outflow and G with its inflow, the error appears in the checks that link them, and the code reports it there." At Eq. (7), add: "Here $r_i$ is still the cell budget error. It is the inflow less the outflow less the change in storage over the step, and so stays small."

**8. The travel time compared with particles is not the travel time defined in Methods.**
*Location:* "Mean travel time"; SI Text S1, "Travel time to where the water enters the sink cell".
*Problem:* $m_i/F_i$ includes the time spent in the sink cell. PRT and MODPATH stop particles on entering it. Example C compares full-mixing and particle travel times by source and cell by cell, and the SI says the compared time is the variant $(m_i - x_i)/F_i$. Methods never mentions this, so a reader will not know which time the 45.7 against 45.9 years compares. Nothing in the main text points to the SI paragraph either.
*Fix:* One sentence at the end of the subsection: "This time includes the time spent in the cell where the water leaves. Particle trackers stop at entry to a sink cell, so the code also gives the time to that point ({SI: method in full}), and Section 4 uses it for comparison with particles."

**9. The checks paragraph names three checks but says what only one of them compares.**
*Location:* "Volumes and the water-budget checks", second paragraph.
*Problem:* "The forward–backward check compares the backward and forward traces of one source and destination" does not say what quantity is compared. Example C (outline point 2) reports that the two volumes agree within 1 × 10⁻⁶ m³/d, which the reader can only connect if Methods says the check compares the volume from the source to the destination. "Boundary-control check" is a coined name with no reason given, and a modeller will not guess that it means "every cell's water goes somewhere".
*Fix:* "The forward–backward check requires the volume from a source to a destination to be the same whether it is traced backward or forward." "The third check traces every sink of the model at once and requires the fractions to all sinks to add up to 1 in every cell with outflow. We refer to it as the boundary-control check." Keep the closing caveat on what the checks cannot show; it is well placed.

**10. Fig. 1b uses a different notation from Eqs. (7) and (8).**
*Location:* Fig. 1b, bottom row labels ($V_i^n, c_i^n$ and $V_i^{n+1}, c_i^{n+1}$); Eqs. (7) and (8) ($V_i^{\mathrm{old}}$, $V_i^{\mathrm{new}}$, $c_i^{n-1}$, $c_i^n$); caption ("V is the cell's water volume").
*Problem:* The share $c$ matches (end of step $n$ is $c^n$ in both), but the volumes are $V^n$ in the figure and $V^{\mathrm{old}}$, $V^{\mathrm{new}}$ in the text. A reader moving between Eq. (7) and the figure has to work out that $V_i^{\mathrm{new}}$ of step $n$ is the figure's $V_i^n$, and, under the "gwt" option, that it is not the $V_i^{\mathrm{old}}$ of step $n+1$.
*Fix:* Either relabel the figure ($V_i^{\mathrm{new}}$ at the end of a step, $V_i^{\mathrm{old}}$ at the start of the next) or add one sentence after Eq. (7): "In Fig. 1b, $V_i^{\mathrm{new}}$ of step $n$ is the volume at the end of step $n$." The figure change is cleaner.

**11. Fig. 1a needs a sentence of reading guidance in the text, and the net value in the caption.**
*Location:* the Fig. 1a paragraph in "The backward fraction F"; caption (a).
*Problem:* The text explains only the well cell. A reader will ask why the two lower-left cells have the same F as the well cell (all their water passes through it) and why F falls along the upper row (more of each cell's water bypasses the well to the river). These are exactly the patterns that make F maps readable. The outline (§4, Fig. 1 row) proposed showing net F = 1 beside gross 0.55. The text gives the net numbers (11 m³/d), but the figure and caption do not, so the gross–net comparison has no visual anchor.
*Fix:* Add one sentence after "F = 6/11 = 0.55": "All the water of the two cells west of the well cell flows into it, so they share its F. The cells of the upper row send part of their water past the well to the river, so their F is lower." Add to caption (a): "Under net accounting the well cell would have F = 1 and the well would be attributed all 11 m³/d."

**12. The solver paragraph is more than a practitioner needs and duplicates the SI.**
*Location:* "The backward fraction F", the paragraph after [[Figure 1]] (one solve, flow order, flow loops, XT3D).
*Problem:* The useful messages for the reader are two: one solve gives every destination group, and flow loops (with XT3D, or between streams and the aquifer) are solved exactly rather than cut. The definition of a flow loop and the XT3D citation are repeated word for word in SI Text S1, paragraph 3 of the matrix section. "The code solves the cells in the order the water flows" is implementation detail. The practitioner point that FlowSource deletes the smallest flow in each loop is only in the SI, but it is the reason the reader should care.
*Fix:* Two sentences in the main text: "Eq. (3) holds in every cell, so one solve of the linear equations gives the fractions to every destination group at once. Where water circulates among cells, as it can with the XT3D option of MODFLOW 6 (Provost et al. 2017), the code solves each such flow loop exactly instead of deleting a flow as FlowSource does (Foley and Black 2013; {SI: method in full})." Drop the loop definition from the SI or from the main text, not both.

**13. Transient tracking: the flow-imbalance paragraph and the initial-water caveat can be shorter.**
*Location:* "Transient flow …", the paragraph after Eq. (7) and the paragraph on initial water.
*Problem:* Three sentences explain $r_i c_i$ and the GWT flow-imbalance correction, and a fourth gives the all-tagged test. For a practitioner the point is one sentence: the budget error leaves at the cell's own mix, as in GWT. "Initial water is not necessarily water from before pumping began, because a simulation need not start before pumping" is example B's caveat and is repeated there (outline B point 4). Saying it twice breaks the voice rule "say a point once".
*Fix:* "The term $r_i c_i$ lets the cell budget error leave the cell at the cell's own mix, as the flow-imbalance correction of GWT does (Langevin et al. 2022). A run that tags all water then gives c = 1 in every cell, as it should." Keep the definition of initial water and move its caveat to example B only. This frees room for finding 6.

**14. Backward tracking in time: two sentences belong in the SI.**
*Location:* "Backward tracking in time", the sentence "One value describes both kinds of water, because the implicit scheme mixes them alike", and the sentence on the transient forward–backward check.
*Problem:* The first is a point about the numerical scheme that a reader does not need in order to read a map of w. The second states Eq. (S9) in words in a sentence that is hard to parse ("the tagged inflow and initial water, each multiplied by its share w"), and SI Text S1 repeats the same plain reading. The subsection is the second longest in Methods for an output used in one figure panel (Fig. 2f) and one paragraph of example B. The rest of the subsection (purpose, the contrast with MODPATH backward tracking, Eq. (8), the three shares adding to 1, the limit to F) is clear and should stay. The reading of Eq. (8) is clear but, under the author's new rule, should shrink to its core idea (finding 23).
*Fix:* Move both sentences to SI Text S1, and replace the second with a clause: "As in steady flow, a forward–backward check links the forward and backward runs ({SI: method in full})." Example B then quotes the 67.7 % match in initial water as the check in action.

**15. The mean travel time $m_i$ is hard to understand as defined.**
*Location:* "Mean travel time", definition of $m_i$ before Eq. (5).
*Problem:* "Let $m_i$ be the mean travel time … counted only for water that leaves through the destination and weighted by $F_i$" reads as a time, but it is a time times a fraction. A reader only learns this three sentences later from "the mean travel time … is $m_i/F_i$". The term $\tau_i F_i$ in Eq. (5) is then hard to follow.

The code solves for $m_i = F_i t_i$, which obeys an equation of the same form as Eq. (3)." Then Eq. Also say in a few words why the time is "defined under gross accounting only" (under net accounting the destination cells absorb water that does not leave there).

## Answers to the six questions

**1. Enough to understand and trust the outputs without the SI?** For steady-state F, G, volumes, gross and net accounting and travel times, yes, once findings 5, 8, 9 and 15 are fixed. Still missing from the main text: package nodes and linked models (1), the consequence of full mixing (4), the volume options and sub-steps (6), and the sink-cell travel time used against particles (8). Belongs in the SI instead: the flow-order and loop-definition detail (12), the flow-imbalance detail (13), the "one value" sentence and the transient forward–backward check statement (14), and Eq. (6) (5).

**2. Order and flow.** There is an overview, but it should map the introduction's questions to outputs and to examples A, B and C (3). Each block builds on the last, apart from gross and net accounting, which should follow F (2). The transient pair (snapshots, then transient tracking, then backward in time) is well ordered and sets up example B, including the Bedekar et al. (2024) comparison through the GWT equivalence of G and c. Example A is set up by F and the contributing area but needs finding 4 to prepare the particle comparison. Example C is set up by volumes and travel time but needs findings 1, 8 and 9.

**3. Equations.** Eight is about right for *Groundwater* readers of a methods paper, provided each has a short plain reading. Under the author's new rule that reading is one or two sentences of core idea (finding 23), which makes keeping the equations cheaper than explaining them in prose. I would cut to six or seven: merge Eqs. (2) and (3) on one line (19) and move Eq. (6) to the SI (5). Eqs. (1), (2)/(3), (4), (5), (7) and (8) each carry an output the examples use. Eq. (8) was the author's decision (decision 11) and its three-route reading justifies it.

**4. Fig. 1.** It supports the text well. Panel (a) is cited at the worked example and again at gross and net accounting. Panel (b) is cited at the snapshot ("top") and transient tracking ("bottom"). Panel (a) is under-used in the prose (11), and panel (b)'s volume labels differ from Eq. (7) (10). Panel (b) shows only storage release under "snapshot". The text also mentions storage gain as a sink, which is fine without a picture.

**5. Length.** See the table above: about 2,100 words, with the transient blocks, the solver paragraph and the term-by-term equation readings trimmed, and the overview, full-mixing and transient-volume content grown.
